## Macro Thread

Overnight, WTI crude slipped **$1.01** to **$92.73** and Brent fell **$1.07** to **$105.58** — the sharpest single-session retreat for either benchmark in over a week — as diplomatic signals from the Middle East briefly tempered the fear of a wider supply disruption. MCX Crude last closed at **₹8,980/bbl** and may open softer when trading begins at 9:00 AM IST, with the rupee at **₹95.97** doing little to cushion the slide. The signal to watch today is whether WTI can hold above **$92** — a close below that level would suggest traders are removing geopolitical premium rather than simply pausing.

## Geopolitical Premium Unwinding — SHIFTING

For three editions, the dominant story was a market terrified of Middle East supply disruptions pushing crude and gold upward together. Today that alignment is fracturing: crude has pulled back sharply while gold's retreat is comparatively modest, suggesting the two are no longer moving as one unified fear trade. The shift matters because it implies traders are beginning to separate what is genuine supply risk from what was positional momentum — and that distinction, once it takes hold, historically reprices the two assets differently.

## The Market Is Saying

Oil crossing **$100** looked inevitable a week ago; a retreat to **$92.73** (WTI) in one session is the market testing whether the war premium was borrowed. COMEX Gold at **$4,175** is down only **$15.5** from its last close — a far shallower pullback than crude's, which tells you the demand for gold as a safe harbour is not disappearing with the first sign of diplomacy. Silver at **$59.86** on COMEX is down **$0.65**, and the gold-silver ratio now sits at **69.7** — silver's industrial half is feeling the pressure of a softer crude complex more acutely than gold's pure safe-haven half. MCX Copper closed last session at **₹1,424.45/kg** and has not yet traded today; the industrial-demand lens on copper will be the cleanest read on whether this is a genuine de-escalation or a one-session pause. Henry Hub natural gas at **$3.17** is essentially flat, confirming that natgas is running on its own domestic storage logic and carrying none of the Middle East premium that briefly inflated crude.

## Historical Context

The twist worth watching here: gold falling into an apparent de-escalation while crude retreats more sharply is historically the pattern that reasserts the safe-haven bid once traders realise diplomatic signals rarely resolve supply uncertainty permanently. The contrary read, held by analysts positioned short on crude, is that a sustained geopolitical premium above **$90** WTI historically becomes self-defeating as demand destruction from high prices accelerates before any physical supply is actually lost. In past episodes where crude fell more than gold in a single session, the divergence has tended to widen further before it narrows — meaning the two assets may reprice independently for several more sessions rather than snapping back into lockstep.

## What Kills It

A confirmed ceasefire or a verifiable withdrawal of forces from a contested zone would strip the remaining geopolitical premium from crude quickly; de-escalation announcements in past episodes have historically removed supply-risk pricing rapidly and with little reversal. For gold, the killer is different: a US Consumer Price Index (CPI) print on Wednesday that comes in materially below expectations would reduce the case for real-yield erosion that has been supporting gold even as risk appetite fluctuates — and that would challenge both sides of the current safe-haven argument simultaneously.
