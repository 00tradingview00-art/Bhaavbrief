## Macro Thread

Overnight, US economic data reinforced expectations that the US Federal Reserve's rate committee (FOMC) will hold rates higher for longer, lifting the dollar index and triggering fear-driven selling across commodities globally. The direct MCX implication falls hardest on gold, which dropped **₹2,998** to **₹131,225/10g**, with a stronger dollar raising the import cost floor while simultaneously reducing gold's appeal as a hedge against currency erosion. The level to watch today is whether COMEX gold holds above **$3,794/oz** — a close below that would signal the selloff has institutional conviction behind it, not just intraday repositioning.

## Dollar-Strength Trade Reasserts — BUILDING

Three sessions of rupee strengthening and crude softening had quietly built a dollar-weakness assumption into MCX prices. That assumption broke overnight. The dollar index firming — not a geopolitical event, not supply news — is the single mechanism driving today's broad decline across gold (**-2.23%**), silver (**-3.39%**), crude (**-2.09%**), and copper (**-1.99%**) simultaneously. What changed versus yesterday: Edition #148 noted crude telling "a different story" from gold's mild recovery; today both are telling the same story, and the story is dollar.

## The Market Is Saying

A stronger dollar has repriced nearly every commodity on MCX in a single session, and the breadth of the decline is the signal worth reading. Gold at **₹131,225** has surrendered gains built across two prior sessions; the **-2.23%** move reflects investors pulling back from gold as a safe harbour when the instrument doing the hedging work — the rupee — itself weakens against the dollar. Silver at **₹163,950** fell **-3.39%**, wider than gold's move, and that gap matters: the industrial half of silver's demand story (solar panel manufacturing, semiconductors) is amplifying the selloff beyond what pure safe-haven unwinding would produce alone. Crude at **₹7,921** is down **-2.09%**, with WTI at **$84.82** — the geopolitical premium that briefly appeared to stabilise crude near **₹8,000** has now been overridden by demand-side doubt embedded in the dollar-strength read. Copper at **₹1,251.95** fell **-1.99%**, consistent with softening manufacturing expectations rather than any single supply event. Natural gas at **₹296** is essentially flat (**-0.30%**), a reminder that Henry Hub at **$3.17** — up **+1.51%** overnight on early-winter demand signals — operates on its own weather-driven logic and carries no dollar-strength sensitivity of the same character.

## Historical Context

Dollar-strength episodes have historically produced broad, simultaneous declines across precious metals and industrial commodities on MCX, with the pressure typically most acute in the first one to two sessions before traders reassess whether the dollar move is structural or temporary. The twist worth watching: gold is falling in a period when global rate expectations remain unsettled — historically, once the initial dollar-strength shock passes, the safe-haven demand for gold has reasserted, making a sustained multi-session breakdown the exception rather than the rule. EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report releases — due tonight at 9:00 pm IST — have historically moved MCX Crude by an average of **2.49%** (max **8.13%**) in the following session, based on the last 24 occurrences, which means crude's next directional move may be data-driven rather than narrative-driven.

## What Kills It

The dollar-strength narrative breaks if tonight's EIA petroleum data shows a sharper-than-expected inventory draw, which would reintroduce a supply-side floor under crude independent of currency dynamics. For gold and silver, the narrative loses grip if COMEX gold closes back above **$3,794** in tonight's session — that would indicate the selloff was intraday positioning rather than a sustained repositioning away from precious metals. A meaningful rupee recovery through the **₹93.30** level would also reduce the import-cost pressure that is amplifying MCX declines beyond the COMEX move.
