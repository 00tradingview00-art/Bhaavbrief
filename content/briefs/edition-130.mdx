## Macro Thread

WTI crude fell **$4.45** overnight to **$99.21** — its sharpest single-session drop in weeks — while Brent simultaneously rose **$0.90** to **$110.45**, producing a Brent premium of more than **$11** per barrel that reflects a genuine fracture in how markets are reading the geopolitical supply threat. ## War Premium Fracturing — SHIFTING

The single trade that has dominated Indian commodity markets since edition 127 — geopolitical risk lifting gold and crude together — is no longer moving as one bloc. Yesterday's edition noted WTI had dropped nearly **$4**; today it drops another **$4.45**, while Brent moves in the opposite direction. This divergence is the market's way of separating "US domestic supply adequacy" from "Middle East shipping and export risk" — two very different stories that were briefly priced as one.

## The Market Is Saying

The WTI-Brent split is the loudest signal in today's session. WTI's collapse to **$99.21** suggests traders are gaining confidence that US domestic supply chains are insulated from the conflict, while Brent's climb to **$110.45** tells you the physical market for internationally traded crude — the kind India buys — still carries a meaningful geopolitical premium. COMEX gold fell **$22.10** to **$4,138/oz**, a move that reflects some cooling in fear-driven demand for gold as a safe harbour, but the pullback is modest relative to gold's recent climb from below **$4,200**. Silver is the outlier: COMEX silver rose **$0.68** to **$57.65/oz**, diverging from gold — and the gap between them is the industrial component speaking. The gold-silver ratio now sits at **71.8**, meaning silver is strengthening relative to gold, which historically points to industrial demand expectations holding up even as the pure safe-haven bid softens. MCX Copper at **₹1,398.65/kg** has not yet traded today, and its next print will matter: copper has no geopolitical premium to lose, so if it opens flat or lower, that tells a story about underlying manufacturing demand that the WTI number does not.

## Historical Context

In past episodes where WTI and Brent have diverged sharply during an active geopolitical event, the commodity that tends to lag longest is the one whose premium is built on uncertainty rather than actual supply loss. The twist worth watching: analysts on the other side of the current crude-premium trade argue that once a geopolitical event fails to produce a verified, sustained supply disruption, the entire premium built on fear historically unwinds faster than it built — and that Brent's current elevated level may be the last holdout before a catch-down to WTI, not WTI catching up to Brent. For natural gas, tonight's EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report (at 8:00 pm IST) and tomorrow's EIA Natural Gas Storage Report are the scheduled catalysts: EIA Natural Gas Storage Report releases have historically moved MCX Natural Gas by an average of **2.39%** (max **9.67%**) in the following session, based on the last 24 occurrences.

## What Kills It

The narrative of a fracturing war premium survives only as long as WTI keeps falling and Brent holds. A sudden, verified disruption to a major Middle Eastern export terminal — or a credible escalation in shipping lane security — would collapse the WTI-Brent spread and reunite crude's upward move. For gold, a resumption of fear-driven demand above **$4,160/oz** on COMEX would signal the safe-haven trade has not actually faded, merely paused. De-escalation announcements have historically stripped geopolitical premiums from both gold and crude quickly, but the mechanism works in reverse too: any confirmed supply event would rebuild the premium in a single session.
