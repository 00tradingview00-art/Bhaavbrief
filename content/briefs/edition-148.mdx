## Rupee Recovery Splits Commodity Markets — SHIFTING

For three consecutive editions, a weakening rupee amplified every imported commodity's pain. Today that force has reversed: USD/INR has pulled back to **₹93.69** from **₹93.98**, and the currency tailwind is now working differently for each commodity. Gold gets a lift in dollar terms that partially offsets the rupee's strengthening — MCX gold ends up modestly positive. Crude, however, is falling in dollar terms and cannot hide behind currency arithmetic, making today the first session where the two anchor commodities have moved in opposite directions since Edition #145.

## Historical Context

Episodes where gold rallies while crude and industrial metals slide simultaneously have historically preceded periods of heightened caution in broader markets — the commodity complex stops moving as a unified risk-on trade and begins fragmenting by function. In past instances of this fragmentation pattern, gold's safe-harbour demand has tended to deepen if the equity or currency stress persists beyond a few sessions. The twist worth watching: the contrary read, grounded in past Middle East supply-surplus episodes, is that gold's safe-harbour bid at these levels can prove short-lived once traders confirm the geopolitical risk premium in oil is genuinely deflating rather than pausing — cheaper oil historically reduces inflation anxiety, which in turn reduces one of gold's supporting arguments. EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report releases have historically moved MCX Crude by an average of **2.49%** (max **8.13%**) in the following session, based on the last 24 occurrences — tonight's report is therefore the most consequential single data point for crude's near-term direction.
