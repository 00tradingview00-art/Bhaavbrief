## Macro Thread

Overnight, gold on COMEX surged **3.13%** to **$4,195.60/oz** — the single largest one-day move in several weeks — as investors pulled back from risky assets ahead of the US Federal Reserve's rate committee (FOMC) conclusion, with Reuters and CNBC both noting that the fear-driven move into gold overwhelmed any dollar-strength headwind. On MCX, this translated directly into gold jumping **+1.25%** to **₹147,584/10g**, with the rupee's mild strengthening to **₹95.19** partially capping what would otherwise have been a larger domestic gain. Watch whether COMEX gold holds above **$4,150/oz** through the afternoon session — a retreat below that level would signal the overnight move was positioning noise rather than conviction.

## Safe-Haven Reassertion — BUILDING

After two consecutive editions where gold retreated on reduced geopolitical fear, the safe-haven demand for gold as a secure harbour has snapped back with force. The trigger is not new data — it is the absence of clarity: traders are unwilling to hold risk positions into an FOMC meeting where even a neutral statement could be read as hawkish. What has changed versus yesterday's edition is that gold is no longer drifting sideways at **₹141,506** — it has moved **₹6,078** in a single session, a **4.3% absolute rupee gain** from Wednesday's close, signalling that fear-driven positioning has returned in size.

## The Market Is Saying

The puzzle that defines this session is simple: COMEX gold is up **3.13%** but COMEX silver is up **4.44%** — and yet MCX silver sits unchanged at **₹229,236/kg**. Gold's **₹147,584** level, sitting **14.94% above** the import parity price of **₹128,403**, reflects the safe-haven component working through the system cleanly. Silver's COMEX surge to **$62.75/oz** should have pulled MCX silver higher, but the flat MCX print suggests domestic market participants are not yet extending the industrial leg of the silver thesis — the solar and semiconductor demand signal is not confirmed locally. The gold-silver ratio at **66.9x** is compressing from Wednesday's **68.4x**, which historically means silver is playing catch-up to gold's safe-haven move rather than leading on industrial demand. Crude at **₹6,589/bbl** and WTI at **$69.03** show a modest **+0.66%** move — geopolitical premium is present but contained, meaning this is not a supply-disruption day. Copper at **₹1,284.95/kg** is up **+0.78%**, consistent with steady manufacturing expectations, not a fear signal — the industrial lens is not flashing recession or boom, just continuity.

## Historical Context

In past episodes where gold surged sharply into an FOMC meeting — particularly when the prior two sessions had seen gold fall — prices have historically consolidated near the spike high in the 24 hours following the Fed statement before the next directional leg began. The MCX spread over import parity, currently at nearly **15%**, has in similar past instances reflected peak domestic hedging demand rather than sustained retail accumulation. The contrary read, based on past rate-pause cycles, is that any gold rally built entirely on FOMC ambiguity — rather than a confirmed dovish pivot — has historically reversed within 48 to 72 hours once the statement removes uncertainty in either direction.

## What Kills It

A US Federal Reserve statement that signals rates will remain elevated longer than the market expects — or explicitly pushes back against near-term cuts — would remove the ambiguity that is fuelling fear-driven gold demand. A simultaneous crude oil decline would confirm the geopolitical premium is deflating, removing the inflation-fear underpinning. Those two together, not just one, are the combination that historically deflates this trade quickly.

**Edge of the Day:** The MCX gold spread over import parity stands at **14.94%** — monitor whether this widens further through the session, as a spread above 15% has historically indicated domestic demand is absorbing the global move rather than arbitraging it away.
