## Crude-Led Inflation Fear Displacing the Post-Fed Relief Trade — REVERSING

Yesterday's post-Fed relief trade — gold surging as rate anxiety cleared — has run into a harder, louder story: crude oil pushing toward and through $100 per barrel, dragging inflation fears back to the centre of every trader's screen. WTI crude at $89.82 (up 0.98% on MCX at ₹8,549/bbl) is not just an energy story; it is a macro story. When oil rises this sharply amid Middle East tensions and signs of OPEC fracturing along political lines, the dominant question shifts from "what will the Fed do next?" to "how bad does inflation get if crude stays here?" That question is what is now driving every commodity move today.

## The Market Is Saying

Gold's retreat — MCX Gold at ₹1,54,801/10g, down 0.66%, with COMEX at $4,513/oz — is the clearest signal that the post-Fed relief trade has exhausted itself. Gold rose on the assumption that the US Federal Reserve's rate committee (FOMC) would ease the pressure on rates; but if crude at $100 reignites inflation, that rate-easing assumption becomes fragile, and gold loses the argument that drove it higher. Silver at ₹2,66,485/kg is up 2.11% — a divergence worth reading carefully. Silver's industrial demand profile means it benefits from the same inflationary impulse that crude is generating, without being as directly exposed to the rate-sensitivity that is weighing on gold. Crude itself at ₹8,549/bbl is confirming the narrative: the move is supply-driven, rooted in Middle East war risk and a reported fracturing within OPEC that raises doubts about coordinated production discipline. Copper at ₹1,354.15/kg, up 1.01%, and Natural Gas at ₹321.70/mmBtu, up 0.95%, are both participating — industrial commodities are reading this as a genuine supply-shock-driven inflation signal, not a fear-driven selloff. The USD/INR at ₹94.91 adds a domestic layer: a stronger dollar typically pressures rupee-denominated imports, meaning Indian commodity prices absorb an additional cost layer on top of the global crude surge.

## Historical Context

During past episodes of oil-led inflation scares — the 2022 Russia-Ukraine supply disruption being the most recent comparable — MCX Gold initially underperformed as rate-hike fears offset safe-harbour demand, while MCX Silver and MCX Copper outperformed on industrial inflation pricing. In those periods, the gold-silver ratio (how many units of silver equal one unit of gold) compressed, meaning silver caught up to and sometimes outpaced gold. Today's silver outperformance of 2.11% against gold's -0.66% is consistent with that historically documented pattern. MCX Crude, in past geopolitically driven oil spikes, has also shown a tendency to front-run peace-talk outcomes — meaning sharp reversals have occurred when diplomatic signals emerged unexpectedly.

## What Kills It

A credible ceasefire announcement or documented progress in Iran-related peace talks would remove the geopolitical premium from crude almost immediately. If WTI crude falls back below $85, the inflation-fear argument collapses, gold's rate-sensitivity narrative returns as the dominant force, and silver and copper lose their inflationary tailwind. Watch diplomatic newswire on Iran and any OPEC emergency meeting signals — either would be the circuit-breaker for today's dominant story.

**Edge of the Day:** WTI crude's ability to hold above $89 through the New York session — if it retreats below that level on peace-talk headlines or demand-concern data, today's inflation-fear narrative loses its anchor and gold's direction becomes the next question to answer.
