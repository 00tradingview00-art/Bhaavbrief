## Macro Thread

US Nonfarm Payrolls for October came in far ahead of expectations over the weekend, reinforcing the view that the American economy remains resilient even as energy costs have climbed. For MCX commodities, a stronger US labour market tightens the case for the US Federal Reserve's rate committee (FOMC) to hold rates higher for longer, which would ordinarily weigh on gold — yet gold is rising today, suggesting a separate fear-driven bid is overriding that logic. Watch whether COMEX Gold can hold above **$4,102** through the evening session; a close below that level would suggest the payrolls-rate pressure is finally winning.

## Geopolitical Premium Unwinding, Safe-Haven Bid Rebuilding — SHIFTING

Edition #142 identified a fracture: Brent crossed **$100** while gold fell, which was the wrong configuration for a clean geopolitical trade. That fracture is now resolving — but not in the direction crude bulls wanted. Brent has dropped to **$96.53**, WTI to **$89.69**, and the geopolitical premium that inflated crude across the past fortnight is visibly deflating. Meanwhile gold at **₹143,429** and silver at **₹187,082** are climbing, meaning investors are pulling back from energy exposure and rotating toward metals as a fear-driven safe harbour — a classic narrative shift, not a continuation.

## The Market Is Saying

The split between crude and gold today is the clearest signal this edition has seen in several sessions. Crude's **-1.32%** move on MCX, a drop of **₹113** from last close, tells you that traders are discounting the supply-disruption story — either ceasefire signals are circulating, or demand-side doubts from the strong payrolls print (higher rates, slower growth) are trimming the demand outlook. Gold's **+0.62%** gain of **₹877** reflects the other side of that same fear: investors who shed crude are not moving to cash, they are moving to gold. Silver at **+1.07%**, adding **₹1,982**, is outpacing gold, and the gap is meaningful — silver's industrial component (solar, semiconductors) is picking up alongside the safe-haven component, suggesting confidence in physical demand has not collapsed. Copper at **₹1,348.30**, up **+0.67%**, reinforces that read through its industrial-demand lens: manufacturing expectations are not deteriorating, which is inconsistent with a pure fear trade and suggests the crude selloff is supply-premium deflation, not a global-slowdown signal. Henry Hub natural gas fell sharply to **$3.02**, a move entirely disconnected from Middle East dynamics — nat gas trades on its own weather and storage fundamentals, and today's drop reflects neither geopolitics nor the broader metals bid.

## Historical Context

Past episodes where crude's geopolitical premium deflated while gold held or climbed have tended to produce a sustained, multi-session realignment rather than a single-day reversal. The contrary read is that gold's current rise may itself be borrowed: strong payrolls historically compress real-yield expectations in a way that reasserts dollar strength, and a firmer dollar has in past instances stripped the safe-haven bid from gold before the geopolitical story could reassert itself. The twist worth watching is whether today's gold move is a genuine rotation or simply the last buyers arriving late to a trade that the smarter positioning has already begun to unwind.

## What Kills It

A single credible de-escalation announcement — a ceasefire framework, a confirmed diplomatic channel, or a meaningful drawdown in regional military posture — would strip what remains of the fear-driven metals bid quickly. Historically, de-escalation signals have reversed safe-haven premiums in gold sharply, often within the same session the news breaks. On the crude side, the narrative revival would require a supply shock confirmation: a verifiable output disruption, not a threat. If WTI reclaims **$92** on hard supply news rather than sentiment, the geopolitical premium trade would have grounds to re-engage and would challenge gold's current lead.
