## Macro Thread

Overnight, WTI crude fell **$3.52** to **$98.27/bbl** while Brent moved in the opposite direction, rising **$1.61** to **$110.40/bbl** — a split of nearly **$12** between the two benchmarks that has no clean fundamental explanation beyond diverging market structures. MCX Crude, which last closed at **₹9208/bbl**, is likely to open under downward pressure from the WTI decline, though the Brent strength may limit how far that pressure carries. The key signal today is whether WTI holds above **$98** — a break below would suggest the geopolitical war premium embedded in US crude is genuinely unwinding, not just rotating.

## Crude's Dual Benchmark Split — BUILDING

Two editions ago, this newsletter flagged that WTI and Brent were beginning to diverge. Today, that divergence has hardened into a structural story: WTI is softening on US-specific supply dynamics while Brent continues to price Middle East risk. What has changed versus yesterday is the magnitude — the WTI-Brent spread widening to nearly **$12** is a level that forces traders, refiners, and importers to take a view on which benchmark is telling the truth about global supply tightness.

## The Market Is Saying

Something is wrong with the simple "oil is up because of the war" story. If Middle East risk were the sole driver, both benchmarks would move together — they are not, and that gap is the signal. COMEX Gold at **$4130.3/oz** slipped **$12.3** overnight, which, through the safe-haven and real-yield lens, suggests fear-driven demand for gold is easing marginally at the margin — not collapsing, but not accelerating. COMEX Silver fell a sharper **$1.00** to **$56.71/oz**; with silver's dual lens, the industrial component (solar, semiconductors) appears to be dragging harder than the safe-haven component, hinting that growth expectations are softening beneath the geopolitical noise. Henry Hub natural gas fell sharply to **$2.78/mmBtu**, a move of nearly **6.2%** — this is a standalone storage and weather story, entirely decoupled from Middle East dynamics, and it will set the tone for MCX NatGas (**₹264.7/mmBtu** last close) at open.

## Historical Context

In past episodes where WTI and Brent have diverged sharply while a geopolitical event remains unresolved, MCX Crude has historically reflected a blended signal — Brent's premium providing a floor while WTI's softness caps the upside. The EIA Natural Gas Storage Report releases tonight have historically moved MCX Natural Gas by an average of **2.39%** (max **9.67%**) in the following session, based on the last 24 occurrences — making tonight's report the single most quantifiable volatility trigger on today's calendar. The contrarian read on the WTI selloff is that it may be noise: past episodes of sharp intraday WTI drops during active Middle East supply disruptions have historically seen partial recoveries once US session positioning resets, suggesting the move overstates the actual change in supply risk.

## What Kills It

A confirmed ceasefire announcement or a verifiable de-escalation in the Middle East would collapse Brent's geopolitical premium rapidly — past de-escalation episodes have stripped that premium quickly, pulling Brent back toward WTI and narrowing the spread. On the other side, if US inventory data or rig count data show domestic supply building faster than expected, WTI's decline would look structural rather than temporary, and MCX Crude would face sustained downward pressure regardless of Brent. Tonight's EIA Natural Gas Storage Report at **8:00 pm IST** is the first scheduled data point that will test whether the Henry Hub selloff has legs.
