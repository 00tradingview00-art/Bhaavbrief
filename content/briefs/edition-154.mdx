## Macro Thread

Renewed Iranian attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz overnight pushed WTI crude up **$2.60** to **$84.65** and flipped the geopolitical premium — which had been fading since Edition #152 — back to the dominant force across energy markets. MCX Crude rose **1.33%** to **₹7,862/bbl**, with a weakening rupee at **₹92.93** amplifying the global price shock for Indian importers. The signal to watch today is whether Brent confirms or diverges: Brent is actually down **$0.30** to **$91.10**, while WTI surged — that wedge between the two benchmarks will indicate whether the premium is genuine supply disruption or a thinner, sentiment-driven spike.

## Geopolitical Supply Shock — BUILDING

Three editions ago, this narrative looked exhausted; today it has new oxygen. Iranian attacks on commercial tankers have reintroduced a credible physical supply threat, and WTI's **+3.17%** single-session move is the market's clearest statement that traders are repricing that risk. What changed versus yesterday: Edition #153 showed crude falling **-1.03%** as the safe-haven bid retreated — today that entire move is reversed and then some, with crude adding **₹103** from its prior close alone.

## The Market Is Saying

A sudden supply disruption in a critical shipping lane is the one trigger that makes gold, silver, and crude move in the same direction simultaneously — and that is exactly what today's tape shows. MCX Gold added **₹923** to **₹1,22,060/10g**, reflecting renewed demand for gold as a safe harbour as risk aversion spreads beyond energy. MCX Silver, which carries both a safe-haven and an industrial component, surged **₹4,200** to **₹1,46,717/kg** — a **+2.95%** move that outpaced gold's **+0.76%**, suggesting the industrial half of silver's demand is also being repriced as supply-chain anxiety rises. MCX Copper's **+0.65%** gain to **₹1,134.45/kg** is a mild positive through the industrial-demand lens, though it lags the energy and precious metals complex meaningfully — this restrained move says manufacturing expectations are not yet celebrating a supply shock, only adjusting. MCX Natural Gas fell **-2.12%** to **₹336.50/mmBtu** even as Henry Hub surged **+8.67%** to **$3.61** — that divergence is not noise; it reflects Indian domestic gas dynamics that are structurally decoupled from Middle East geopolitics, and the MCX drop deserves to be read on its own terms, not forced into the tanker-attack narrative.

## Historical Context

Geopolitical supply disruptions tied to Strait of Hormuz incidents have historically produced sharp, fast moves in crude that then test whether physical tightness is real or anticipated. The contrarian read, grounded in past Hormuz episodes, is that the premium fades quickly once shipping routes are confirmed open — tanker diversion adds cost and time, but rarely stops flows entirely, which historically strips the geopolitical component from prices before it can embed itself in fundamentals. Silver's outperformance of gold in similar risk episodes has historically signalled that the industrial demand component is being upgraded alongside the safe-haven bid — when those two forces align, silver's moves have been sharper and faster than gold's.

## What Kills It

A formal statement from Iranian authorities or a credible diplomatic intermediary confirming that the tanker incidents were isolated rather than policy-directed would strip the geopolitical premium from crude quickly. Past de-escalation announcements in similar episodes have historically reversed the energy spike within one to two sessions, while gold tends to hold its ground longer as rate-cut expectations absorb some of the safe-haven demand. Watch the Brent-WTI spread: if Brent cannot confirm WTI's move and closes Friday above **$91.10**, the physical tightness story strengthens; if Brent slides further, the spike looks more like a positioning event than a genuine supply alert.
