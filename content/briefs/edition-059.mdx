## Macro Thread

The single event shaping Tuesday's market is the anticipated Trump-Xi meeting, which traders are treating as a potential de-escalation signal in the US-China trade confrontation that has driven fear-driven demand for gold since April. If diplomatic language emerges from that meeting, the primary engine behind gold's climb from **$3992** to **$4198** in three editions faces its first credible challenge. Watch whether COMEX gold holds above **$4142** through the early US session — a break below **$4100** would confirm that positioning, not just caution, is unwinding.

## Safe-Haven Unwind — FADING

The trade that carried COMEX gold from **$3992** (Edition 056) to **$4198** (Edition 058) in under a week is now giving back ground, with COMEX at **$4142** and MCX Gold at **₹145800**, down **-0.76%** on the day. Yesterday's narrative was that weak US jobs data had opened the door for the US Federal Reserve's rate committee (FOMC) to cut rates, adding a second engine to the gold rally alongside geopolitical fear. What has changed today is the Trump-Xi meeting signal: the prospect of trade-war easing is pulling investors back from gold and toward riskier assets, draining one of the two engines that had been running simultaneously.

## The Market Is Saying

Gold retreating while crude advances is a specific message: the market is not broadly fearful today, it is selectively repricing geopolitical risk. COMEX gold is down **-0.32%** to **$4142**, while Brent crude is up **+0.74%** to **$72.52** — the crude move carries a geopolitical supply premium from Middle East tension, while the gold move suggests that premium is not spreading into broader fear. MCX Silver at **₹233136** is falling harder than gold at **-1.25%**, and with the gold-silver ratio at **67.4x** (down from 68.4x in Edition 056), silver's industrial half is also softening. MCX Copper at **₹1279.90**, down **-0.58%**, confirms that industrial demand expectations are not strengthening — a Trump-Xi meeting generating optimism about trade would ordinarily lift copper, and its absence here is the divergence worth watching. The USD/INR at **₹95.34** is marginally higher than yesterday's **₹95.21**, which amplifies MCX gold's rupee-denominated decline modestly beyond what COMEX alone explains.

## Historical Context

In past episodes where a dominant safe-haven rally met a credible diplomatic signal — US-China Phase One talks in late 2019 being the most cited parallel — gold historically corrected 3-6% from its recent peak before either resuming or consolidating, depending on whether the diplomatic signal produced a binding outcome. During similar periods, MCX gold's decline was partially cushioned by rupee weakness, which has historically absorbed 0.5-1% of the COMEX move. The twist worth watching: gold falling into an active Middle East tension environment has historically proven short-lived, rarely sustaining beyond 48-72 hours before demand for gold as a safe harbour reasserts — a clean break below **$4100** on COMEX would be the anomaly that challenges that historical pattern. The contrary read, held by analysts who track physical demand cycles, is that Indian jewellery buyers historically step in meaningfully when MCX gold retreats toward the import parity level, currently calculated at **₹126963**, providing a structural demand floor well below current prices.

## What Kills It

A Trump-Xi meeting that produces no joint statement or a breakdown in talks revives the fear trade immediately. Separately, any FOMC communication this week suggesting rate cuts are further away than June pricing implied would reinforce gold's real-yield attractiveness and rebuild the second engine of the rally.

## Who Is Affected

**Businesses:** A jewellery manufacturer sourcing gold at current MCX levels of **₹145800/10g** faces input costs that remain **₹18,837** above the import parity price of **₹126963** — this spread, at **14.84%**, reflects the accumulated safe-haven premium built over recent weeks, and a sustained fading of that premium would compress the spread toward historical norms. **Investors:** MCX Gold front-month contract participants are watching **₹145000** as the next observable support level — this was the approximate ceiling from the Edition 056 session and its role as a floor now defines near-term positioning. **Consumers:** Retail gold jewellery prices at physical stores, which typically track MCX with a 2-3 day lag, moved higher through last week's rally and are unlikely to fall at the counter unless MCX sustains below **₹144000** for multiple sessions.

**Edge of the Day:** The MCX gold import parity spread at **14.84%** — if this compresses below 12% as COMEX retreats further, it signals the safe-haven premium is being genuinely repriced, not just temporarily paused.

**Tomorrow:** US Federal Reserve FOMC meeting minutes release (timing approximately 11:30 PM IST) — language indicating the committee sees fewer than two cuts in 2026 challenges the rate-cut engine of the gold rally and deepens the current fading thesis; language consistent with two or more cuts would reactivate that engine and test whether geopolitical demand alone is enough to resume the uptrend.
