## Macro Thread

Overnight, COMEX gold surged **$89.1** to **$3,604.5/oz** — the sharpest single-session recovery since the three-day unwind began in Edition #149 — driven by a combination of renewed geopolitical tension signals and a softening in US Treasury yields that reduced the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets. For MCX, the direct implication is a sharp reversal in precious metals, with silver climbing **₹3,364** to **₹151,177/kg** and gold adding **₹2,744** to **₹124,522/10g**, amplified modestly by a weaker rupee at **₹92.84**. The level to watch today is whether COMEX gold can hold above **$3,604.5** into the US session close — a retreat below that level would suggest this recovery is positioning noise rather than a genuine narrative reversal.

## Safe-Haven Snap-Back — SHIFTING

Three consecutive editions tracked the safe-haven unwind as gold fell from above **₹131,000** to **₹122,857**. Today that unwind is in question. What has changed is the speed: gold and silver are not drifting back — they are snapping, which is the character of a short-covering move rather than a gradual fundamental re-entry.

## The Market Is Saying

Three sessions of safe-haven selling just met its first credible challenge. MCX Silver at **₹151,177/kg** leads the move at **+2.28%**, fractionally ahead of MCX Gold's **+2.25%** — the gap is small but meaningful. Silver carries a dual identity: part safe harbour, part industrial input for solar panels and semiconductors, and when both components align with a macro reversal, silver's move tends to outpace gold's. The gold-to-silver ratio sitting at **85.8** means gold is still historically expensive relative to silver, and a narrowing of that ratio typically reflects improving industrial confidence layered on top of safe-haven demand. MCX Crude at **₹7,761/bbl** is up only **+0.86%** even as geopolitical signals that lifted gold also circulated overnight — Brent's **-3.03%** slide to **$89.76** is the divergence that matters here, suggesting today's precious metals bid is predominantly safe-haven driven rather than a broad geopolitical risk premium. MCX Copper at **₹1,161.20/kg** added a modest **+0.72%**, consistent with cautious industrial optimism but not the kind of surge that would signal a full-throated demand recovery. MCX NatGas at **₹303.60/mmBtu** fell **-0.98%** even as Henry Hub jumped **+6.04%** to **$3.62** — this divergence is likely contract-timing related and, as always, NatGas carries no Middle East geopolitical premium.

## Historical Context

Snap-back sessions following multi-day safe-haven unwinds have historically been volatile in both directions for precious metals. The twist worth watching: the contrary read from analysts on the other side of this trade is that sharp single-session recoveries after a prolonged unwind are often exhaustion bounces — historically, the fundamental driver that caused the original selling (rising real yields or a firming dollar) has not yet reversed, which means the recovery risks fading if those macro conditions reassert. The rupee at **₹92.84** is slightly weaker than the prior close, adding a marginal cushion to MCX prices in rupee terms, but that cushion is thin and can reverse quickly if dollar demand resumes.

## What Kills It

The clearest kill for this snap-back narrative is a renewed climb in US Treasury real yields or a fresh dollar strengthening episode — either would reprice the opportunity cost of holding gold and silver and resume the unwind. A COMEX gold close back below **$3,604.5** tonight would be the first observable signal that today's recovery lacked follow-through buying. De-escalation in any geopolitical flashpoint that is currently supporting safe-haven demand would similarly strip the premium quickly, as past episodes of sudden tension-reduction have shown.
