## Macro Thread

Brent crude fell **$6.79** overnight — one of the sharpest single-session drops in recent weeks — as markets reassessed the actual supply disruption risk embedded in Middle East tensions, with no confirmed infrastructure hit triggering a partial unwind of the geopolitical premium. MCX Crude, last closing at **₹9,800/bbl**, faces downward pressure at Tuesday's open, as the Brent selloff removes the risk-war premium that had been supporting Indian oil import costs. The level to watch today is whether WTI holds above **$101.38** — a close below that would confirm that the geopolitical premium is structurally deflating, not just pausing.

## Geopolitical Premium Unwind — SHIFTING

The narrative that has dominated Indian commodity markets for weeks — a war-risk floor under crude prices — is now visibly shifting. Brent's **-5.82%** overnight move is not a routine pullback; it is the market reducing the probability weight on a direct supply-infrastructure disruption. What changed versus yesterday is that Edition #132 tracked crude bouncing back toward **₹9,516** as the hot-war framing held. Today, Brent is doing the opposite, suggesting that framing is losing participants — even as the conflict itself continues.

## The Market Is Saying

The overnight price action raises a direct puzzle: crude is falling sharply while Henry Hub natural gas surged **13.18%** to **$2.99/mmBtu** — two commodities, two entirely different stories on the same night. Brent at **$109.93** after falling **$6.79** from its prior close tells you the market believes the supply threat was exaggerated relative to prices already paid. WTI at **$101.38**, down **$1.89**, is falling less aggressively than Brent — the WTI-Brent spread compression is worth watching as an indicator of where the geopolitical discount is unwinding faster. On gold, COMEX slipped **$7.00** to **$4,000.90/oz**, a modest move that signals investors pulling back from risky assets are not rushing into gold as a safe harbour either — the safe-haven demand for gold is not amplifying the crude selloff, which historically happens when the risk-off impulse is commodity-specific rather than macro-broad. COMEX Silver at **$53.88/oz**, up **$0.17**, is holding fractionally — its industrial component (solar, semiconductors) is providing a small offset to the safe-haven softness, which is exactly the divergence the dual-lens framework predicts when broader risk sentiment is mixed.

## Historical Context

Episodes where a large geopolitical premium unwinds from crude without a confirmed de-escalation — the conflict persists but the supply-disruption probability is repriced lower — have historically produced sharp, uneven moves across the commodity complex. The natural gas market in past such episodes has behaved as a standalone signal entirely disconnected from crude's geopolitical narrative, driven instead by weather and storage data. The twist worth watching: the contrarian read, based on past conflict-premium cycles, is that partial unwinds of this kind have historically reversed sharply when a single infrastructure incident confirms what the market just stopped pricing — meaning the premium can return faster than it departed.

## What Kills It

One confirmed attack on a Gulf oil-loading terminal or pipeline would immediately reassert the geopolitical premium and reverse the Brent selloff. On the natural gas side, a warmer-than-expected weather outlook from the NOAA — which releases its Heating Degree Day (HDD) forecast Tuesday at **1:30 am IST** — could challenge the Henry Hub surge by reducing near-term demand assumptions. De-escalation announcements from either party to the conflict have historically stripped the crude premium quickly; any ceasefire headline is the single biggest overnight risk to holding the current bearish crude thesis.

**Businesses:** An oil marketing company importing at typical refinery volumes faces a materially lower crude bill at today's Brent level versus last week's highs — but the fortnightly retail fuel revision window means consumers will not see that relief immediately, and if the premium reasserts before the next revision, the lower input cost may never pass through. **Investors:** MCX Crude front-month traders are most exposed, with the market focused on whether **₹9,800/bbl** — the last close — holds as a reference or breaks lower on Tuesday's open in line with Brent's overnight move. **Consumers:** Piped cooking gas and CNG users face a delayed signal — the Henry Hub surge of **13.18%** in the US points toward tighter global LNG supply, which historically feeds into Indian city gas pricing with a lag, meaning gas bills face upward pressure even as petrol and diesel costs may ease.
