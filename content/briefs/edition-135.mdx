## The Market Is Saying

The gap between WTI and Brent narrowing dramatically overnight is the most unusual signal on the board this morning. Brent's drop implies a supply story specific to non-US grades — possibly Middle East export flows or a demand read from European buyers — while WTI's more modest decline at **$100.87** suggests the US domestic picture remains tighter. Gold at **$4,073.5** is behaving as a fear-driven safe harbour, not a growth asset; it is rising alongside a crude selloff, which happens when investors are uncertain about the direction of the global economy rather than simply optimistic or pessimistic. Silver at **$54.38/oz** rose only **+0.49%** against gold's **+0.87%**, and the gold-silver ratio sits at **74.9** — silver's industrial component is dragging on what would otherwise be a stronger safe-haven move. MCX Copper at **₹1,397.4/kg** has not traded yet today, but the Brent drop is not consistent with strong manufacturing demand, and any softness in copper when MCX opens would confirm that the industrial demand story is weakening alongside the crude price. Henry Hub at **$3.07** is a standalone story driven by early winter weather positioning — it carries no Middle East geopolitical premium and should be read entirely on its own supply-demand logic.
