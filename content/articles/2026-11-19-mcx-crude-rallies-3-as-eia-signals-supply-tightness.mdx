**WHAT HAPPENED**

**MCX Crude is up ₹232/bbl (3.00%) to ₹7733/bbl**, outperforming all other energy and metals in the evening session, while **MCX Natural Gas gained 2.77% to ₹315/mmBtu**. The divergence reveals a supply-side narrative: EIA's weekly petroleum report (Wednesday 8 PM IST) showed crude inventory flat at **+0.0 mb** for the week ending 13 November, but natural gas storage drew **612 Bcf** — faster than the seasonal 5-year average of 540 Bcf for this week. Zero crude inventory movement at a time when refineries typically draw inventory for winter processing is tighter than expected, signalling demand is holding or margins are compressing.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

This is consistent with US refinery maintenance cycles (peak September–October) now receding, allowing refiners to replenish working inventory without accumulating stock. A flat EIA print in this window means physical buyers are absorbing supply as fast as it arrives — historically, that precedes 1–2 weeks of draws as seasonal heating demand kicks in. The natural gas draw acceleration (612 Bcf vs 540 Bcf average) reflects early winter demand; Henry Hub sits at **$3.37/mmBtu** while MCX Natural Gas at current levels (**₹315** × **₹93.44** USD/INR = **$3.37/mmBtu parity**) suggests zero basis premium, meaning Indian importers face no structural advantage on LNG procurement. The crude complex has held the **₹7667** intra-day low firmly, with WTI at **$82.34** supporting the **₹7733** level (≈ **$82.34** × **₹93.44** transmission).

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

**IOC, BPCL, and HPCL face a decision on forward crude hedging into late November**: the flat EIA print and rising natural gas storage draws suggest the refinery margin squeeze (GRM compression from crude rising faster than product prices) may ease if crude consolidates here rather than extending higher. HPCL's Visakh and Kochi refineries, which process a higher sour-crude diet, benefit immediately if crude stabilises — their heavy-light differential (typically favoring complex refiners) narrows when light WTI spikes faster than heavy grades. OMCs holding un-hedged inventory contracts expiring this week face marking-to-market losses of approximately **₹7–12 crore per mb of unhedged crude** at today's levels if they defer fresh long-term purchases.

**BOTTOM LINE**

The market has repriced the November–December heating season from "demand destruction" risk to "steady seasonal demand with flat supply"; the flat EIA crude print is not bearish here, it is neutral-to-bullish because it suggests physical tightness, not abundance. The key shift: until yesterday, crude was selling on perceived global demand softness; today's EIA confirms US demand is holding, rebalancing sentiment from "build expected, sells off" to "balanced, stable here".

**WHAT TO WATCH**
