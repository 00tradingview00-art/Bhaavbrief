**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Natural Gas has declined **₹6.70 to ₹292/mmBtu (-2.25%)** in the evening session, mirroring weakness in Henry Hub at **$3.12/mmBtu (-0.92%)**. This move reveals a structural bearish signal: US natural gas storage is running materially above the 5-year seasonal average heading into November withdrawal season, which historically should see tightening inventories as heating demand rises. The market is pricing in that this storage surplus will persist through winter, suppressing Henry Hub economics and transmitting directly to MCX via NYMEX linkage.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

Henry Hub at **$3.12** sits in the marginal LNG export zone — most US export trains remain economically viable, but fewer incremental cargoes are being sanctioned. At this level, European TTF and Asian JKM buyers face weaker incentives to bid aggressively for US LNG cargoes, allowing domestic inventory builds to continue. The **₹292 MCX level is 1.3% above the 20-SMA of ₹291**, confirming the selloff has only partially corrected recent strength; USD/INR stability at **₹93.93** means the rupee is not offsetting the Henry Hub decline, so the full dollar-denominated weakness is flowing through to Indian traders.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

GAIL and Petronet LNG, India's primary LNG importers, see reduced pressure to activate costlier spot procurement — their term contracts become more competitive relative to spot, allowing them to defer expensive spot cargoes into December and beyond. City gas distribution networks (IGL in Delhi, MGL in Mumbai) will not face margin compression from LNG spike, but they also lose the opportunity to pass lower costs immediately to end consumers due to 30–60 day lag in tariff adjustments. Fertilizer producers reliant on gas feedstock (60% of urea) face no near-term relief, as APM-priced domestic gas remains insulated from global moves, but any government decision to relax domestic gas price controls would now encounter weaker global reference benchmarks, reducing political cost.

**BOTTOM LINE**

US storage surplus at a structurally elevated level is incompatible with a cold-winter premium. Unless temperatures plummet sharply in December or an unexpected supply disruption occurs, Henry Hub will remain anchored in the **$2.80–$3.30** range, keeping MCX Natural Gas range-bound and pressuring any recovery rally toward **₹300** (resistance 294, 302 above).

**WHAT TO WATCH**

**EIA weekly storage report (Thursday, 8:30 PM IST)**: Watch for the storage draw vs 5-year average. If the draw is **more than 80 Bcf below the seasonal norm**, it confirms the surplus regime persists and MCX Natural Gas will test **support at ₹289** by Friday close; a draw **closer to or above average** would invalidate this selloff and trigger a snap recovery above **₹300**.
