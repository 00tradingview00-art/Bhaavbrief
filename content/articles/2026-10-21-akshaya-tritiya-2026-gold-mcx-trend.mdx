## Introduction

Traders tracking the akshaya tritiya 2026 gold buying mcx price trend are really tracking four separate variables — COMEX spot, USD/INR, Indian import duty, and seasonal physical demand — colliding at a single calendar date. Historically, the weeks surrounding Akshaya Tritiya compress all these forces into a narrow window, producing price behaviour distinct from ordinary trading sessions.

## The Mechanism

The transmission from global gold markets to MCX runs through a precise formula: MCX Gold (₹/10g) = (COMEX $/troy oz ÷ 31.1035) × 10 × USD/INR × 1.12. That final multiplier of 1.12 embeds the current effective duty stack — 6% Basic Customs Duty plus 3% Agriculture Infrastructure Development Cess plus 3% GST, as restructured in the July 2024 Union Budget.

Step-by-step, the mechanism works as follows. COMEX prices move on US real yield expectations and dollar strength. A rising US 10-year TIPS yield increases the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold, historically pressing COMEX lower. That COMEX print, converted at the prevailing USD/INR fixing, is then scaled by the duty multiplier to arrive at import parity on MCX. Around Akshaya Tritiya specifically, Indian jewellers and wholesalers front-load physical procurement in the weeks prior, tightening domestic supply and occasionally lifting the MCX spot premium above import parity. Central bank demand data from the World Gold Council's quarterly reports can accelerate or dampen the COMEX leg before the festival window even opens.

## India-Specific Context

Indian gold prices diverge from COMEX in ways that are structural, not incidental. Each 1% change in import duty shifts MCX price by approximately ₹1,000 per 10g, independent of any global move — this is pure policy risk. A depreciating rupee amplifies MCX prices even when COMEX is flat; a 1% INR weakening against the dollar adds roughly the same proportional lift to the domestic price. MCX contracts trade in lot sizes of 1 kg (100 units of 10g), meaning basis risk between COMEX and MCX can crystallise quickly when the government adjusts duty mid-cycle or when RBI intervenes in currency markets. SEBI circuit limits on MCX gold (typically ±6% daily) can also interrupt price discovery during sharp global moves, trapping positions that were hedged against a freely moving COMEX reference.

## Historical Episodes

In 2020, Akshaya Tritiya fell during the COVID-19 lockdown with physical markets closed, yet MCX gold futures surged roughly 40% over the broader April–August window as COMEX broke above $2,000/oz and the rupee weakened past ₹76, illustrating how the formula's two components can amplify simultaneously. In 2022, the Russia-Ukraine conflict drove a geopolitical premium into COMEX through February–March, lifting MCX Gold approximately 8–10% in the weeks around the festival before US Federal Reserve rate signals reversed part of the move. In 2023, the government's surprise duty reduction from 15% to 6% BCD (with AIDC restructuring) compressed MCX prices by roughly ₹5,000–6,000 per 10g almost overnight, demonstrating how duty changes dwarf short-term demand seasonality.
