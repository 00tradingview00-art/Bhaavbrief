### 3. Cross-Asset Connections

USD/INR (the exchange rate between the US dollar and Indian rupee) is quoted at **₹94.95**. This is a relatively firm rupee. When the rupee strengthens, COMEX commodity prices — which are in dollars — translate into lower MCX prices in rupees, all else equal. Today, that mild rupee strength partially offsets even a flat COMEX session, creating a slight downward bias at MCX open. The gold-silver ratio (how many silver units equal one gold unit) remains range-bound, signalling no strong preference between the two metals currently.

### 4. Import Parity Check

Gold had the largest relative overnight reference move. Here is the math:

- COMEX Gold: approximately **$3,290 per troy ounce**
- 1 troy ounce = 31.1035 grams; MCX trades in 10 grams
- COMEX price per 10g = ($3,290 ÷ 31.1035) × 10 = **$1,058.06**
- Convert to rupees: $1,058.06 × ₹94.95 = **₹100,463**
- Add basic customs duty (approximately 10%) + GST (3%) = roughly 13% total levy
- Theoretical MCX fair value: ₹100,463 × 1.13 ≈ **₹1,13,523**

*MCX Gold trading near ₹97,800 sits below this theoretical import parity level. The gap reflects local supply-demand, trader positioning, and timing differences — not a mispricing signal.*
