## Introduction

Understanding how India's gold hallmarking rules affect MCX gold demand requires tracing the link between retail jewellery policy and physical gold consumption at the wholesale and futures level. When the government mandates certified purity standards, it directly reshapes the volume and composition of gold flowing through the supply chain — and that shift eventually registers on MCX.

## The Mechanism

India's Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) hallmarking mandate — rolled out in phases from June 2021 — requires jewellers to sell only BIS-certified gold (HUID-marked, minimum 14K/18K/22K). The transmission to MCX prices works as follows.

First, jewellers who previously used below-standard alloys or recycled, unverified gold must now source certified, refined gold from approved refiners or via import channels. This raises demand for verifiable, high-purity gold — the same grade underlying MCX contracts.

Second, non-compliant stock held by smaller jewellers gets liquidated or withheld from the retail market, temporarily compressing visible supply. Third, compliant jewellers increase procurement from certified refiners, lifting physical offtake. This higher physical demand feeds into the import-parity price that anchors MCX:

**MCX Gold (₹/10g) = (COMEX $/troy oz ÷ 31.1035) × 10 × USD/INR × 1.12**

The 1.12 multiplier reflects 6% Basic Customs Duty + 3% AIDC + 3% GST (post-July 2024 budget). Any surge in certified physical procurement amplifies import volumes, which — when duty remains constant — tightens the domestic market and can push MCX prices above the import-parity floor. The spread between MCX spot and COMEX-derived fair value widens during these demand spikes.

## India-Specific Context

India's gold price does not simply mirror COMEX because of three structural layers. The import duty regime (currently 6% BCD + 3% AIDC) means each 1% duty change shifts MCX by approximately ₹1,000/10g independently of global moves. The rupee conversion adds a second variable — a depreciating INR amplifies MCX prices even when COMEX is flat. GST at 3% on jewellery purchases further alters consumer price points, affecting the volume at which retail demand clears.

MCX contracts trade in lots of 1 kg (100 units of 10g), making them accessible to jewellers and merchants hedging physical inventory. SEBI regulations prohibit compulsory physical delivery structures used in some global markets, so MCX predominantly serves as a price-discovery and hedging venue. RBI's foreign exchange management rules also govern gold import financing, meaning credit conditions and LC (letter of credit) availability influence how quickly hallmarking-driven demand translates into actual imports.

## Historical Episodes

In 2021, when BIS hallmarking became mandatory for jewellers in 256 districts, compliance-driven procurement by larger certified jewellers contributed to a domestic premium over import parity widening to approximately ₹500–₹800/10g through late 2021, against a near-zero premium in the preceding months.

In 2022–23, as hallmarking extended to smaller towns and HUID registration volumes crossed 100 million pieces, recycled gold (kacha gold) was partially displaced from the supply chain. This structural shift supported Indian physical demand running above the five-year average, even as COMEX corrected nearly 12% from its 2022 peak.

In the July 2024 Union Budget, a duty cut from 15% to approximately 9% (combined BCD + AIDC) triggered a single-session MCX move of roughly ₹4,000–₹5,000/10g, demonstrating how policy-driven demand unlocks interact sharply with the import-parity formula.

## What to Watch

Monitor BIS HUID registration data (published monthly on the BIS portal) for volume acceleration. Track DGCI&S monthly gold import tonnage for demand confirmation. Watch COMEX front-month prices and USD/INR spot daily — both feed directly into the import-parity calculation. RBI MPC meeting dates (roughly every two months) can shift rupee volatility around gold pricing. World Gold Council quarterly demand reports (released 4–6 weeks after each quarter ends) provide the most reliable hallmarking-linked jewellery offtake estimates. MCX circuit limits (currently ±6% daily) cap intraday moves during sharp policy surprises.
