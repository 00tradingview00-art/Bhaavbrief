## Introduction

Understanding what is the best time to trade MCX commodity India volatility begins with recognising that MCX price action clusters around three distinct windows: the European open, the US session overlap, and post-settlement adjustments from COMEX, LME, and NYMEX. Traders who time their participation around these windows historically capture a disproportionate share of intraday range.

## The Mechanism

MCX commodity prices transmit global benchmark moves through a live rupee conversion chain. The step-by-step pathway works as follows:

1. **Global benchmark moves** — COMEX gold, LME copper, or NYMEX crude settles at a new price in USD per troy ounce, metric tonne, or barrel.
2. **Spot USD/INR conversion** — MCX's pricing engine applies the prevailing RBI reference rate or live interbank USD/INR to convert the dollar price into rupees.
3. **Import parity adjustment** — For physically deliverable metals, the landed cost formula applies: `MCX Price ≈ (Global Benchmark × USD/INR) + Basic Customs Duty + IGST + Insurance + Freight`. For crude oil, the formula substitutes refinery gate economics.
4. **Contract-specific lot multiplier** — Each MCX contract (e.g., Gold Mini = 100 grams, Crude Oil = 100 barrels) scales the per-unit price to a contract value in INR.
5. **MCX circuit filter activates** — If the resulting price move breaches MCX's daily price limit (typically ±4% for precious metals, ±6% for energy), trading halts temporarily, compressing liquidity and widening bid-ask spreads.

The highest volatility window on MCX therefore coincides with 7:30 PM–9:00 PM IST, when COMEX opens and USD/INR liquidity is still live.

## India-Specific Context

Indian MCX prices diverge from global benchmarks for several structural reasons. Basic Customs Duty on gold currently sits at 15%, meaning a 1% move in COMEX gold amplifies into a larger INR price change when the rupee simultaneously depreciates. IGST at 3% on gold adds a further fixed layer. For crude oil, India's import dependence means domestic prices track Brent closely, but the PPAC-administered price revision cycle can delay retail pass-through. SEBI's position limits on MCX restrict large operators from carrying oversized overnight exposure, which periodically causes sharp mark-to-market squeezes near contract expiry. RBI's intervention in the USD/INR market can momentarily decouple MCX gold from COMEX when the rupee is managed within a narrow band, creating brief arbitrage windows that resolve quickly once RBI steps back.

## Historical Episodes

Three episodes illustrate the magnitude of volatility that concentrated timing can produce:

- **2020 (COVID safe-haven surge):** MCX Gold breached ₹56,000 per 10 grams in August 2020, a move of approximately 45% from January 2020 levels, driven by simultaneous COMEX strength and a rupee that weakened past 75 to the dollar.
- **2022 (Russia-Ukraine, crude spike):** MCX Crude Oil touched ₹9,000 per barrel in March 2022, rising nearly 60% in under three months, tracking NYMEX WTI above $130 while USD/INR held near 76–77.
- **2023 (Banking stress, gold gap-up):** Following the Silicon Valley Bank collapse in March 2023, MCX Gold gapped up approximately 3–4% at the Monday open, compressing within two sessions as COMEX consolidated — illustrating how weekend global events translate into sharp MCX opening moves.
