## Introduction

The MCX trading holidays 2026 list determines every session gap Indian commodity traders must account for when holding overnight or multi-day positions in gold, silver, crude oil, and base metals. MCX observes a defined set of national and exchange-specific holidays each year, and gaps in trading sessions directly affect margin requirements, price discovery, and settlement obligations.

## The Mechanism

When MCX closes for a scheduled holiday, the global benchmark markets — COMEX for gold and silver, NYMEX for crude oil, and LME for copper, aluminium, and zinc — continue trading uninterrupted. This creates a price gap risk that transmits to MCX prices the moment the exchange reopens.

The transmission pathway works as follows. During the MCX closure, COMEX gold may move — say, from $2,300 to $2,350 per troy ounce. When MCX reopens, the new MCX gold price is recalculated using the import parity formula:

**MCX Price (INR/10g) = (COMEX price in USD/troy oz ÷ 31.1035) × spot USD/INR × 10 × (1 + import duty rate) × (1 + GST rate)**

If the rupee simultaneously weakens from ₹83.50 to ₹84.20 per dollar during the holiday, both the dollar move and the currency move stack — amplifying the opening gap. MCX sets daily price limits (circuit filters) at ±3% for most contracts, and ±6% as an extended limit. If the gap at opening breaches these bands, the exchange invokes a temporary call auction mechanism before resuming continuous trading. Open interest carried through the holiday session faces mark-to-market settlement the next available trading day, compressing what would normally be gradual price adjustment into a single opening print.

## India-Specific Context

India's commodity price structure adds layers that global benchmarks do not capture. Gold and silver imports attract a basic customs duty of 15% plus an Agriculture Infrastructure Development Cess of 5%, making Indian landed cost structurally higher than COMEX prices. GST at 3% applies to physical gold transactions. Crude oil imports carry customs duty plus domestic petroleum levies that partially insulate domestic prices from sharp daily NYMEX swings — though MCX crude contracts do track NYMEX closely because they are cash-settled against international prices converted at the RBI reference rate. SEBI's position limits and margin frameworks also constrain how quickly large participants can reposition after a holiday gap, which sometimes extends post-holiday price volatility beyond a single session.

## Historical Episodes

In March 2020, MCX gold opened approximately 2–3% higher on the first trading day after a weekend-plus-holiday gap, as COMEX surged amid early pandemic-driven safe-haven demand — the move was compressed into a single open rather than spread across multiple sessions. In April 2022, MCX crude oil contracts saw opening gaps of roughly 4–5% after extended holiday closures coincided with rapid NYMEX moves driven by Russia-Ukraine supply disruptions; the extended circuit filter was triggered on at least one occasion. In October 2023, a mid-week MCX holiday during a period of Middle East geopolitical tension caused a sharp single-session adjustment in MCX gold of approximately 1.5–2%, as the rupee and COMEX both moved against existing open positions simultaneously.

## What to Watch

Monitor these specific signals ahead of any date on the MCX trading holidays 2026 list:

- **RBI MPC meeting dates** — rupee volatility spikes around policy announcements
- **COMEX and NYMEX settlement prices** on the evening before a holiday — these anchor the opening gap calculation
- **EIA weekly petroleum report** (every Wednesday, US time) — if it falls during an MCX closure, crude gaps widen
- **IMD monsoon progress reports** — affect agri-commodity sentiment and indirect metal demand
- **LME open interest data** — signals base metal positioning heading into the gap
- **MCX circuit filter width** — currently ±3% initial, ±6% extended for most metals contracts
