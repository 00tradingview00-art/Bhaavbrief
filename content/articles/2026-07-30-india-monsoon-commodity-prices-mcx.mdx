## Introduction

Understanding how India's monsoon forecast affects commodity prices on MCX starts with a single fact: roughly 50% of India's agricultural output depends on kharif rainfall, making the IMD's seasonal outlook a direct input into futures pricing. When that forecast shifts — from normal to deficient, or vice versa — the ripple moves through agri commodities, edible oils, energy demand, and even metals within days.

## The Mechanism

The transmission pathway runs in a clear sequence. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) releases its Long Range Forecast (LRF) in April, with an update in June. The LRF quantifies rainfall as a percentage of the Long Period Average (LPA); below 90% is classified deficient, above 110% is excess.

**Step 1 — Crop output expectation shifts.** A deficient forecast immediately revises down kharif acreage projections for soybean, cotton, castor, and chana. Traders reprice crop size.

**Step 2 — Import parity pricing kicks in.** If domestic supply is expected to fall short, the import parity price becomes the price floor. For refined soy oil on MCX, that calculation runs approximately as: *Import Parity Price = (CBOT Soy Oil in USD/tonne × Exchange Rate) + Import Duty (currently 5.5% on crude soy oil) + GST (5%) + Port Handling + Inland Freight*. A deficient monsoon compresses the gap between domestic price and import parity, keeping prices elevated.

**Step 3 — Edible oil complex reprices.** Soy oil, mustard oil (RM Seed futures), and refined palmolein on MCX move in correlation because they are substitutable in India's consumption basket.

**Step 4 — Indirect effects on energy and metals.** Lower agricultural output raises rural income uncertainty, softening domestic demand expectations for industrial commodities. Simultaneously, a weak monsoon historically narrows the current account, pressuring the rupee, which in turn lifts MCX gold and silver prices mechanically through the INR conversion of COMEX benchmarks.

## India-Specific Context

India's commodity pricing diverges from global benchmarks through several structural layers. Import duties on edible oils — currently around 5.5% on crude variants and 13.75% effective duty on refined — create a price wedge that the domestic market trades above or below depending on monsoon expectations. GST at 5% on edible oils is non-recoverable for small traders, embedding it into the price floor.

For MCX-listed agri contracts, SEBI's position limits and the mandatory delivery mechanism in commodities like castor seed (traded on NCDEX, not MCX) mean that open interest can compress sharply when monsoon uncertainty is high. MCX's circuit filter of ±4% to ±6% on agri contracts can suspend price discovery precisely when monsoon news is breaking, creating gap openings. The rupee's response to a below-normal monsoon forecast — typically a 1–2% depreciation over two to three weeks — adds a layer of currency-driven upside to MCX metal prices independent of COMEX.

## Historical Episodes

In 2023, when IMD revised its monsoon forecast downward in August citing El Niño effects, MCX castor seed futures rose approximately 8–10% over four weeks as sowing area in Gujarat contracted. Mustard (RM Seed) on NCDEX saw similar tightening.

In 2018, a patchy spatial distribution of rainfall — with Madhya Pradesh receiving only 83% of LPA — pushed MCX soybean futures up roughly 12% between July and September, even as CBOT soy moved less than 4% over the same period, demonstrating the India-specific premium.

In 2015, deficient monsoon conditions (87% of LPA nationally) contributed to a sustained rally in chana futures, with prices rising nearly 40% from June to December as domestic production shortfall estimates widened progressively.

## What to Watch

- **IMD LRF release dates**: typically April 15 and June 1 each year
- **IMD weekly rainfall bulletins**: published every Wednesday during June–September
- **Kharif sowing progress data**: Ministry of Agriculture releases it weekly from June onward
- **USDA WASDE report**: monthly, for global oilseed supply context
- **RBI MPC meeting dates**: rupee sensitivity to rate decisions amplifies monsoon-driven metal moves on MCX
- **MCX circuit breaker triggers**: watch for ±4% moves in agri contracts on IMD update days as a signal of position unwinding
