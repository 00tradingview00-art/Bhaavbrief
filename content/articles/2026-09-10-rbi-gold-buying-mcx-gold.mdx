## Introduction

Understanding how RBI gold buying affects MCX gold India prices requires tracing a chain that runs from sovereign reserve decisions through global benchmarks, import parity arithmetic, and rupee conversion before landing on your terminal. When the RBI accumulates gold, it tightens global supply, signals institutional demand, and exerts indirect but measurable pressure on the domestic price structure.

## The Mechanism

RBI gold purchases operate through two parallel channels: the global COMEX benchmark and the domestic import parity calculation.

**Global channel:** The RBI buys gold on international markets, typically through the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) or over-the-counter dealer networks. Large sovereign purchases, when aggregated with other central bank buying — particularly from EM central banks diversifying away from USD — reduce the available float of physical gold. This tightens the supply-demand balance on COMEX, historically pushing spot USD prices higher.

**Domestic channel:** MCX Gold prices are not set independently — they derive from import parity:

> **MCX Gold (₹/10g) = (COMEX $/troy oz ÷ 31.1035) × 10 × USD/INR × 1.12**

The 1.12 multiplier reflects post-July 2024 Budget duties: 6% Basic Customs Duty + 3% AIDC + 3% GST. When COMEX rises because of central bank demand, the numerator increases directly. Simultaneously, if the RBI's reserve accumulation signals rupee-defensive policy, USD/INR can soften slightly — partially offsetting the COMEX lift. The net effect on MCX depends on which force dominates: in most past episodes, COMEX appreciation has outpaced any rupee strengthening, producing higher MCX prints.

## India-Specific Context

India's domestic gold price is structurally more expensive than the global benchmark due to layered taxation. The current effective duty load of roughly 12% means every COMEX move is amplified by that factor before reaching MCX. Additionally, MCX contracts trade in ₹/10g on a 1 kg lot size, so traders are exposed to both COMEX volatility and USD/INR basis risk simultaneously.

A critical India-specific variable is that how RBI gold buying affects MCX gold India prices also depends on the government's willingness to adjust import duty. A 1% duty change shifts MCX by approximately ₹1,000/10g — a policy lever that can absorb or amplify external price moves. The July 2024 Budget cut duties sharply, compressing the MCX-COMEX spread temporarily. SEBI circuit limits on MCX (typically 6% per session) can also interrupt price discovery during sharp global moves, creating intraday gaps that resolve at the next open.

## Historical Episodes

**2022 — Post-Ukraine central bank surge:** Global central bank gold purchases hit a 55-year high (World Gold Council data). COMEX rose roughly 15% from March lows to the August peak. MCX Gold moved from approximately ₹50,000 to ₹53,000/10g across the same window, partly cushioned by a temporarily stronger rupee.

**2023 — EM reserve diversification wave:** As de-dollarisation narratives intensified, central banks — including the RBI adding over 16 tonnes in H1 — contributed to COMEX touching $2,080/oz. MCX correspondingly approached ₹62,000/10g, with the weak rupee (near ₹83/$) amplifying the import parity calculation.

**2024 — RBI accelerated buying, duty cut:** The RBI added over 72 tonnes through the year. Despite the July duty cut compressing MCX by roughly ₹4,000-5,000/10g overnight, the underlying COMEX rally to record highs above $2,700/oz pushed MCX to new records near ₹80,000/10g by year-end.

## What to Watch

- **WGC Quarterly Demand Trends report** — published ~4 weeks after quarter-end; shows RBI and global central bank tonnage
- **RBI Weekly Statistical Supplement** — foreign exchange reserves table discloses gold holdings in USD value
- **COMEX front-month settlement** — daily reference for import parity calculation
- **USD/INR spot rate** — RBI's FX intervention posture directly affects MCX basis
- **Union Budget / CBIC notifications** — duty change announcements trigger immediate MCX repricing of ~₹1,000/10g per 1% change
- **US 10Y TIPS real yield** — rising real rates historically compress gold's upside regardless of central bank buying
