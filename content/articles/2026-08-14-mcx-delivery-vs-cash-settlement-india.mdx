## Introduction

The choice between MCX delivery vs cash settlement commodity India contracts determines whether a position closes with physical goods changing hands or simply a cash difference — and the mechanism behind each path affects pricing, margin, and basis risk in fundamentally different ways. Understanding how MCX commodity contracts expire is not optional for serious market participants; it directly governs P&L, warehouse logistics, and hedging effectiveness.

## The Mechanism

At expiry, MCX contracts follow one of two paths depending on the commodity specification.

**Cash-settled contracts** (crude oil, natural gas, most base metals on MCX) settle against a reference price — typically the last half-hour weighted average price (WAP) of the contract on expiry day, or a benchmark like NYMEX/LME converted to INR. The formula is:

> **Final Settlement Price (FSP) = International Benchmark Price × RBI Reference Rate ± Exchange-defined adjustment**

MCX publishes the FSP on its website after market close on expiry day. Open positions that are not squared off are automatically settled at this FSP. No physical commodity moves.

**Delivery-settled contracts** (gold, silver, agricultural commodities) require the seller to tender a Delivery Allocation Notice (DAN) to MCX's clearing corporation (MCXCCL). The buyer receives a Delivery Order (DO) against an MCXCCL-accredited warehouse. The settlement price is the Exchange Delivery Settlement Price (EDSP), calculated as the weighted average of the last few trading sessions. The buyer pays the seller EDSP × lot size, plus applicable GST and assay charges. Physical goods transfer within the stipulated delivery period — typically two to four days post-expiry.

Basis (spot price minus futures price) converges toward zero as expiry approaches; any divergence creates arbitrage that professional participants actively close.

## India-Specific Context

Indian commodity prices at expiry diverge from global benchmarks for several structural reasons. Import duty on gold currently sits at 15%, meaning the MCX gold EDSP inherently carries a premium over COMEX spot when domestic supply is import-dependent. GST at 3% on gold and 18% on most industrial commodities layers additional cost into physical delivery economics. Currency conversion uses the RBI's official reference rate published at approximately 1:30 PM IST, creating a brief window of rupee-rate uncertainty for cash-settled contracts. SEBI's 2021 circular mandating compulsory delivery for large open positions in agricultural futures added regulatory teeth to delivery discipline. MCX contract lot sizes — 1 kg for gold, 30 kg for silver — are calibrated to Indian trade quantities, unlike CME contracts sized for institutional global flows. Collectively, these factors mean MCX expiry prices regularly trade at a measurable premium or discount to simple currency-adjusted global prices.

## Historical Episodes

**Gold, October 2020:** When international gold was around USD 1,900/oz, MCX gold futures expired near ₹51,000/10g — roughly 8–10% above the simple currency-adjusted COMEX price — because import duty and pandemic-driven supply disruptions widened the India premium sharply.

**Crude oil, April 2020:** The NYMEX WTI May contract went negative globally. MCX crude cash-settled contracts fell approximately 30% intraday before circuit limits were triggered, settling near ₹965/barrel — exposing how international benchmark dislocations transmit directly into cash-settled MCX positions.

**Natural gas, October 2021:** Global LNG tightness pushed MCX natural gas futures up nearly 40% over two weeks into expiry, with the final settlement tracking Henry Hub's surge converted at prevailing RBI rates.

## What to Watch

- **MCX expiry calendar**: Published monthly; delivery intention period opens 4–5 days before expiry
- **RBI reference rate**: Released daily at ~1:30 PM IST; critical for all cash-settled FSP calculations
- **COMEX/LME/NYMEX settlement prices**: Published after US market close; next-day MCX open reflects any overnight move
- **MCX open interest data**: Sharp OI decline in final week signals squared-off positions versus delivery intent
- **SEBI circulars on compulsory delivery**: Regulatory threshold changes alter delivery economics immediately
- **MCX circuit filter limits**: Typically 4–6% for metals; breach triggers settlement review
