**WHAT HAPPENED**
**MCX Natural Gas fell 2.80% to ₹294.90/mmBtu** while the broader commodity complex — gold, silver, copper — all advanced on dollar weakness, signaling that natgas is selling off on supply fundamentals, not macro risk-off. **Henry Hub sits at $3.10**, down from **$3.20** yesterday, as US weather forecasts have shifted cooler early-autumn expectations to normal-to-mild patterns through mid-October. The EIA weekly storage report (released Thursday ~8:30 PM IST) showed a draw of **65 Bcf**, below the 5-year seasonal average of **72 Bcf**, confirming that injection-season tightness has eased and storage levels are normalizing faster than historical norms.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
At **Henry Hub &lt;$3.10**, US LNG export economics turn marginal for smaller producers; export volumes moderate and global oversupply pressure mounts. **MCX NatGas tracks NYMEX with USD/INR amplification** — the **3.63%** session loss mirrors Henry Hub's weakness plus a softer rupee environment (USD/INR at **₹95.06**). This transmission is mechanical: spot Henry Hub moves 10%, MCX typically follows within 0.5–2 sessions. The storage draw below 5-year average is the critical structural signal — injection season (May–October) is ending without building the tight cushion that would support winter prices, meaning November–March withdrawal season will face ample inventory and seasonal downward price pressure.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
**City gas distribution (CGD) operators — IGL, MGL, Mahanagar Gas, Gujarat Gas** — operate on cost-plus margins and benefit operationally when LNG import costs (Petronet Dahej/Kochi spot prices, which correlate to Henry Hub) decline. A sustained **$3.10** Henry Hub level typically translates to spot LNG around **$11–12/mmBtu**, reducing their feedstock burden and narrowing the lag before margin recovery in retail CNG and piped-gas bills. Conversely, **gas-based fertilizer plants (urea producers using captive feedstock)** face a reprieve: lower feedstock costs reduce pressure on government subsidy budgets, potentially unlocking deferred capacity restarts through Q4 2026.

**BOTTOM LINE**
The structural shift is mild-weather normalization ending any near-term Henry Hub support. We are no longer in an injection-season squeeze; storage is building to normal levels, and the market has repriced away winter tightness risk. Without a cold-snap catalyst or geopolitical LNG disruption, Henry Hub will test downside toward **$2.80–3.00** before buyers step in.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
**EIA weekly storage release next Thursday (23 October, ~8:30 PM IST)** — if the draw continues at 65–70 Bcf (below 5-year avg), MCX will likely tag **₹290 support**. Conversely, a draw of 80+ Bcf above seasonal norms would trigger a snapback into the **₹300 round level** and confirm that winter demand is accelerating earlier than models suggest.
