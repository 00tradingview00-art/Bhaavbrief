## Introduction

Understanding how India's strategic petroleum reserve affects crude prices requires knowing that SPR releases add domestic supply without changing global benchmarks, creating a temporary divergence between WTI movements and actual MCX delivery economics. India's three underground SPR facilities — at Visakhapatnam, Mangaluru, and Padur — hold roughly 5.33 million metric tonnes of crude, and their activation or replenishment cycles feed directly into domestic price signals that MCX traders cannot afford to ignore.

## The Mechanism

When the Indian Strategic Petroleum Reserve Limited (ISPRL) releases crude into the domestic market, the transmission pathway runs as follows.

ISPRL releases stored crude → domestic refiners (IOC, BPCL, HPCL) gain access to inventory outside normal import channels → effective supply in the Indian market rises without a corresponding WTI or Brent move on NYMEX → the import premium embedded in MCX pricing faces downward pressure.

Under normal conditions, MCX Crude prices follow the import parity formula:

**MCX Crude (₹/bbl) = WTI ($/bbl) × USD/INR × 1.025**

The 1.025 multiplier captures the import duty differential and basic customs duty loading. When ISPRL releases stock, refiners temporarily reduce spot import volumes. This softens the demand pressure on the USD/INR leg — importers buy fewer dollars to pay for cargoes, which can modestly support the rupee, further compressing the INR translation of WTI prices. Conversely, when ISPRL replenishes reserves — typically buying crude opportunistically during price dips — it adds to import demand, putting upward pressure on both USD/INR and the effective MCX price, even if WTI holds steady. The magnitude depends on replenishment volume relative to India's daily import run-rate of approximately 4.5 million barrels per day.

## India-Specific Context

India's MCX Crude contract is denominated in ₹ per barrel with a lot size of 100 barrels, meaning every ₹1 move in the contract equals ₹100 in profit or loss per lot. This INR denomination means the rupee's movement against the dollar is an independent price driver — a rupee depreciation from ₹83 to ₹85 against the dollar lifts MCX Crude by roughly 2.4% even if WTI does not move.

Customs duty on crude imports currently sits at zero, but the 1.025 import parity multiplier reflects other landed cost components. GST does not apply directly to crude but affects downstream products. SEBI-mandated daily circuit limits on MCX Crude — typically ±4% with an extended ±6% limit — mean that even if a global shock drives WTI beyond those thresholds overnight, MCX prices cannot reflect the full move in a single session, creating a staggered price discovery that traders need to account for across consecutive sessions.

## Historical Episodes

In **2020**, when the COVID-19 demand collapse briefly drove WTI futures to negative territory on NYMEX, MCX Crude hit circuit limits on consecutive sessions and settled near ₹965 per barrel — a move of over 60% from its pre-crash levels in weeks — exposing how violent the INR translation can be during extreme global events.

In **2022**, following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, Brent surged past $130/barrel. India simultaneously began negotiating discounted Russian Urals crude. ISPRL deferred replenishment, effectively leaving domestic supply cushioned while global benchmarks ran — MCX Crude moved approximately 40% over three months before retreating.

In **2023**, when ISPRL announced coordinated SPR releases alongside the IEA, MCX Crude softened by roughly 5–7% over the subsequent two weeks even as WTI recovered partially, illustrating the domestic supply-side divergence the mechanism can create.

## What to Watch

- **US EIA inventory data**: every Wednesday, 8 PM IST — a draw historically correlates with WTI strength feeding into MCX
- **OPEC+ meeting calendar**: each confirmed production cut of 1 mbpd has historically moved WTI ₹500–₹1,000 per barrel on MCX in past episodes
- **ISPRL tender notices**: published on the PPAC and ISPRL websites when replenishment tenders are floated
- **USD/INR rate**: RBI MPC decisions (bi-monthly) directly affect the INR translation multiplier
- **China PMI**: released monthly — weak manufacturing PMI has historically been the sharpest demand-side dampener on MCX Crude
