**WHAT HAPPENED**

**MCX Natural Gas has fallen ₹9.50/mmBtu (2.10%) to ₹308.00/mmBtu**, tracking a weakening Henry Hub at **$3.25/mmBtu** (down **1.6%** session). The move reveals renewed bearishness on US natural gas supply adequacy—this is injection season (May–October), and recent EIA storage data is printing well above the 5-year seasonal average, signaling oversupply relative to historical norms. With Henry Hub below **$3.30**, LNG export margins from US coastal terminals are compressing, reducing incentive for US producers to push cargoes into the Asian spot market (JKM), which directly suppresses competition for Indian import volumes.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

Henry Hub at **$3.25** translates to an import parity check for Indian LNG buyers: at current **USD/INR 95.28**, a typical cargo landed in India (Dahej or Kochi) costs approximately **₹310–312/mmBtu** after terminal margins and regasification. This means MCX NatGas at **₹308** is now *below* spot import cost for new cargoes, creating a structural arbitrage—traders holding long positions face mark-to-market losses if Henry Hub remains range-bound. The weakness in MCX NatGas also reflects reduced JKM premiums; Asian LNG spot prices have not strengthened despite October demand seasonality, confirming that US storage surplus is keeping a lid on global LNG pricing even as winter approaches.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

**Petronet LNG** and **GAIL**, India's primary LNG importers, face a procurement timing decision: spot LNG at current Henry Hub levels is attractive for November–December delivery (heating season), but locking in tonnage this week assumes Henry Hub will not weaken further. Conversely, **IGL (Indraprastha Gas)** and **MGL (Mahanagar Gas)**, which source LNG feedstock via cost-plus pass-through, will see downstream CNG and piped gas rates remain flat or ease slightly in the next 30–60 days if this weakness persists—deferring any price hikes they might have telegraphed to retail customers.

**BOTTOM LINE**

US storage surplus is overriding seasonal heating-demand tailwinds; this is a structural shift from last October when an early cold snap pushed Henry Hub above **$4.50**. Until EIA storage moves back to the 5-year average (currently tracking ~10% above), Henry Hub has a downside bias, and MCX NatGas will struggle to break above **₹315/mmBtu** resistance.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

**EIA weekly storage report this Thursday (~08:30 PM IST)**: if the draw is more than 80 Bcf below the 5-year average for this week, Henry Hub could spike 5–8%, pulling MCX NatGas back to **₹318–320** by Friday close. Any surprise cold weather forecast for the US Northeast over the next 10 days would flip this narrative instantly.
