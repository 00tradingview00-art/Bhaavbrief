## Introduction

Understanding how global manufacturing PMI affects MCX copper prices is essential for any Indian trader or jeweller tracking copper's volatile swings. PMI releases — particularly from China, the Eurozone, and the US — act as the single most watched forward indicator for copper demand, and their impact flows to MCX within hours of publication.

## The Mechanism

Manufacturing PMI (Purchasing Managers' Index) measures factory activity. A reading above 50 signals expansion; below 50 signals contraction. Because copper is consumed overwhelmingly in construction, electrical wiring, and industrial machinery, any shift in factory activity directly alters demand expectations.

The transmission pathway works in steps:

1. **PMI released** (China Caixin PMI drops on the first business day of each month) → LME 3-month copper prices reprice within minutes on the London Metal Exchange
2. **COMEX copper** (US proxy, quoted in US cents per pound) follows LME, often gapping at the New York open
3. **MCX price** is derived via import parity:

   **MCX Copper (₹/kg) = COMEX (¢/lb) ÷ 100 × 2.20462 × USD/INR × 1.05**

   The 1.05 factor captures basic customs duty and landing costs. If COMEX moves from 420¢/lb to 440¢/lb and USD/INR holds at 83, MCX moves from roughly ₹769/kg to ₹806/kg — a ₹37/kg swing on a 2,500 kg lot translates to approximately ₹92,500 contract-level exposure.

4. **LME warehouse stocks** amplify or dampen the PMI signal. A strong PMI landing alongside falling LME inventory creates a compounded move; rising inventory softens it.

The mechanism is mechanical and fast. Indian MCX prices typically reflect the global PMI reaction before the morning session opens.

## India-Specific Context

Indian copper prices do not move one-for-one with LME or COMEX because of structural overlays unique to the domestic market.

The basic customs duty on copper imports (currently 5%) is baked into the import parity formula. GST at 18% applies at the point of domestic trade but does not directly affect MCX futures pricing. The USD/INR exchange rate introduces a second variable — a weakening rupee inflates MCX prices independent of any global copper move. During periods when RBI intervenes aggressively to defend the rupee, this dampens the full pass-through of a COMEX rally.

MCX copper contracts are sized at 2,500 kg with prices quoted in ₹ per kg, making a single lot worth roughly ₹24 lakh at current price levels. SEBI's position limits and circuit filter rules (typically ±6% daily) can halt price discovery precisely when global PMI reactions are most violent — a critical operational detail for traders managing intraday exposure.

## Historical Episodes

**2015–2016 China slowdown:** When China's official manufacturing PMI fell below 50 consecutively through late 2015, LME copper dropped from approximately $6,000/tonne to under $4,400/tonne — a fall of over 25%. MCX copper fell in parallel from around ₹340/kg to near ₹280/kg during the same period.

**2020 pandemic recovery:** After the April 2020 collapse, China's Caixin PMI rebounded sharply to 54.4 by July 2020. LME copper rallied nearly 60% over the following twelve months. MCX copper climbed from roughly ₹370/kg to above ₹720/kg by May 2021, partly amplified by a weakening dollar.

**2022 rate shock:** When US ISM Manufacturing PMI began contracting mid-2022 alongside aggressive Fed rate hikes, COMEX copper fell from above 480¢/lb to near 315¢/lb. MCX copper dropped approximately 25% over six months despite rupee depreciation partially cushioning the fall.

## What to Watch

- **China Caixin Manufacturing PMI**: Released on the first business day of each month — this is the highest-impact single data point for copper
- **US ISM Manufacturing PMI**: Released the same day; confirms or contradicts the China signal
- **LME copper warehouse stock levels**: Published daily at lme.com — watch for consecutive weekly drawdowns
- **USD/INR spot rate**: RBI reference rate published each afternoon affects next-day MCX opening
- **Chile/Peru news wires**: Strike actions at Escondida or Cerro Verde frequently coincide with PMI-driven moves, amplifying price swings
- **MCX circuit filter status**: A ±6% daily limit can freeze positions when PMI reactions are sharpest
