## Introduction

How gold recycling scrap supply affects MCX gold prices is a question every Indian jeweller and commodity trader should understand precisely, because recycled gold directly competes with imported gold in setting domestic price levels. When scrap supply rises sharply, it reduces India's reliance on imports, compresses the import-parity premium, and pushes MCX prices lower relative to COMEX benchmarks.

## The Mechanism

Recycled gold enters the domestic market through jewellers, refiners, and bullion dealers who melt old jewellery, coins, and industrial scrap into tradeable bars. This supply bypasses the import channel entirely, meaning no basic customs duty, no AIDC, and no GST is paid on the metal itself — it already exists within India's borders.

The import parity formula that anchors MCX pricing is:

**MCX Gold (₹/10g) = (COMEX $/troy oz ÷ 31.1035) × 10 × USD/INR × 1.12**

This 1.12 multiplier reflects the 6% BCD, 3% AIDC, and 3% GST levied post the July 2024 Union Budget. When scrap supply is abundant, domestic physical gold trades at a discount to this import parity level — dealers have no reason to import when cheaper recycled metal fills demand. This discount narrows the spread between spot physical prices and MCX futures, pulling MCX prices toward the lower physical reference rather than the full import-parity anchor. The transmission pathway runs: rising scrap volumes → lower physical dealer premiums → softer basis on MCX → MCX price underperforms COMEX on a rupee-adjusted basis.

## India-Specific Context

India's gold market carries structural features that amplify scrap supply's impact. First, the import duty layer (currently ~12% all-in) means the spread between COMEX-converted prices and domestic prices is unusually wide by global standards, creating room for recycled metal to trade at meaningful discounts without threatening dealer margins. Second, India's large informal gold stock — estimated at over 25,000 tonnes held by households — means latent scrap supply is enormous and price-sensitive; a sharp MCX rally historically triggers rapid monetisation. Third, RBI's restrictions on gold imports by certain entities and the mandatory hallmarking regime under BIS affect how quickly recycled gold enters the formal supply chain. MCX's 1 kg lot size means even modest scrap-driven price softness can affect mark-to-market positions significantly.

## Historical Episodes

In 2020, when MCX gold touched ₹56,200/10g in August driven by pandemic uncertainty, recycled scrap volumes in India surged by an estimated 15–20% year-on-year as households and jewellers monetised holdings at elevated prices — contributing to the subsequent cooling from peak levels even as COMEX remained firm. In 2013, the global gold price correction of approximately 28% triggered a wave of distress selling and scrap recycling globally; India's recycled supply spiked, though elevated import duties introduced mid-year partially cushioned the MCX downside. In 2022, when the rupee weakened past 82/USD while COMEX corrected, MCX held relatively firm — but recycled supply from jewellers squeezed by thin margins kept physical premiums contained, limiting MCX's upside relative to what rupee depreciation alone would have implied.

## What to Watch

Monitor the World Gold Council's quarterly demand trends report, which publishes India-specific recycled gold tonnage data. Watch MCX physical delivery premium or discount relative to import parity — a consistent discount signals excess domestic supply, including scrap. Track domestic hallmarking centre throughput data from BIS as a proxy for formal recycled volumes. RBI MPC dates matter because rate decisions influence the rupee, which shifts import parity and therefore the incentive to recycle versus import. COMEX options expiry dates can spike volatility that triggers fresh waves of household scrap selling in India.
