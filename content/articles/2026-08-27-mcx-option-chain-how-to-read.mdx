## Introduction

Understanding how to read MCX option chain data India-wide is a foundational skill for any serious commodity trader operating on the Multi Commodity Exchange. The option chain is not a price predictor — it is a real-time map of market positioning, hedging pressure, and implied volatility across strike prices.

## The Mechanism

The MCX option chain displays calls and puts for a given commodity contract — gold, silver, crude oil, natural gas — across multiple strike prices for a defined expiry. Here is how each column functions in sequence.

**Open Interest (OI):** The total number of outstanding contracts at each strike. High call OI at a strike signals that a large number of participants have paid premium for upside protection or speculation at that level. High put OI indicates the same on the downside.

**Change in OI:** Tells you whether new positions are being created or old ones are being unwound. Rising OI alongside rising premium indicates fresh positioning. Falling OI with rising premium suggests short covering.

**Implied Volatility (IV):** Derived by reverse-engineering the Black-Scholes model from the market price of an option. When IV spikes, the market is pricing in an expected large move. IV across strikes is rarely flat — the resulting shape (smile or skew) reveals directional bias.

**Put-Call Ratio (PCR):** Calculated as: PCR = Total Put OI ÷ Total Call OI. A PCR above 1 indicates more put writing or buying, historically associated with hedging pressure on the downside. A PCR below 0.7 suggests concentrated call activity.

**Greeks (Delta, Gamma, Theta):** Delta measures how much an option price moves per ₹1 change in the underlying MCX contract. Gamma measures how fast delta itself changes. Theta measures daily time decay — critical for near-expiry weekly contracts on MCX.

Reading across a row — strike by strike — reveals where the market's pain points and maximum concentration lie.

## India-Specific Context

MCX option chains carry India-specific distortions that global chain readings do not. Gold and silver options on MCX are priced in INR per 10 grams and INR per kg respectively, not in USD, so the USDINR exchange rate is baked into the premium itself. A sharp rupee depreciation can inflate option premiums even when COMEX gold is flat.

Import duty on gold (currently 15% basic customs duty plus GST) creates a structural premium in MCX spot versus London spot, which anchors strike selection differently from international norms. MCX also enforces circuit limits — typically 4% to 6% for metals and 5% for energy — which can freeze option pricing during high-volatility sessions, rendering mid-session chain data temporarily unreliable. SEBI regulates the strike intervals and lot sizes, meaning not every strike has liquid OI, and illiquid strikes carry wide bid-ask spreads that distort IV readings.

## Historical Episodes

In 2020, during the COVID-19 demand collapse, MCX crude oil April futures briefly turned negative in the international session, and the MCX option chain for crude showed call IV collapsing while put IV surged to extreme levels — the PCR on crude compressed below 0.4 within two sessions, reflecting concentrated bearish positioning of a historically rare magnitude.

In 2022, when the Russia-Ukraine conflict triggered a commodity surge, MCX gold crossed ₹55,000 per 10 grams. The option chain revealed a sharp call OI buildup at ₹54,000–₹56,000 strikes, with IV on calls expanding by approximately 30–40% within a week.

In 2023, the Indian government's surprise hike in gold import duty caused MCX gold to gap up roughly 2–3% intraday, and the option chain's put OI at lower strikes unwound rapidly as those positions became deep out-of-the-money overnight.

## What to Watch

Specific triggers that historically cause MCX option chain data to shift sharply:

- **US Federal Reserve FOMC statement dates** — affects gold and silver IV immediately
- **RBI MPC decision dates** — USDINR movement reprices INR-denominated options
- **EIA weekly petroleum inventory release** — every Wednesday, 8:30 PM IST — crude and natural gas chains react within minutes
- **SEBI circular dates** — lot size or margin changes alter OI patterns structurally
- **MCX expiry calendar** — option chain data thins dramatically in the final two sessions before monthly expiry, distorting PCR readings

Learning how to read MCX option chain data India traders access through the MCX website or third-party platforms requires cross-referencing these triggers against OI shifts — not reading any single column in isolation.
