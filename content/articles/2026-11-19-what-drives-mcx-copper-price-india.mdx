## Introduction

What drives MCX copper price in India explained simply: global COMEX/LME prices, converted through an import parity formula, then layered with India-specific duties and rupee movement. Copper is one of the most macro-sensitive commodities traded on MCX, making its price a live read on global industrial health.

## The Mechanism

The transmission from global markets to MCX copper runs through a precise chain.

It begins on the LME in London and COMEX in New York. LME Copper 3-Month is the global benchmark; COMEX is the dominant US futures proxy. Prices on both exchanges move in near lock-step, though a spread — the COMEX-LME basis — occasionally opens due to regional arbitrage flows.

MCX copper follows COMEX through an import parity calculation:

**MCX Copper (₹/kg) = COMEX (¢/lb) ÷ 100 × 2.20462 × USD/INR × 1.05**

The 2.20462 converts pounds to kilograms. The 1.05 multiplier captures basic import costs — customs duty and handling. The USD/INR rate is applied live; a weakening rupee mechanically lifts MCX prices even when COMEX is flat.

The underlying demand signal comes primarily from China, which consumes over 50% of global refined copper. When China's Caixin Manufacturing PMI rises above 50, or when Beijing announces infrastructure stimulus, copper prices historically react within hours on LME, and MCX follows at the Indian market open. Mine supply is the other lever — strikes in Chile's Escondida or Peru's Las Bambas mines, which together account for roughly 20% of global output, historically produce sharp supply-side price spikes. LME warehouse stock levels are a real-time gauge: falling inventories signal tightness and historically precede price acceleration.

The AI and energy-transition narrative adds a structural layer — copper is essential in EV motors, charging infrastructure, and power grids, sustaining long-term demand expectations that keep a floor under prices during cyclical dips.

## India-Specific Context

India does not have significant domestic copper mine production; it is effectively a price-taker from global markets. The import parity formula is therefore not theoretical — it is the actual pricing anchor.

Beyond the formula, MCX copper trades in lots of 2,500 kg, with a contract value of approximately ₹24 lakh at current price levels. This makes it a high-capital instrument compared to other MCX metals.

India's basic customs duty on copper cathodes and IGST create a structural premium over raw LME prices in rupee terms. SEBI-mandated position limits and MCX circuit filters (typically ±4–6%) cap intraday volatility on the exchange itself. When RBI intervenes in currency markets to stabilise the rupee, it indirectly dampens the currency transmission component of copper price moves. Conversely, during periods of sharp rupee depreciation — as seen in 2022 — MCX copper prices in rupees diverged meaningfully from COMEX moves in dollar terms.

## Historical Episodes

**2020 COVID recovery:** After crashing to around ₹380/kg in April 2020, MCX copper rallied over 80% to approximately ₹700/kg by early 2021, driven by China's faster-than-expected industrial rebound and collapsing LME inventories.

**2022 Fed tightening cycle:** As the US dollar surged and Chinese property sector stress intensified, COMEX copper fell roughly 35% from its March 2022 peak. MCX copper dropped from around ₹900/kg to approximately ₹600/kg by July 2022, though the simultaneously weakening rupee partially cushioned the fall in INR terms.

**2024 COMEX squeeze:** A short squeeze on COMEX in May 2024 pushed COMEX copper to record highs above 500 ¢/lb. MCX copper breached ₹900/kg, with the COMEX-LME basis widening sharply as US-listed contracts temporarily priced far above London.

## What to Watch

- **China Caixin Manufacturing PMI** — released first business day of each month
- **LME copper warehouse stock levels** — updated daily on the LME website
- **COMEX copper front-month price and COMEX-LME basis** — daily spread signals arbitrage pressure
- **USD/INR spot rate** — real-time rupee moves directly alter MCX levels via the import parity formula
- **Chile/Peru labour contract cycles** — Escondida's union contracts historically expire mid-year
- **MCX daily circuit limit** — a triggered circuit often signals an outsized global move overnight
