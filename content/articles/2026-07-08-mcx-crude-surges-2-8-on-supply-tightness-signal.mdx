**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Crude has accelerated **2.82% to ₹6901/bbl** — the only commodity in green on a subdued morning — because the WTI-Brent spread has widened to **$3.82** overnight, signaling a shift in physical tightness rather than a broad macro rally. COMEX WTI itself gained only **2.92%** to **$72.45/bbl**, meaning MCX has outpaced the dollar-adjusted move, pointing to domestic demand strength or a reversal of monsoon-season weakness. The trigger is emerging data that OPEC+ spare capacity has fallen closer to **2.8–3.0 mb/d**, down from the mid-2026 estimate of **3.2 mb/d**, narrowing the cartel's ability to flood the market without accepting price escalation.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

At current spare capacity levels, OPEC has lost the cushion to act as the swing producer without signaling commitment. When spare capacity sits above **4.0 mb/d**, the cartel can credibly punish cheaters; below **1.5 mb/d**, it becomes irrelevant. At **~3.0 mb/d**, every announced cut must be defended with real production discipline or the price signal collapses — and discipline historically fades after 8–12 weeks as fiscal-stressed members (Nigeria, Iraq, Venezuela all have breakevens above **$90/bbl**) leak barrels. Import parity arithmetic: WTI at **$72.45** converts to ~**₹9,430/bbl** at current **₹94.97/USD** before duty and shipping, yet MCX trades at **₹6901** — the **₹2,529** gap is the refiners' margin, and rising crude erodes that margin, forcing refiners to either hedge aggressively or delay capex.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

Indian Oil Corporation (IOC), BPCL, and HPCL face a critical procurement decision this week: sustained crude above **₹6,900** means the 15-day rolling average for the next petrol/diesel revision (due mid-July) will trend higher, forcing a retail price hike of **₹2–3/litre** unless government intervenes. For aviation, every **₹500/bbl rise** in crude inputs pushes ATF costs up by ~**₹1.5/litre**, eroding the hedge buffers that IndiGo and Air India built during softer crude months — carriers will begin reviewing route yields and capacity deployment if crude sustains above **₹7,100/bbl** (WTI **$76+**).

**BOTTOM LINE**

This is not a speculative spike or dollar-weakness artifact — the **2.8%** move on a **2.9%** WTI gain reveals OPEC's margin for error has shrunk, shifting the crude complex from cartel-managed to supply-constrained. That structural shift, not today's headline, matters for positioning.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

Watch **WTI close above $73.50/bbl** (Brent **$77.25+**) — the last time Brent-WTI spread widened beyond **$4.00**, backwardation lasted 18 trading sessions and crude rallied a further **3–4%** on physical strength. If tonight's US crude inventory print (EIA, 10:30 pm IST Wednesday) shows a draw of **>3 million barrels**, the backwardation signal will confirm; a build will invalidate and trigger a reversal to **₹6,767** support (day low).
