## Introduction

What drives MCX cardamom prices in India is a combination of domestic crop output from Kerala and Tamil Nadu, Guatemala's dominant global supply position, and shifting export demand from Gulf nations and China. When any one of these levers moves sharply, MCX futures — quoted in INR per kg — respond within days.

## The Mechanism

Cardamom's price transmission follows a clear chain. Guatemala produces roughly 70–75% of global cardamom supply; India produces 15–20%, concentrated in Kerala's Idukki district and Tamil Nadu's Nilgiris. When Guatemala faces drought, disease (like Katte virus outbreaks in past cycles), or logistics disruption, global benchmark prices at the Guatemalan exporters' association level spike first.

Indian domestic prices at the Spices Board of India e-auctions in Bodinayakanur and Kumily react within one to two weeks, as traders reprice physical lots. MCX cardamom futures — a 100 kg contract settled against these domestic auction prices — then reflect the revised physical market level.

The export demand channel works in parallel. India's cardamom exports go primarily to Saudi Arabia, UAE, and Kuwait. When Haj and Ramadan seasons approach, Gulf import demand rises sharply. Exporters bid aggressively at auctions, lifting physical prices, and MCX futures follow. The simplified pricing relationship: **MCX Price ≈ Bodinayakanur Auction Price + Carrying Cost (storage + finance) ± Basis**. When physical auction prices jump 10%, MCX spot-month contracts historically close that gap within three to five sessions.

## India-Specific Context

Several structural factors make MCX cardamom diverge from raw global signals. First, India imposes a basic customs duty of 70% on cardamom imports, which effectively insulates domestic prices from cheap Guatemalan material flooding the market — India is a net exporter, not importer, so this duty rarely fires directly, but it anchors a price floor psychology. Second, the INR/USD exchange rate matters because export realisations are in dollars; a weaker rupee increases export competitiveness, which tightens domestic supply and pushes MCX prices higher. Third, SEBI-mandated MCX circuit limits — typically ±3% per session for agri contracts — can delay price discovery during sharp moves, causing gap openings in subsequent sessions. Spices Board of India auction data, released weekly, serves as the primary settlement reference for MCX's cash-settled cardamom contracts.

## Historical Episodes

In 2019–2020, a combination of erratic Kerala monsoon and strong Gulf demand pushed MCX cardamom from approximately ₹1,400/kg to above ₹3,000/kg — a move of over 110% within roughly 12 months — marking one of the sharpest commodity rallies on Indian exchanges that year.

In 2021–2022, Guatemala recovered production aggressively after earlier weather disruptions. Simultaneously, Indian crop output in Idukki improved. MCX cardamom corrected approximately 45–50% from its peaks, settling near ₹1,500–1,700/kg levels through much of 2022.

In late 2023, erratic southwest monsoon distribution in Kerala caused uneven flowering, tightening small-cardamom supply ahead of the festive and export season, and prices recovered roughly 30–35% from their mid-year lows within two quarters.

## What to Watch

- **Spices Board of India weekly e-auction results** from Bodinayakanur and Kumily — price and volume both matter
- **IMD southwest monsoon onset and progress** for Kerala (June–September), especially Idukki district rainfall data
- **Guatemala crop reports** from AGEXPORT (Guatemalan exporters' association), released periodically
- **Indian spice export data** from Spices Board monthly trade statistics
- **INR/USD rate** — sharp rupee depreciation historically correlates with export-driven domestic tightening
- **MCX circuit limit triggers** — consecutive ±3% closes signal a repricing event is underway
