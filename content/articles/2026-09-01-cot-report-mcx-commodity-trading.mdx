## Introduction

Understanding how to read the COT report for MCX commodity trading in India gives traders a structured window into global speculative positioning before price moves propagate to domestic markets. The Commitments of Traders (COT) report, published weekly by the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), maps the aggregate positions of commercial hedgers, non-commercial speculators, and small traders across COMEX, NYMEX, and CBOT contracts.

## The Mechanism

The CFTC releases the COT report every Friday at 15:30 U.S. Eastern Time, covering positions as of the preceding Tuesday. The transmission to MCX prices follows a defined chain:

1. **Position shift identified:** Non-commercial (speculative) net-long or net-short positioning changes sharply on COMEX gold or NYMEX crude futures.
2. **Global benchmark reprices:** Spot gold on COMEX or WTI crude on NYMEX adjusts during U.S. and London trading sessions.
3. **INR conversion applied:** MCX prices derive from the global benchmark using the formula — **MCX Price (₹) = [COMEX Price (USD/troy oz) × 10 × Spot USD/INR] ÷ 31.1035** for gold (converting to grams and rupees).
4. **Carry and basis adjustment:** MCX futures prices add a cost-of-carry premium above the spot-derived value, reflecting Indian interest rates and contract tenure.
5. **Domestic price settles:** MCX opening the next morning gaps up or down, reflecting the overnight COMEX or NYMEX move already priced by algorithms and arbitrageurs.

When non-commercial net longs on COMEX gold breach historical extremes — typically above 250,000 contracts — it signals crowded positioning that historically precedes sharp reversals, which then compress MCX prices within 48–72 hours.

## India-Specific Context

Indian prices diverge from direct global parity for several structural reasons. Gold imports attract a basic customs duty of 15% plus an Agriculture Infrastructure Development Cess of 5%, making the landed cost substantially above COMEX-derived parity. GST at 3% layers further on gold jewellery transactions. For crude oil, the government levies excise duty and state VAT independently of global price changes, creating a partial buffer.

The rupee's daily fixing against the dollar — governed by RBI's managed float policy — introduces an additional variable. A weakening rupee amplifies MCX price rises even when COMEX is flat. MCX contracts themselves are cash-settled in INR with SEBI-mandated daily circuit limits of 4–6%, preventing runaway intraday moves. This means the full extent of an overnight COMEX repositioning may take two trading sessions to fully reflect on MCX.

## Historical Episodes

**2020 — COVID safe-haven surge:** Non-commercial net longs on COMEX gold reached record levels above 300,000 contracts through mid-2020. MCX gold rose from approximately ₹41,000 per 10 grams in March 2020 to a peak near ₹56,200 in August 2020 — a move of roughly 37% in five months.

**2022 — Crude positioning reversal:** Non-commercial longs on NYMEX crude collapsed sharply in Q3 2022 as recession fears mounted. MCX crude fell from approximately ₹9,000 per barrel in June 2022 to near ₹6,200 by November 2022, a compression of roughly 31%.

**2023 — Silver short squeeze signal:** A sharp drop in non-commercial net shorts on COMEX silver in early 2023 preceded MCX silver rallying from approximately ₹58,000 to ₹73,000 per kg within six weeks — a move of around 26%.

## What to Watch

- **CFTC COT release:** Every Friday, 15:30 U.S. ET — check non-commercial net position change week-on-week
- **COMEX/NYMEX open interest:** Spikes above 20% weekly change signal crowding
- **USD/INR spot rate:** RBI reference rate published at 13:30 IST daily
- **RBI MPC meeting dates:** Six scheduled annually — rate decisions alter carry cost embedded in MCX futures
- **MCX circuit filter trigger:** 4% move intraday signals institutional stop-loss cascades
- **China PMI release:** First business day of each month — major demand signal for base metals on MCX
