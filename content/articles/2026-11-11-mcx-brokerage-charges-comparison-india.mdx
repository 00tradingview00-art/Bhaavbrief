## Introduction

An MCX brokerage charges comparison between discount and full-service brokers in India reveals a cost gap that can meaningfully erode margins on high-frequency commodity trades. Understanding exactly how each model prices its service — and what that means per lot on gold, crude oil, or copper — helps traders make structurally informed decisions.

## The Mechanism

Brokerage on MCX trades is charged as either a flat fee per lot or a percentage of turnover, depending on the broker model.

**Discount brokers** typically charge a flat fee — commonly ₹20 per executed order, regardless of contract size. On a gold mini lot (100 grams, notional value roughly ₹6,00,000 at current prices), this translates to an effective rate of approximately 0.003% per side.

**Full-service brokers** typically charge 0.03% to 0.05% of turnover per side. On the same gold mini lot, that is ₹180–₹300 per executed order — 9x to 15x higher than the flat-fee alternative.

The total round-trip cost calculation runs:

> **Total brokerage cost = (Entry brokerage + Exit brokerage) + STT + Exchange transaction charges + GST on brokerage + SEBI turnover fee + Stamp duty**

STT on MCX non-agri futures is 0.01% on the sell side. Exchange transaction charges on MCX run approximately 0.0026% of turnover. GST applies at 18% on the brokerage component specifically — not on the full trade value. Stamp duty is levied by the state at 0.002% on the buy side.

For active traders placing 10–20 lots daily, the brokerage differential between models compounds significantly across a month.

## India-Specific Context

SEBI regulates broker fee structures, but does not cap brokerage rates — brokers set their own schedules within exchange norms. MCX contracts are INR-denominated, so global price moves must pass through the USD/INR conversion before impacting contract P&L, and brokerage is calculated on the INR turnover value, not the dollar equivalent.

GST at 18% on brokerage is a fixed regulatory layer that applies equally to both broker types — but because the base brokerage is lower for discount brokers, the absolute GST outgo is also lower. Full-service brokers may bundle research, relationship managers, and margin funding access into their fee, which some jewellers and hedgers find operationally relevant. SEBI's 2018 circular requiring segregation of advisory and execution services further formalized the distinction between these two models.

## Historical Episodes

In **2020**, when crude oil MCX prices turned negative in the global market and MCX had to settle contracts administratively, traders discovered that brokerage costs were trivial relative to settlement losses — but those with higher per-trade costs had their margin buffers eroded faster during the volatile period.

In **2019**, MCX gold volumes surged approximately 35% year-on-year as the INR weakened sharply. Discount broker platforms reported disproportionate growth in retail MCX accounts, partly attributed to the visible cost differential becoming a deciding factor for price-sensitive traders entering the commodity space.

In **2022**, LME nickel's historic short squeeze caused MCX nickel to hit upper circuits across multiple sessions. Traders placed large volumes of orders in compressed timeframes; under flat-fee structures, their brokerage cost per lot remained fixed while full-service traders saw proportionally higher costs against surging notional values.

## What to Watch

Monitor these specific triggers to anticipate cost-relevant volume spikes on MCX:

- **MCX margin revision circulars** — issued before high-volatility events, they affect effective capital required per lot
- **SEBI turnover fee revision notifications** — periodically adjusted, directly impact total transaction costs
- **RBI MPC meeting dates** — INR movement post-announcement shifts notional contract values and therefore full-service brokerage outgo
- **MCX daily circuit limit breaches** — signals extreme sessions where order frequency, and therefore total brokerage load, rises sharply
- **Exchange transaction charge revision notices on MCX website** — historically revised during annual periods

A structured MCX brokerage charges comparison across discount and full-service models in India should include all statutory levies alongside headline brokerage to reflect true round-trip cost accurately.
