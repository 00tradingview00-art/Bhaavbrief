## Introduction

What is max pain theory MCX options India traders keep referencing near expiry? It is the strike price at which the total monetary loss for all open option buyers — across both calls and puts — is mathematically maximised, and where price has historically shown a tendency to gravitate as expiry approaches.

## The Mechanism

Max pain is calculated by summing the total payout MCX would owe option buyers if the underlying settled at each available strike. The strike where that aggregate payout is lowest — meaning option sellers collectively lose the least — is the max pain point.

The step-by-step pathway works as follows. As expiry nears, large option writers — typically institutions, proprietary desks, or commercial hedgers — hold concentrated short positions in calls and puts across multiple strikes. These participants have a structural interest in the underlying settling near the strike that minimises their payout obligation. They can, within position limits, hedge their delta exposure by transacting in the underlying futures contract. This delta-hedging activity — buying futures when price falls below max pain, reducing futures exposure when price rises above it — creates a gravitational pull toward the max pain strike. The effect intensifies in the final two to three sessions before expiry because gamma accelerates rapidly, making delta adjustments more frequent and larger in size. The pull is not guaranteed, but the mechanical pressure is real and observable in open interest concentration data.

## India-Specific Context

MCX options on gold, silver, crude oil, and base metals settle against the MCX futures price — not directly against COMEX, LME, or NYMEX. This creates a domestic max pain level that can sit at a meaningful premium or discount to global benchmarks once rupee conversion, import duty, and GST are layered in. For gold, the import duty structure (currently 6% basic customs duty plus applicable cess) and GST at 3% mean the MCX spot reference trades at a structurally elevated level versus dollar-denominated COMEX prices. A rupee depreciation spike near expiry can therefore shift the effective max pain strike in INR terms even if the dollar price of the commodity is flat. Additionally, SEBI-mandated position limits on MCX options constrain how aggressively any single entity can defend a strike, which moderates — but does not eliminate — the gravitational effect seen in less-regulated markets.

## Historical Episodes

In late 2022, MCX crude oil options approached expiry during a period of rupee volatility. The front-month contract spent the final three sessions oscillating within a narrow INR 200–300 band around the 6,500 strike, which aligned closely with the calculated max pain level, before settling approximately 1.2% away from it.

In 2020, during the COVID-related crude collapse, max pain theory broke down visibly — MCX crude settled far outside any rational max pain band as NYMEX WTI briefly turned negative, a reminder that extreme global dislocations override domestic strike-price mechanics.

In 2023, MCX gold options near a major expiry saw price compress toward the ₹59,000–₹59,500 zone in the final week, a range that corresponded to peak open interest concentration, before expanding sharply post-settlement.

## What to Watch

Monitor MCX options open interest chain data daily in the final week before expiry — the strike with the highest combined call and put open interest is a practical proxy for the max pain zone. Watch RBI MPC announcement dates, as an unexpected rate decision can reprice the rupee and shift INR-denominated max pain levels abruptly. SEBI's weekly F&O participant-wise position data reveals whether commercial hedgers are defending specific strikes. For crude oil specifically, EIA weekly inventory release timing relative to MCX expiry day is a key variable — a data surprise can overwhelm the gravitational pull entirely.
