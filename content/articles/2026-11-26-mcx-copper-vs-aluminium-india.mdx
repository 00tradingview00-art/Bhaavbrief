## Introduction

Traders tracking the mcx copper vs aluminium price comparison india often notice that copper commands a significantly higher per-kg price than aluminium — and that the gap between them widens and narrows in ways that are not random. Understanding this spread requires knowing how each metal is priced globally, how that price transmits to MCX, and why the two metals respond differently to the same macro event.

## The Mechanism

Copper's MCX price is anchored to COMEX copper through an import parity formula:

**MCX Copper (₹/kg) = COMEX price (¢/lb) ÷ 100 × 2.20462 × USD/INR × 1.05**

The 1.05 multiplier captures the approximate 5% basic customs duty on copper imports. When COMEX copper moves, the INR equivalent shifts instantly — and MCX futures converge to that import parity level through arbitrage. If MCX trades significantly below import parity, domestic traders find it cheaper to buy MCX than import physical copper, compressing the gap.

Aluminium follows a parallel LME-anchored pathway, but its price drivers are structurally different. Aluminium is energy-intensive to smelt — roughly 14,000 kWh per tonne — so natural gas and electricity prices in China, the Gulf, and Europe directly affect production costs. When Chinese smelters curtail output during power shortages, aluminium tightens faster than copper. Copper, by contrast, is mining-supply driven: strikes in Chilean and Peruvian mines, ore grade decline, and long project lead times dominate the supply side. This means the two metals can diverge sharply even when the USD/INR rate is unchanged, because their upstream supply chains respond to entirely different stress points.

The spread between copper and aluminium on MCX therefore reflects the weighted difference in their global supply-demand balances, filtered through the same rupee conversion and duty structure.

## India-Specific Context

India imports a significant share of its refined copper, making import parity the binding constraint on MCX copper prices. The 5% basic customs duty, plus 18% GST on the transaction value, means Indian traders pay a structural premium over LME spot. For aluminium, India is both a producer and importer — Hindalco and Vedanta's domestic output creates a partial buffer — so the LME linkage is slightly looser. MCX copper contracts are sized at 2,500 kg, giving a contract value of roughly ₹24 lakh at current price levels, making it a high-capital instrument. MCX aluminium lots are smaller, lowering the capital barrier. SEBI's position limits and daily circuit filters (typically ±4% on base metals) can temporarily decouple MCX prices from overnight LME moves, particularly around Indian market holidays when arbitrage desks are absent.

## Historical Episodes

In 2021-22, copper on MCX rallied from approximately ₹580/kg to nearly ₹870/kg — a move of roughly 50% — driven by post-pandemic demand recovery, USD weakness, and China's infrastructure stimulus cycle. Aluminium moved sharply as well but lagged copper's percentage gain.

In mid-2022, as the US Federal Reserve aggressively hiked rates, the dollar strengthened materially. Despite this, LME copper corrected nearly 30% from its peak within months, pulling MCX copper down in tandem.

In 2023, power rationing across Chinese provinces caused aluminium smelter curtailments, narrowing the copper-aluminium spread on MCX as aluminium outperformed on a relative basis during that specific window.
