## Introduction

The question of mcx gold mini vs guinea vs petal which to trade comes down to three variables: lot size, margin requirement, and how precisely the contract matches your hedging or speculative exposure. Each contract tracks the same underlying price mechanism but serves a structurally different trader profile.

## The Mechanism

All three MCX gold contracts derive their INR price from the same global benchmark through an import parity calculation:

**MCX Gold Price (₹/10g) = (COMEX $/troy oz ÷ 31.1035) × 10 × USD/INR × 1.12**

The 1.12 multiplier captures 6% Basic Customs Duty, 3% AIDC, and 3% GST as revised post the July 2024 Union Budget. The transmission chain works as follows: COMEX moves on US real yield shifts (10-year TIPS), central bank demand data from WGC, and geopolitical stress events. That COMEX price converts to INR at the live USD/INR spot rate, then the import duty layer is applied. A weakening rupee amplifies MCX price even when COMEX is flat — a ₹1 move in USD/INR shifts MCX gold by roughly ₹300–350 per 10g. Each 1% change in import duty independently shifts MCX prices by approximately ₹1,000 per 10g. The three contracts — Gold Mini (100g), Guinea (8g), and Petal (1g) — all reference this same derived price; they differ only in lot size and therefore in absolute margin outlay and P&L per tick.

## India-Specific Context

India's MCX gold pricing carries structural layers absent in COMEX. The import duty regime — currently BCD at 6% plus 3% AIDC plus GST — means Indian prices can diverge sharply from COMEX moves if the government adjusts duty, as it did in July 2024 when the BCD was cut from 15% to 6%, compressing the MCX premium overnight. SEBI regulates contract specifications, position limits, and circuit filters on MCX; a 4% daily circuit limit applies to gold contracts. The RBI's management of USD/INR through intervention affects how much of a COMEX rally actually transmits to MCX. Jewellers and importers in India face a basis risk: the MCX-COMEX spread can widen unpredictably during duty changes or sharp currency moves, making the smaller Guinea and Petal contracts useful for granular hedge sizing against physical inventory in odd quantities.

## Historical Episodes

In 2020, when COVID-19 drove COMEX gold to record levels above $2,000/troy oz and the rupee simultaneously weakened past ₹75, MCX Gold Mini delivered approximately 40% returns within the calendar year — the rupee amplification effect was clearly visible. In July 2024, the Union Budget cut BCD on gold from 15% to 6%; MCX gold fell roughly 6–7% intraday — nearly ₹4,500–5,000 per 10g — before partially recovering as COMEX held firm, illustrating how duty changes create contract-specific dislocations irrelevant to COMEX holders. In 2022–23, sustained US Federal Reserve rate hikes pushed 10-year TIPS real yields above 1.5%, suppressing COMEX gold; yet a weakening rupee kept MCX prices relatively supported, demonstrating that global and domestic drivers can pull in opposite directions simultaneously.
