## Introduction

The gold mining stocks vs MCX gold futures correlation India traders observe is real but imperfect — mining equities carry operational leverage that pure futures contracts do not. Understanding where the two diverge is as important as knowing where they converge.

## The Mechanism

Gold mining stocks and MCX Gold futures share a common anchor: the COMEX spot price in USD per troy ounce. From there, the transmission paths split.

For MCX futures, the import parity formula is the binding link:

**MCX Gold (₹/10g) = (COMEX $/troy oz ÷ 31.1035) × 10 × USD/INR × 1.12**

The 1.12 multiplier reflects the current duty structure — 6% Basic Customs Duty, 3% AIDC, and 3% GST, as revised in the July 2024 Union Budget. When COMEX moves 1%, MCX moves approximately 1% in rupee terms, adjusted for the prevailing USD/INR rate. A weakening rupee amplifies this — COMEX can stay flat while MCX rises purely because the dollar has strengthened against the INR.

Gold mining stocks — primarily global names like Barrick or Newmont, or Indian proxies traded on NSE — respond to COMEX through a different filter. Mining companies carry fixed operating costs (labour, energy, equipment). When gold prices rise, revenue expands while costs stay relatively stable, compressing margins in reverse when prices fall. This "operational leverage" means a 10% rise in COMEX gold has historically produced 20–30% moves in mid-tier miner equities. MCX futures, by contrast, mirror COMEX almost linearly once rupee and duty factors are applied.

## India-Specific Context

India has no large listed gold mining company, so the gold mining stocks vs MCX gold futures correlation India traders encounter is largely cross-market — comparing NSE-listed global mining ETFs or ADR proxies against domestic MCX contracts.

MCX Gold trades in lot sizes of 1 kg (100 units of ₹ per 10g), and prices embed the full import duty stack. When the government changes duty — as it did dramatically in the July 2024 budget, cutting the combined rate — each 1% duty shift moves MCX price by approximately ₹1,000 per 10g, a move entirely disconnected from COMEX or any mining company's earnings. SEBI circuit limits on MCX (typically ±6% daily) can also cause temporary dislocations. Mining stocks traded on foreign exchanges face no such circuit mechanism, meaning they can gap more freely on overnight COMEX moves.

## Historical Episodes

In 2020, when COMEX gold surged roughly 25% as pandemic uncertainty peaked, mid-tier global mining stocks rose 40–60% over the same period — illustrating operational leverage clearly against MCX's near-linear 28% rupee-adjusted gain.

In 2022, when US 10-year real yields spiked sharply and COMEX fell approximately 15% from its March highs, gold miners corrected 25–35%, amplifying the underlying metal's move, while MCX losses were partially cushioned by a weakening rupee holding the INR price decline to around 8–10%.

In mid-2024, following the Union Budget duty cut, MCX Gold fell nearly ₹4,000–5,000 per 10g within days despite COMEX remaining stable — a purely India-specific shock that mining stocks globally did not reflect at all.

## What to Watch

Monitor these specific data releases when tracking this relationship:

- **US 10Y TIPS yield** (daily, Bloomberg/Fed): drives COMEX direction, feeds MCX via the formula
- **USD/INR fixing** (RBI reference rate, 12:30 PM IST daily): amplifies or dampens MCX moves independently of COMEX
- **WGC quarterly central bank demand report**: signals structural gold demand shifts
- **Union Budget and customs duty notifications**: the single biggest India-only MCX shock trigger
- **MCX daily circuit limit alerts**: signal when basis risk between COMEX and MCX is temporarily breaking down
