## Introduction

Castor seed oil price drivers in India Gujarat MCX markets converge around a single crop grown almost exclusively in Saurashtra, making price formation unusually concentrated and sensitive to local shocks. Gujarat accounts for roughly 85–90% of India's castor seed output, so a single agro-climatic variable — rainfall distribution across Rajkot, Junagadh, and Amreli districts — can move MCX futures by double digits within weeks.

## The Mechanism

The transmission from field to futures follows a clear chain. During kharif sowing (June–August), IMD rainfall data for Saurashtra determines acreage. If sowing is delayed or below-normal rain cuts planted area, traders revise the projected crop size downward.

The step-by-step pathway runs as follows:

1. **Mandi arrivals** at Gondal and Keshod mandis post-harvest (November–February) signal actual supply against estimates.
2. **Castor oil extraction**: approximately 46–48 kg of oil is derived from 100 kg of seed. When seed prices rise, oil prices follow with a fixed extraction-ratio lag — *Castor oil price ≈ (Seed price × 0.47) ÷ recovery rate + processing margin (₹4–6/kg)*.
3. **Export demand** drives the next link. India supplies roughly 70–75% of global castor oil exports. When European oleo-chemical manufacturers or Chinese lubricant buyers place large orders, domestic oil prices are pulled upward.
4. **MCX futures** (Castor Seed — 100 kg lot; Castor Oil — 1,000 kg lot) reprice in real time as spot mandis update, compressing or widening the basis between Rajkot spot and MCX near-month contract.

The result is that an export inquiry in Rotterdam or Shanghai can transmit to Rajkot mandis within 48–72 hours and appear in MCX settlement prices the same session.

## India-Specific Context

Several structural factors make castor seed oil price drivers in India Gujarat MCX markets behave differently from any global oleo-chemical benchmark. India imposes a basic customs duty of 100% on castor seed imports, effectively insulating domestic seed prices from international arbitrage — there is no meaningful import parity ceiling on seed. Castor oil exports, however, attract zero duty, keeping the oil price tightly coupled to global demand. GST at 5% on seed and 12% on refined castor oil creates a tax wedge that widens processing margins on paper but compresses refiner flexibility in practice. The rupee-dollar exchange rate is a direct multiplier: a 1% depreciation in INR/USD mechanically lifts the INR-equivalent export realisation, supporting domestic oil prices. MCX castor seed contracts carry a ±4% daily circuit limit, which can suspend price discovery during sharp supply shocks.

## Historical Episodes

Three episodes illustrate the magnitude of price moves this mechanism can produce.

In **2018–19**, back-to-back below-normal monsoons in Saurashtra cut Gujarat's castor crop by nearly 30%. MCX castor seed futures rose from approximately ₹3,800/quintal to above ₹5,200/quintal — a move of roughly 37% over eight months.

In **2021**, a surge in Chinese demand for castor oil derivatives coincided with a tight domestic crop. MCX castor oil futures crossed ₹1,400/10 kg from a base near ₹1,050/10 kg, representing a 33% rally inside six months.

In **2023**, excess rainfall in key Saurashtra districts during pod-filling triggered yield losses. Arrivals at Gondal mandi fell sharply; MCX near-month seed contracts gained approximately 18–20% between October and December.

## What to Watch

- **IMD weekly rainfall bulletins** for Rajkot, Junagadh, and Amreli districts (June–September)
- **Gondal and Keshod mandi daily arrival data** (November–March)
- **Solvent Extractors' Association (SEA) monthly export figures**, released around the 15th of each month
- **China PMI manufacturing data**, released on the last day of each month — signals downstream lubricant demand
- **INR/USD daily RBI reference rate** — a weakening rupee historically supports castor oil export realisations
- **MCX circuit limit triggers** — a ±4% move halts trading and signals an acute supply or demand shock
