## Introduction

The digital gold vs MCX gold futures India comparison is one of the most practically important decisions facing Indian commodity traders and jewellers today. Both instruments track the same underlying metal, yet their cost structures, liquidity profiles, purity standards, and regulatory frameworks differ enough to produce meaningfully different outcomes across market conditions.

## The Mechanism

MCX Gold futures price is anchored to an import parity formula:

**MCX Gold (₹/10g) = (COMEX $/troy oz ÷ 31.1035) × 10 × USD/INR × 1.12**

The 1.12 multiplier reflects the current duty structure: 6% Basic Customs Duty (BCD) + 3% Agriculture Infrastructure Development Cess (AIDC) + 3% GST, as revised in the July 2024 Union Budget. This means every MCX tick is a function of three live variables — COMEX spot, the USD/INR exchange rate, and Indian import duty policy.

Digital gold, offered by platforms like MMTC-PAMP, SafeGold, and Augmont, is priced by the provider against live spot gold but carries its own cost layer: a making/platform spread (typically 0.5–1% above spot on purchase, 1–2% below on redemption) plus applicable GST. There is no futures curve, no margin requirement, and no lot-size constraint. Purity on accredited digital gold platforms is 24K (999.9 fineness), with physical delivery possible above certain thresholds.

MCX futures require a 1 kg lot (100 units of 10g), margin currently around 4–5% of contract value, and carry basis risk — the gap between MCX price and COMEX-derived import parity can widen when duty changes or USD/INR moves sharply within a session.

## India-Specific Context

India's gold price does not simply mirror COMEX because the import duty wedge is large and policy-sensitive. Each 1% change in BCD shifts MCX Gold by approximately ₹1,000 per 10g, creating an abrupt repricing event disconnected from global benchmarks. The July 2024 duty cut from 15% to 9% (combined BCD + AIDC) compressed this wedge sharply, compressing the MCX-COMEX spread within hours.

For digital gold, GST at 3% applies on every purchase — identical to physical gold — making it less tax-efficient than Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) for long holding periods. MCX futures, being exchange-traded derivatives regulated by SEBI and cleared through ICCL, carry counterparty protection that digital gold platforms — operating without a SEBI or RBI mandate on solvency guarantees — currently do not provide. SEBI's commodity derivative circuit limits (typically ±6% daily on MCX Gold) also cap intraday volatility in a way that digital gold platforms do not replicate.

## Historical Episodes

In 2020, when the US Federal Reserve cut rates to near-zero and COMEX Gold surged to then-record $2,075/troy oz, MCX Gold crossed ₹56,000/10g for the first time — a move of roughly 40% from January levels — amplified by simultaneous rupee depreciation past USD/INR 75.

In 2022, when the Indian government raised import duty from 10.75% to 15% (July 2022), MCX Gold jumped approximately ₹3,000–₹4,000/10g within two sessions, while COMEX moved only marginally — a pure domestic duty transmission.

In 2024, the reverse occurred: the Union Budget's duty reduction to an effective 9% combined rate caused MCX Gold to drop roughly ₹4,000–₹5,000/10g intraday on July 23, while COMEX remained broadly stable, illustrating the asymmetric basis risk that MCX traders carry versus digital gold holders.
