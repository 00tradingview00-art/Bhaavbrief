## Introduction

Understanding how to hedge with MCX commodity futures India is essential for any business carrying commodity price exposure across gold, silver, crude oil, or base metals. MCX futures allow Indian merchants to lock in prices for future delivery, offsetting losses in the physical market with corresponding gains in the futures position.

## The Mechanism

A hedge works by taking an equal and opposite position in the futures market relative to your physical exposure. A jeweller holding 10 kg of gold inventory carries the risk that gold prices fall before the stock is sold. By entering an equivalent short position on MCX Gold futures, any price decline that erodes inventory value is offset by a gain on the futures leg.

The price relationship follows import parity pricing:

**MCX Price (INR/10g) = COMEX Price (USD/troy oz) × (INR/USD rate) ÷ 31.1035 × (1 + Import Duty) × (1 + GST) + handling/freight**

When global gold trades at USD 2,000/troy oz and USD/INR is 83, the base MCX equivalent is approximately ₹53,400/10g before duties. A hedger monitors this relationship continuously. If the futures price deviates significantly from import parity — called basis risk — the hedge is imperfect. The basis (spot price minus futures price) narrows toward expiry, which is when most hedges are rolled or closed. Rolling means closing the near-month contract and opening the next contract before expiry to maintain continuous coverage.

## India-Specific Context

Indian commodity prices carry several layers that global benchmarks do not. Gold imports attract a basic customs duty of 15% plus Agriculture Infrastructure Development Cess of 5%, substantially widening the gap between COMEX prices and MCX delivery prices. GST at 3% applies to physical gold transactions. Crude oil on MCX references Brent but is priced in INR, making the rupee the single most important conversion variable — a 1% rupee depreciation directly inflates MCX crude prices by approximately 1%.

MCX contracts carry SEBI-mandated position limits and circuit filters (typically ±4–6% daily) that can prevent a hedge from being executed at distressed prices. RBI's intervention in the forex market indirectly affects MCX prices by managing rupee volatility. Businesses learning how to hedge with MCX commodity futures India must account for these layers when sizing positions.

## Historical Episodes

In 2020, when crude oil crashed internationally following demand destruction during COVID lockdowns, MCX Crude Oil futures fell from approximately ₹4,000/barrel to below ₹1,000/barrel — a drop exceeding 70% — within weeks. Refiners with short futures positions offset significant physical inventory losses.

In 2022, when the Russia-Ukraine conflict triggered a sharp rally in global commodity prices, MCX Gold moved from approximately ₹47,000/10g to above ₹55,000/10g within three months — a move of roughly 17%. Jewellers who had hedged forward orders avoided cost overruns.

In 2013, when the rupee fell sharply from around 54 to 68 against the dollar, MCX Gold rose nearly 30% in INR terms even as COMEX prices were declining — illustrating currency amplification unique to India.

## What to Watch

- **COMEX/LME settlement prices** daily, especially during US and London trading hours
- **RBI MPC policy dates** (six times annually) for rupee direction signals
- **USD/INR spot rate** on RBI reference page — a move beyond ₹0.50 intraday is material
- **Union Budget customs duty announcements** for gold and silver import levies
- **EIA weekly inventory data** (every Wednesday, 8:30 PM IST) for crude oil
- **MCX circuit filter triggers** — when hit, reassess hedge execution timing before next session opens
