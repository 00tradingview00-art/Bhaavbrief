## Introduction

MCX commodity tax treatment income tax India 2026 rules classify futures profits as non-speculative business income, making them taxable at slab rates with CTT (Commodity Transaction Tax) deductible as a business expense. Understanding exactly how CTT, capital gains provisions, and ITR filing obligations interact determines your net post-tax return on every MCX trade.

## The Mechanism

MCX commodity futures taxation flows through a specific legal and regulatory chain. Here is the step-by-step transmission:

**Step 1 — Transaction classification.** The Income Tax Act, Section 43(5), treats MCX futures as non-speculative business transactions (unlike equity intraday, which is speculative). This single classification governs everything downstream.

**Step 2 — CTT levy.** Every sell-side MCX futures transaction attracts CTT at 0.01% of trade value for non-agricultural contracts (gold, silver, crude oil, base metals). Agricultural contracts remain CTT-exempt. The formula is: **CTT payable = Trade value × 0.0001**.

**Step 3 — Income computation.** Net profit (realised P&L across the financial year) from MCX futures is added to your total business income. Losses can be carried forward for four assessment years, but only set off against non-speculative business income — not salary, not capital gains.

**Step 4 — Tax rate application.** The applicable slab rate under the new tax regime (FY 2025-26 onwards) applies — up to 30% plus surcharge and cess for high-income traders. CTT paid during the year is deductible as a business expense under Section 37.

**Step 5 — ITR filing.** MCX traders must file ITR-3 (business income schedule), with audit mandatory if turnover exceeds ₹10 crore (or ₹2 crore under presumptive taxation thresholds).

## India-Specific Context

Several structural factors make MCX commodity taxation distinct from global commodity trading environments.

First, the rupee conversion mechanism means your MCX gold or crude oil contract P&L is already denominated in INR — there is no separate forex gain/loss computation, unlike direct COMEX or NYMEX positions held by Indian residents abroad (which attract separate FEMA and income tax scrutiny).

Second, MCX contracts are cash-settled (except for select contracts under SEBI's delivery-based framework), so physical delivery tax implications — GST at 3% on gold delivery, for instance — rarely apply to futures traders but are critical for jewellers using MCX delivery contracts.

Third, SEBI-mandated daily MTM (mark-to-market) settlement means unrealised gains within a settlement cycle are cash-settled daily, complicating annual turnover calculations. ICAI guidance defines futures turnover as the absolute sum of all settlement differences, not gross trade value.

## Historical Episodes

Three episodes illustrate how tax-related policy shifts created measurable MCX price behaviour.

In 2013, when CTT was first introduced on non-agricultural MCX contracts (Budget announcement by Finance Minister P. Chidambaram), MCX crude oil and metal volumes dropped approximately 40-50% within three months as traders shifted strategies — spreads widened as liquidity thinned.

In 2018-19, the GST Council's clarification that options premiums on commodity derivatives attract 18% GST caused MCX options open interest to collapse by roughly 60% in the months immediately following the circular.

In 2023-24, amendments to the new tax regime reducing surcharge on incomes above ₹5 crore effectively lowered the peak marginal rate from 42.74% to 39%, improving net post-tax returns for high-frequency MCX traders — base metal daily volumes historically showed a visible uptick in that assessment year.
