## The catalyst

The dominant catalyst for the coming week is not a central bank meeting or a supply disruption. It is calendar-driven and India-specific: **Dhanteras falls on 6 November 2026 and Diwali on 8 November 2026** — the single most concentrated retail gold-and-silver demand window in the Indian year, the day jewellers see their highest single-day footfall.

What makes this year's window a genuine catalyst rather than a seasonal footnote is *what it is colliding with*. In the latest snapshot (31 Oct, 03:39 IST), **COMEX Gold fell 3.63% to $3,884.9/oz and COMEX Silver fell 5.86% to $49.14/oz** overnight — a disorderly repricing of the offshore benchmark. Meanwhile **MCX Gold barely moved, at ₹138,419/10g (-0.05%), and MCX Silver at ₹178,900/kg (-0.13%)**.

That gap is the story. The derived import-parity figures put fair-value MCX Gold at **₹118,994/10g (a 16.32% spread)** and MCX Silver at **₹150,516/kg (an 18.86% spread)**. Those wedges are not only large, they are *inconsistent with each other*: the snapshot carries an explicit **PARITY_WEDGE_DIVERGENCE warning** flagging a 2.54-point gap against a 2-point tolerance, with the note that one leg of one feed — MCX or COMEX side — is likely stale or wrong.

So the week's central question is mechanical, not directional: **does the domestic price converge toward offshore, does offshore bounce toward domestic, or does festival physical demand legitimately hold the premium wide?** Dhanteras week is precisely when that question gets answered with real order flow rather than screen prices.

## What changed this week

The precious complex spent the week losing its bid. Coverage dated 29 October flagged **MCX Silver down 2.77%** on dollar strength, then a 30 October note recorded a **5.13% silver drawdown** attributed to industrial-cycle fears touching solar and semiconductor demand — the gold-silver ratio now sits at **79.1**. The 28 October Fed items — a cut to 2027 guidance and an inflation signal — were explicitly framed as repricing gold, silver, crude and copper along the rate path.

Energy ran the other way. MCX Crude closed **+2.14% at ₹9,917/bbl**, the fourth consecutive strong session after +3.04% (28 Oct) and +2.34% moves, with headlines citing Strait of Hormuz tanker fires, Iran strike fears and an EU pipeline veto after the Dutch PM rejected a Russian gas return. Note the internal tension: **WTI rose 0.93% to $102.95 but Brent fell 2.22% to $112.33** — the risk premium is not uniformly priced across benchmarks.

Natural gas unwound its spike: after a reported **+17% move to ₹293/mmBtu** on 27 October, MCX Nat Gas is back to **₹276.7 (-1.81%)** with **Henry Hub at $2.88 (-2.34%)** and a storage-surplus signal cited. Base metals softened broadly — **copper ₹1,373.65 (-1.49%), nickel ₹1,449.2 (-2.50%), zinc ₹405.8 (-0.89%)**, aluminium flat. **USD/INR firmed to 95.27 (-0.25%)**, removing the rupee tailwind that had been cushioning MCX from offshore weakness earlier in the week.

## MCX transmission

The chain is standard: MCX ₹ price ≈ COMEX $ × USD/INR × unit conversion × (1 + import duty and levies) + domestic premium/discount. Three legs matter this week.

**Leg one — offshore.** A 3.6% gold and 5.9% silver fall offshore is the largest single input shock in the snapshot. If genuine and sustained, it argues for downward pressure on the rupee leg unless something offsets it.

**Leg two — currency.** The rupee *strengthened*. A falling USD/INR amplifies, rather than offsets, an offshore decline when translating to ₹/10g. One week's commentary already noted rupee strength "reversing commodity rally momentum."

**Leg three — domestic premium.** This is where Dhanteras enters. Festival physical demand typically shows up as compression of dealer discounts and firmer local premia. That can keep MCX sticky versus a falling benchmark — which is one plausible, non-error explanation for part of the 16-19% wedge. The other explanation is a stale feed. The GST capital-goods ITC refund item flagged 29 October is a further, smaller cost-side variable on import economics.

Honest framing: **we cannot resolve which explanation dominates from the snapshot alone**, and the system itself says so. Treat both the gold and silver parity numbers as provisional until the spreads re-converge to within tolerance.

## What would change the view

The thesis weakens if the parity divergence warning resolves as a pure data artefact and recomputed spreads return to normal duty-consistent levels — in that case the "catch-down" branch largely disappears. It also weakens if Brent and WTI re-converge upward together, confirming a broad supply shock that re-establishes a safe-haven bid strong enough to override the Fed-driven repricing. Conversely, a soft Caixin print on 2 November would strengthen the industrial-drag case for silver specifically, independent of anything happening in the festival channel. No options positioning data is supplied, so none is assumed here.
