## The catalyst

The single most consequential number in this week's snapshot is not a price — it is a spread. MCX gold at ₹136,750/10g sits 16.02% above its calculated import parity of ₹117,863, a gross wedge of ₹18,887 per 10 grams. MCX silver at ₹176,543/kg sits 18.51% above its parity of ₹148,970, a wedge of ₹27,573 per kilo. The snapshot engine has flagged this pair as a **PARITY_WEDGE_DIVERGENCE**: the 2.49-point gap between the two spreads exceeds the 2-point internal tolerance, meaning one of the four legs (MCX gold, MCX silver, COMEX gold, COMEX silver) is likely stale or mispriced.

Work the arithmetic and the divergence localises cleanly. COMEX gold at $3,886.3/oz against COMEX silver at $49.12/oz gives a gold-silver ratio of 79.1 — exactly the derived figure. But the MCX cross, converting ₹136,750/10g to a per-gram basis against ₹176.54/g of silver, implies roughly 77.5. MCX silver is therefore priced about 2.1% rich to gold relative to the international cross — and 1.0851 ÷ 1.1602 reproduces precisely that gap. The divergence is not noise spread across four instruments; it is concentrated in the gold-versus-silver relationship on one side of the arbitrage.

Why it matters for the coming week: a 16–18.5% wedge is far wider than the duty-and-GST stack alone typically explains, and it means domestic precious-metals prices are being set by the landed-cost chain — rupee, duty, local premium — more than by overnight COMEX direction. When the wedge is the dominant term, COMEX moves transmit incompletely, and cross-metal hedges built on an assumed stable ratio carry unrecognised basis risk.

## What changed this week

The rupee did the work. USDINR closed the snapshot at 94.33 from 94.12, a 0.22% depreciation. COMEX gold was effectively unchanged at +0.03%, yet MCX gold rose 0.39% — nearly the entire domestic gain is currency and wedge, not bullion. Silver moved the other way: COMEX silver fell 1.70% to $49.12 while MCX silver fell only 1.22%, the rupee again cushioning roughly half a point of the decline. EURINR (-0.09%), GBPINR (-0.21%) and JPYINR (flat) show the move was dollar-specific, not broad rupee weakness.

Elsewhere, energy firmed and industrials faded. MCX crude rose 0.87% to ₹8,372/bbl even as WTI slipped 0.20% to $88.56 and Brent was flat at $95.84 — the same rupee amplification visible in gold. Natural gas fell 1.66% to ₹307.9/mmBtu against a sharper 3.29% Henry Hub drop to $3.23, after a week that saw a 6.83% withdrawal-season spike unwind into inventory-glut coverage. Base metals were uniformly soft: nickel -2.18%, copper -1.48%, zinc -0.98%, aluminium -0.56%, lead the lone gainer at +0.26%. Electricity eased 1.08%.

## MCX transmission

The mechanism to watch is asymmetric pass-through. With the wedge this wide, a given percentage move on COMEX does not arrive at MCX one-for-one; it arrives scaled by the parity ratio and then adjusted by the rupee. That is why crude and gold both outperformed their dollar benchmarks this week while natural gas and silver underperformed theirs less than the dollar leg suggested.

The second-order effect is on the gold-silver ratio itself. If the 2.1% local richness in silver is genuine — a physical-premium or local-tightness story — it should persist and decay slowly. If it is a stale feed, it should snap back inside one or two sessions once timestamps re-sync. Traders cannot distinguish the two from price alone; the practical consequence is that any ratio-based construction between MCX gold and MCX silver currently embeds a basis that may be real or artefactual. The snapshot itself was generated at 15:23 IST, mid-session for MCX and pre-US for COMEX, which makes timestamp mismatch a live candidate explanation.
