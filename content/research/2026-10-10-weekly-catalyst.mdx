## The catalyst

MCX Gold is quoted in rupees per 10g, which means every print lands twice: once through the dollar price of the metal, and once through the exchange rate that translates it. Next week delivers both legs inside a 48-hour window. India's CPI releases Monday 12 October at 16:00 IST, India's WPI follows Wednesday 14 October at 12:00 IST, and US CPI hits the same Wednesday evening at 18:00 IST. For a contract that closed the week at ₹151,503 after a 1.27% session gain, that is the single most consequential cluster on the calendar — and it sits two weeks ahead of the 28 October FOMC decision, meaning both prints will be read primarily as inputs into rate-path expectations rather than as standalone data.

The sequencing matters. India CPI arrives first and speaks to the RBI reaction function, which this month held at 5.5% after a week of market chatter that a hike was coming. A softer domestic print reinforces the hold; a firmer one revives the tightening narrative that headlines flagged earlier in the week as a drag on physical gold and silver demand. US CPI arrives second and speaks to real yields and the dollar — the two channels that set COMEX Gold, which closed at $4,216.30 (+1.43%). When the domestic and the external legs point the same way, MCX moves can amplify well beyond the COMEX percentage move. When they conflict, the rupee absorbs part of the shock and MCX underperforms the international leg.

## What changed this week

The dollar-side bid did the heavy lifting. COMEX Silver gained 3.36% to $61.05 and COMEX Gold added 1.43%, with MCX Silver following at +2.07% to ₹225,820 and MCX Gold at +1.27%. The rupee was a mild headwind rather than a tailwind — USD/INR firmed to 96.95 (-0.19%) — so the domestic rally was imported, not manufactured by currency weakness. That is a meaningful distinction heading into a data week: a rally resting on the dollar leg is more exposed to a hot US CPI than one cushioned by rupee depreciation.

The gold-silver ratio sits at 69.1, with silver the stronger leg all week. Base metals participated — copper +1.41%, zinc +2.03%, aluminium +0.61%, nickel essentially flat at -0.01% — consistent with broad dollar softness rather than a metal-specific story.

Energy traded a two-sided narrative. MCX Crude closed at ₹8,890 (+0.29%) with Brent at $104.43 and WTI at $91.85, after a week in which an EIA inventory draw lifted prices, Bab el-Mandeb security improvements argued supply relief, and tanker-risk headlines argued the opposite. Natural gas was the standout mover at +2.58% to ₹313.70, tracking Henry Hub at $3.20. On the domestic policy side, the GST input-credit change was flagged as a potential easing of jeweller cost structures — relevant to physical demand, not to futures pricing in a single week.

One caveat that belongs in any parity-based reasoning this week: the snapshot's own diagnostics flag a 3.39-point gap between the gold import-parity spread (15.28%) and the silver spread (18.67%), exceeding the 2-point tolerance. That signals one price feed — MCX or COMEX side — may be stale. Treat absolute parity levels as unreliable until the wedge narrows; the directional read on the inflation cluster is unaffected.

## MCX transmission

The mechanism runs in two stages. Stage one: US CPI moves Fed expectations, which move real yields and the dollar index, which moves COMEX Gold and Silver. Stage two: that dollar move reprices USD/INR, which scales the COMEX move into rupees. Silver carries the higher beta through both stages — it moved 3.36% on COMEX against gold's 1.43% this week, and its industrial component adds a growth-expectations channel that gold lacks. Copper shares that second channel, which is why a growth-positive inflation surprise can split precious and base metals.

Crude's exposure is more indirect: US CPI shapes the demand narrative, but the dominant near-term driver remains the physical supply story and the contract roll on 19 October.

## What would change the view

A material widening of the parity wedge beyond its current 3.39-point gap would mean the domestic-versus-international comparison is unusable for the week, and the thesis would need to rest on COMEX and USD/INR alone. A shift in the crude supply narrative — renewed tanker-risk escalation, or confirmation that Bab el-Mandeb flows are normalising — would make energy, not inflation, the week's dominant story. And if the rupee breaks decisively from its current 96.95 area, the currency leg becomes the primary driver of MCX precious metals regardless of what CPI delivers.
