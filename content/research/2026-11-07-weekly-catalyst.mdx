## The catalyst

Diwali falls on **8 November 2026**, opening India's heaviest gold and silver gifting window — coins, jewellery, Dhanteras buying, and the wedding-season stocking that jewellers front-load against. It is the one catalyst in this week's inputs that is India-specific, calendar-certain, and physically transmitted rather than sentiment-driven.

What makes it the dominant catalyst for the coming week is *timing against price*. The festival window opens into a double discount: COMEX gold slipped to **$4,079.5/oz (-0.83%)** and the rupee strengthened to **₹94.15/USD (-0.40%)**. Both legs push the same direction for a rupee buyer. MCX Gold printed **₹142,364/10g (-1.20%)** and MCX Silver **₹184,977/kg (-1.16%)**.

Critically, this is not only a dollar story. The rupee firmed against the entire majors complex — **EUR/INR 105.625 (-0.82%)**, **GBP/INR 126.255 (-0.82%)**, **JPY/INR 60.45 (-0.25%)**. Broad-basket rupee strength lowers the landed-cost arithmetic of every imported commodity simultaneously, which is a different mechanism from a one-off dollar spike and tends to persist longer than a single session.

## What changed this week

The week carried two conflicting impulses, and precious metals ended on the weaker side of both.

**Geopolitics pushed energy up.** Feed items flagged Persian Gulf and Strait tensions, Ukraine refinery strikes, and Zelensky's statement that Ukraine determines its own responses to Russian attacks on energy infrastructure. MCX Crude surged **2.46%** mid-week on supply tightness. Yet the snapshot shows crude ending softer at **₹8,479/bbl (-0.95%)**, with **WTI $90.34** and **Brent $97.76**. Earlier in the week the same feed recorded heavy crude declines attributed to US refinery turnaround season — a **-7.44%** day and a **-4.90%** day. The honest read: crude is being whipsawed between a geopolitical risk premium and a seasonal demand trough, with no clean resolution.

**Precious metals lost their bid twice.** Gold rallied post-US-midterms on falling dollar and yields, then stalled as risk-on returned and the market turned toward pending US jobs data — explicitly flagged in the feed as a dollar-rally risk for MCX gold and silver. Silver's path was violent: a **-5.13%** crash on industrial-demand fears around 30 October, a **+3.94%** break to ₹186,601/kg on 4 November, and back to ₹184,977 now. The **gold-silver ratio at 78.5** reflects silver's higher beta, not a resolved industrial view.

Base metals were quietly heavy: copper **₹1,338.85/kg (-0.91%)** on profit-taking, zinc **₹412 (-0.57%)**, lead **₹194.75**, nickel **₹1,537.90**, aluminium **₹330.05 (+0.06%)**. Natural gas was the lone clear gainer — **₹284.80/mmBtu (+0.96%)** with Henry Hub at **$3.00 (+1.90%)**.

## MCX transmission

The arithmetic is unusually clean this session, and it is worth showing because it tells you where the pressure is coming from.

For gold: COMEX **-0.83%** plus rupee appreciation **-0.40%** equals roughly **-1.23%** — against an actual MCX move of **-1.20%**. Essentially all of the domestic decline is imported. There is no meaningful local premium or discount being added.

For silver: COMEX **-0.51%** plus FX **-0.40%** equals **-0.91%**, against an actual **-1.16%**. The residual ~0.25 points is domestic-specific selling, consistent with the industrial-demand concerns in the feed.

For crude: WTI **-0.20%** plus FX **-0.40%** equals **-0.60%**, against **-0.95%** — a ~0.35-point local residual.

**One essential caveat.** The derived import-parity figures carry a system warning: the gold parity wedge reads **15.29%** against silver's **17.59%**, a **2.3-point gap** beyond the 2-point tolerance, indicating one price feed may be stale. These derived parity numbers exclude duty, GST, and trade premiums, so they are not a clean arbitrage read in any case. Treat the wedge as directional context, not a tradeable level.
