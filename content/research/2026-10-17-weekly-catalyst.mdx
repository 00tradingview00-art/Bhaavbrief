## The catalyst

One number organises the whole week: Brent at **$109.69** against WTI at **$99.04** — a **$10.65 differential**, widened on Friday by Brent's **+3.56%** move versus WTI's **+1.09%**.

That split is the evidence. A demand-led oil rally lifts both benchmarks roughly together. A *seaborne supply* rally lifts Brent first, because Brent prices waterborne barrels exposed to shipping routes, export terminals and refinery outages, while WTI prices a landlocked US delivery point. When the gap widens by ~2.5 percentage points in a single session, the market is paying for **risk of barrels not arriving**, not for stronger consumption.

The supplied week explains why: Ukraine resumed attacks on Russian refineries with Zelenskyy declaring the energy ceasefire over, Iran's Hormuz posture stayed in the headlines with a geopolitical premium described as "locked in," and China's pull on Brazilian crude tightened the Atlantic basin. Three independent channels — refining capacity, chokepoint risk, and cargo reallocation — all push the same direction, and all of them hit *Brent-linked* barrels hardest.

This matters now because **MCX Crude Oil expires Monday, 19 October**. The complex is being asked to converge a WTI-referenced Indian contract at the exact moment the global premium is concentrated in the *other* benchmark.

## What changed this week

Crude did the heavy lifting and nothing else confirmed it.

MCX Crude closed **₹9,572/bbl, +3.09%** on Friday, after +1.82% and +1.53% sessions earlier in the week — a three-step repricing, not a single headline spike. Each of those moves was initially framed around rupee mechanics, but the rupee has since stopped cooperating with that story: **USD/INR at 95.07, down 0.09%**. The rupee is no longer amplifying. What's left in the INR barrel is almost entirely the dollar barrel.

Critically, the rest of the energy complex refused to follow. **Henry Hub fell 3.68% to $3.12**, and MCX Natural Gas sat flat at **₹298.5 (-0.03%)** after a week of 2–3.5% declines attributed to storage surplus and mild weather. If this were a broad energy or inflation impulse, gas would not be bleeding. The divergence is the cleanest internal confirmation that the bid is **supply-specific to crude**, not macro-wide.

Precious metals moved, but modestly and sympathetically: MCX Gold **₹1,48,000 (+0.35%)**, MCX Silver **₹2,11,418 (+0.36%)**, COMEX Gold $4,186.7, COMEX Silver $57.14, gold-silver ratio **73.3**. Base metals were mixed — zinc +0.92%, aluminium +0.51%, copper +0.25%, nickel **-1.06%**. A mixed base complex alongside a 3% crude move is not the signature of a global growth upgrade.

## MCX transmission

Two wedges deserve attention before Monday.

**The crude wedge.** At ₹9,572 and USD/INR 95.07, the MCX contract implies roughly **$100.7/bbl** against a WTI print of $99.04 — a premium of about 1.7%. Part of that is session-timing mismatch between the snapshot stamps; part of it is expiry-week positioning in a contract that rolls monthly and therefore carries less cushion than metals. The mechanism to watch is simple: if Brent's premium reflects *waterborne* risk that WTI cannot fully capture, an expiring WTI-referenced MCX contract can be pulled toward its anchor even while global headlines stay tense. Convergence pressure and narrative pressure can point in opposite directions in the same week.

**The parity wedge — treat with caution.** The snapshot flags a data-integrity warning: gold's import-parity spread reads **15.65%** against silver's **21.05%**, a 5.4-point gap versus a 2-point tolerance. One leg of that calculation (MCX or COMEX side) is likely stale. Parity spreads should **not** be treated as a tradable signal this week until the feed reconciles. The gold-silver ratio of 73.3 is the more robust cross-check available.

## What would change the view

The thesis is that this is a **seaborne supply premium**, and it fails if the Brent–WTI differential compresses materially while crude headlines remain unchanged — that would mean the premium was positioning, not physical risk. It also weakens if natural gas reverses sharply higher alongside crude, which would reframe the move as a broad energy impulse rather than a crude-specific one. Conversely, a widening differential alongside further base-metal softness would strengthen the supply-shock read, not the growth read.

For bullion, the dominant macro driver sits outside this week: the **FOMC decision on 28 October**, which moves US real yields and the dollar. Until then, gold's crude-linked and dollar-linked components are doing the work, and the parity-feed warning limits what can be concluded from Indian premium data.
