## The catalyst

The RBI Monetary Policy Committee decision lands **7 October at 04:30 UTC (10:00 IST)** — inside the Indian trading session, with MCX open. Of the eight scheduled events in the forward calendar, it is the only one tagged to all nine MCX contracts: gold, silver, crude, copper, natgas, zinc, aluminium, lead and nickel.

That tagging is not a formality. Every MCX contract is INR-denominated and benchmarked off a USD-quoted international reference. The repo decision and, more importantly, the accompanying signal on rupee management, move USD/INR — and a USD/INR move reprices the entire board mechanically, with zero change in the underlying dollar price. This is the rare event where the currency leg, not the commodity leg, is the dominant variable.

The setup going in is already currency-led. USD/INR closed at **96.54, up 0.43%** from 96.13. Against the crosses, the rupee held: EUR/INR **109.10 (−0.25%)**, GBP/INR **127.65 (−0.10%)**, JPY/INR **61.265 (−0.08%)**. A rupee weaker against the dollar but firm against euro, sterling and yen is a *dollar-strength* signature, not an India-specific stress signature. That distinction matters for how the MPC is likely to be read.

## What changed this week

The week's feed reads like a currency diary, not a commodity one. Headlines ran from *"MCX Crude rallies 1.55% as rupee weakness resurfaces"* (1 Oct) to *"MCX Crude Falls −2.46% as Rupee Strength Reverses Export Support"* (29 Sep) to *"MCX Natural Gas Falls 3.57% as Rupee Strength Weighs"* (28 Sep) and *"USD/INR Rebounds +0.46% Rupee Reversal Amid Portfolio Flows"* (1 Oct), following *"USD/INR weakens 0.23% on sustained FPI inflows"* (30 Sep). Direction flipped twice in four sessions. The driver did not.

The snapshot shows the same wedge. MCX Gold **₹147,724/10g (+1.10%)** while COMEX Gold printed **$4,162.3 (−0.95%)**. MCX Silver **₹225,545/kg (+0.82%)** against COMEX Silver **$60.42 (−0.51%)**. MCX Crude **₹9,008/bbl (+3.14%)** against WTI **$91.11 (−1.90%)** and Brent **$102.25 (−0.06%)**. MCX NatGas **₹288.1 (−0.76%)** against Henry Hub **$3.04 (+2.29%)**. Four contracts, four sign inversions versus their own benchmarks.

Part of that is translation. Part is timing: the snapshot carries an MCX close dated 1 Oct against later-refreshed international quotes, so same-day comparisons overstate the divergence. **Treat the derived parity numbers with care.** The system itself flagged a `PARITY_WEDGE_DIVERGENCE`: gold import-parity spread **14.35%** versus silver **20.27%**, a 5.92-point gap against a 2-point tolerance, meaning one leg of one feed is likely stale. Import-parity gold reads ₹129,191 and silver ₹187,533; the gold/silver ratio reads **68.9**. Use these as orientation, not as a tradable basis.

Elsewhere: base metals were uniformly soft — nickel **−1.58%**, zinc **−1.36%**, aluminium **−1.12%**, lead **−0.83%**, copper **−0.19%** — consistent with a firmer dollar rather than any fresh demand news. MCX Electricity rose **2.06% to ₹6,487/MWh**. Crude's own week was supply-headline whiplash: Yanbu port restart (29 Sep), a Hormuz rebound note, then Middle East supply surge pressure on 2 Oct. A Supreme Court ruling upholding MCX's circular permitting negative settlement pricing for crude futures was also reported — structurally relevant for contract mechanics, not for next week's direction.

## MCX transmission

Three channels, in order of force for 7 October:

**1. Direct translation.** A 1% USD/INR move reprices every INR contract roughly 1% against an unchanged dollar benchmark. Gold and silver, which carry no domestic supply offset, transmit this most cleanly.

**2. Policy-rate and real-yield channel.** A dovish signal that compresses Indian real yields can support domestic gold demand independently of COMEX, while simultaneously pressuring the rupee — the two push MCX Gold the same way.

**3. Risk-appetite channel.** A hawkish surprise that tightens INR liquidity tends to weigh on industrial-metal and energy positioning, compounding any dollar-side drag on copper, zinc, aluminium, lead and nickel.

Crude carries one extra layer: **OPEC+/JMMC meets Sunday 4 October at 13:00 UTC**, before Monday's MCX open. That creates Monday gap risk in crude *before* the MPC, so crude's week has two sequential catalysts while the other eight contracts have one.

## What would change the view

The thesis weakens if the MPC passes without a material USD/INR reaction, which would hand control back to COMEX and LME. It weakens faster if Sunday's OPEC+ decision produces a large enough supply shock that crude's dollar leg overwhelms the currency leg for the whole complex. And the parity-wedge flag is a live caveat: if the stale feed resolves and the real gold–silver spread converges, the apparent MCX-vs-COMEX inversions shrink, and so does the evidence for a currency-dominant week.
