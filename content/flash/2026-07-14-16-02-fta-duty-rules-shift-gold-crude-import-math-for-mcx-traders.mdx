**WHAT HAPPENED**
The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) issued detailed rules for claiming preferential duties under the India-UK Free Trade Agreement, establishing the documentation and origin verification framework that applies to **gold** and **crude oil** imports qualifying for reduced tariffs.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Importers of **UK-origin gold** and **crude oil** routed through the FTA corridor now face a dual-cost structure: they must meet strict rules-of-origin compliance and maintain certified documentation to claim the preferential rate, or pay the standard import duty on the full landed value. This administrative burden creates a cost-of-compliance floor that directly reprices the import parity for both commodities, shifting the landed cost calculation for MCX futures holders carrying overnight positions and refiners locking in import costs on forward contracts.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Gold importers in Mumbai, Ahmedabad, and Delhi-NCR refineries face a higher compliance cost per kilogram imported under the FTA unless they maintain CBIC-certified chain-of-custody documentation, while crude oil refiners in Gujarat and Maharashtra must now embed the verification timeline into their import logistics to capture preferential rates. Jewellers purchasing gold in the domestic market and petrol pump retailers stocking fuel across India experience an immediate repricing signal if importers pass through compliance costs or absorb tariff penalties.

**BOTTOM LINE**
**Gold refiners and jewellery manufacturers** will see their import-parity costs rise if compliance costs exceed the preferential duty savings, directly compressing refining margins. **MCX gold futures** (August and December contracts) will likely test support at import parity levels adjusted for the new documentation overhead. **Retail petrol prices** could face upward pressure if crude importers absorb FTA verification delays or miss the preferential window.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Monitor CBIC's next circular on deemed-compliant FTA importers (expected by end of Q2) and track whether refiners begin passing compliance costs to spot gold premiums—a signal that the tariff benefit is eroding faster than expected.
