**WHAT HAPPENED**
The government increased import duties on gold and silver through a revised tariff notification, raising the effective landed cost for domestic refiners and banks importing bullion.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Higher tariffs compress margins for **MMTC, ICCL, and bank refineries** importing refined gold, as the duty floor now sits above the MCX spot-futures arbitrage window. This tariff premium incentivizes grey-market inflows—smuggled gold entering via land borders and sea routes—which bypass duty entirely, undercutting legitimate refinery offtake and forcing **MCX gold futures** to trade at artificially widened spreads to physical. The RBI's gold monetization scheme becomes less attractive to depositors when tariff-free domestic recycling costs more than smuggled metal.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Bank treasuries and MMTC refineries importing refined gold bars face an immediate duty cost increase on every kilogram cleared, while jewellery manufacturers in Surat, Mumbai, and Jaipur sourcing gold through both formal imports and informal channels now see a two-tier pricing structure where smuggled metal undercuts tariff-paid supplies. Retail jewellers stocking inventory and end consumers purchasing gold ornaments and coins experience price bifurcation—formal retail absorbs the tariff cost while unaccounted jewellers offer duty-free pricing, eroding documented market share.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Refinery spreads on **MCX gold futures** will widen as the tariff creates a price floor above which legal imports cannot compete, forcing refineries to hedge aggressively or reduce import volumes. **MCX gold** spot-March spreads may invert or flatten as smuggled physical supply dampens official demand. Retail gold prices in branded jewellery stores will rise while unregistered retailers gain margin advantage, fragmenting the consumer market.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Monitor the next **RBI monetary policy review** in February and any DGFT clarification on duty applicability to bank gold deposits, which may signal reversal scope. Watch for MCX gold open interest shifts toward deferred contracts as refiners shift hedging strategies.
