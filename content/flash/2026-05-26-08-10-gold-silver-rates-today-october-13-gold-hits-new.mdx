**WHAT HAPPENED**
Gold struck a fresh record high on MCX, with the benchmark contract trading around ₹160,948/10g, tracking COMEX spot gold at $4,571/oz against a USD/INR rate of ₹95.24.

**MCX IMPACT**
The MCX Gold (December contract) is the primary instrument affected, with the record print driven by a combination of elevated COMEX prices and a weakened rupee amplifying the landed cost for Indian importers. At ₹95.24 per dollar, every $10 move on COMEX translates to approximately ₹952/10g on MCX — a leverage effect that widens the domestic premium above international parity. India's standard 15% customs duty plus GST on gold imports means the effective import cost sits well above raw COMEX-to-INR conversion, structurally supporting MCX prices at current levels. Traders holding long positions in MCX Gold benefit from this dual tailwind — rising dollar prices and rupee depreciation — while physical market participants face compressed margins as import costs remain elevated.

**WATCH**
The next critical trigger is the U.S. CPI data release and any Federal Reserve commentary on rate trajectory, as a softer inflation print would pressure COMEX gold and directly drag MCX below the ₹160,000/10g psychological support level.
