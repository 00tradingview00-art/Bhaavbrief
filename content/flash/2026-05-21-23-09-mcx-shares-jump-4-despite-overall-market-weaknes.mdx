**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX (Multi Commodity Exchange) shares surged 4% against broader market weakness, driven by a sharp spike in commodity trading volumes across gold, silver, crude oil, and base metals contracts.

**MCX IMPACT**
Elevated volatility across MCX's flagship contracts — Gold (₹161,662/10g), Silver (₹261,989/kg), and Crude Oil (₹9,640/bbl) — directly inflates MCX's transaction fee revenue, as the exchange earns per-lot charges on every trade executed. Higher COMEX gold at $4,545/oz and WTI crude at $98.23/bbl translate into wider absolute rupee swings per contract, incentivising higher speculative and hedging participation on MCX. For Indian traders, the USD/INR rate at ₹96.21 amplifies import cost sensitivity — every dollar move in COMEX gold now shifts MCX prices by ₹96.21 per troy ounce before customs duty adjustments, sustaining premium spreads versus COMEX. Copper at $6.3423/lb on COMEX further supports base metal turnover on MCX, adding to exchange revenue breadth.

**WATCH**
MCX's next quarterly earnings disclosure and SEBI's transaction charge review will either confirm sustained revenue growth or signal margin compression for the exchange. A breakdown in COMEX gold below $4,500/oz or WTI crude below $95/bbl would reduce contract value and dampen volume-driven fee income.
