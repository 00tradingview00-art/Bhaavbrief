**WHAT HAPPENED**
Silver on MCX breached the ₹2,50,000/kg level to trade below that threshold, while gold prices also declined on the exchange amid softness in international precious metals markets.

**MCX IMPACT**
MCX Silver (near-month contract) bears direct pressure as the spot rate slips under ₹2,50,000/kg against the live COMEX reference translating to approximately ₹2,56,935/kg — indicating the MCX front-month is trading at a notable discount to the converted COMEX rate, reflecting local demand weakness overriding the currency buffer. MCX Gold similarly retreats from the ₹1,59,597/10g import-parity level, with domestic spot prices lagging the COMEX $4,511/oz benchmark after customs duty and GST are layered in. The spread compression between MCX and COMEX-converted rates signals subdued domestic physical demand, as jewellers and bullion dealers remain on the sidelines rather than absorbing import-parity-linked prices. For Indian traders, the USD/INR at ₹95.70 continues to provide partial insulation against COMEX declines, but that cushion is insufficient to prevent MCX from following the international downtrend when local demand fails to support premiums.

**WATCH**
The next directional trigger is the US PCE inflation print and any Federal Reserve commentary on rate trajectory, as a softer inflation reading will pressure COMEX gold and silver further, pulling MCX contracts below ₹1,57,000/10g and ₹2,45,000/kg respectively.
