**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX gold fell **₹142,413/10g**, down **1.21%**, as renewed inflation concerns and US-Iran geopolitical tensions drove safe-haven demand into currency hedges rather than bullion. Silver tracked lower at **-0.78%**, while natural gas led losses across the complex at **-3.10%**, signalling demand destruction in energy-intensive sectors.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Gold's decline despite geopolitical risk suggests real-yield mechanics are overriding the traditional safe-haven bid — investors are pricing sticky inflation expectations into duration assets rather than physical gold. At current USD/INR of **₹94.53**, the import parity floor for MCX gold (global spot plus conversion costs) sits approximately **₹140,500–141,200/10g**; MCX trading at **₹142,413** implies a **₹1,200–1,900/10g premium** that compresses if US real yields rise further or INR strengthens on capital inflows.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
A jewellery manufacturer importing 100kg monthly faces delayed hedging decisions — locking forward contracts at current premium levels versus deferring on expectations of **₹141,000–level capitulation**. An import-reliant gold refiner managing working capital through MCX short positions benefits from the premium compression risk, but carries execution risk if geopolitical headlines spike. A retail hedger (wedding-linked gold purchases) faces price discovery delays as spot-futures arbitrage narrows.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Gold's **1.21% decline** despite Iran tensions reveals real-yield (not geopolitical) is the dominant driver; traders should monitor whether USD/INR stays above **₹94.50** tomorrow — a break below signals capital flight and forces MCX repricing upward.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
US CPI print (8 July 2026) and any escalation in US-Iran military posturing. MCX open will telegraph whether the **₹141,500** support holds or India-specific demand flows lift the premium.
