**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX Crude fell **1.59% to ₹6,621/bbl** in today's session, extending pressure from US oil weakness overnight. Gold slipped **0.03% to ₹1,60,000/10g**, weighed by a strengthening USD/INR at **₹94.65**, which raises the rupee cost of dollar-denominated bullion imports. Copper alone bucked the trend, gaining **1.16% to ₹1,259/kg**, signalling industrial demand resilience independent of the broader commodity selloff.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Crude's decline reflects a separation of geopolitical premium from demand fundamentals—Middle East tensions have not translated into sustained price support as global growth concerns dominate US/EU session trading. Gold's slide despite a stronger dollar indicates real-yield headwinds are outweighing safe-haven demand; when rupee strength adds **40 paise per gram of import parity pressure** (dollar-gold down + INR up), MCX gold becomes structurally less competitive for fresh hedging against imported supplies. Copper's **1.16% gain contradicts** the risk-off narrative, suggesting equity markets and manufacturing sentiment remain bifurcated from commodity macro.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
An **oil marketing company** setting weekly fuel hedges faces lower crude input costs but must navigate basis risk if rupee strength reverses intra-month. A **jewellery manufacturer** sourcing imported gold on forward contracts sees locked-in rupee costs rise, pressuring margin on retail-bound inventory. An **auto-components producer** using copper-alloy inputs is receiving a demand signal from copper strength that contradicts deflation fears priced into crude.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Crude's **1.59% fall is demand-driven, not geopolitical**—a critical distinction for MCX hedgers pricing Middle East tail-risk. Copper's **+1.16% outperformance signals manufacturing demand has not collapsed**, even as energy retreats.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
US Initial Jobless Claims (Thursday, US morning) for confirmation of demand softness; breach of **₹6,550/bbl support** on MCX would confirm crude demand narrative.
