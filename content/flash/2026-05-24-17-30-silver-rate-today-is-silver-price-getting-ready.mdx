**WHAT HAPPENED**
Silver on COMEX is trading at $76.20/oz, drawing analyst comparisons to the 1980 Hunt Brothers squeeze, raising questions about whether current prices reflect a structurally overbought condition.

**MCX IMPACT**
MCX Silver (near-month contract) is directly exposed, with spot import cost currently benchmarked at approximately ₹257,896/kg after converting COMEX prices at USD/INR ₹95.70. At this level, Indian physical buyers — jewellers and industrial users — face sharply elevated import costs, as India sources silver predominantly through imports, making the landed price sensitive to both COMEX direction and rupee movement. A sustained COMEX correction of even 8–10% from current levels translates to a ₹20,000–25,000/kg drop on MCX, a material swing for traders carrying long inventory. The MCX–COMEX spread remains tight, meaning any global sentiment shift transmits with minimal lag into Indian futures pricing, leaving little buffer for domestic hedgers.

**WATCH**
The next COMEX silver weekly close above or below $74.00/oz — the prior breakout consolidation zone — determines whether the current price structure holds or triggers technical long liquidation. US Federal Reserve minutes and the upcoming US CPI print are the macro triggers that directly move USD/INR and COMEX silver simultaneously, amplifying MCX volatility on both axes.
