**WHAT HAPPENED**
Ukraine has intensified strikes on Russian petroleum refineries ahead of a White House meeting, targeting fuel production infrastructure in western Russia.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Refinery outages in Russia tighten global crude supply, pushing **MCX Crude at ₹8563/bbl** higher as Indian importers and refinery procurement teams face elevated rupee-denominated acquisition costs. Simultaneously, geopolitical risk premiums lift **MCX Gold toward ₹144045/10g** as safe-haven demand rises; bullion dealers and hedging desks holding dollar-linked exposure see rupee inventory values climb as the **USD/INR spot holds at ₹94.53**. Natural gas volatility expands on concerns over regional energy disruption, affecting forward contracting mechanics for power utilities and fertiliser plants.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Indian refiners importing Urals and other Russian grades face margin compression as crude spot premiums widen, forcing them to either absorb costs or pass them to downstream diesel and petrol retailers. Petrochemical manufacturers and lubricant producers using crude derivatives see input repricing ripple through production cycles, while truck operators and fleet owners confront higher fuel pump prices at retail counters within weeks. Households purchasing diesel for agricultural pumps and generators, alongside fertiliser-dependent farmers, experience input cost inflation; jewellery retailers and consumers buying gold-backed investment products absorb the rupee revaluation embedded in fresh purchases.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Indian oil refiners operating on thin export margins will either reduce run rates or lock hedges on MCX Crude futures to protect quarterly earnings. A sustained breach above **₹8700/bbl** on MCX Crude would signal structural supply loss pricing into the contract. Retail diesel prices at petrol pumps will rise by ₹1–3 per litre within the settlement cycle.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Monitor Russian refinery damage assessments and EU import-flow data over the next 72 hours; any OPEC emergency communication or US-led negotiations outcome at the White House meeting will either accelerate or neutralise this risk premium.
