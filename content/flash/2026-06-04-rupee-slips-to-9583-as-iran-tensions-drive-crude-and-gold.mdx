**WHAT HAPPENED**
The **Indian rupee** depreciated **7 paise** to close at **₹95.83** against the US dollar on Thursday, driven by escalating **US-Iran** geopolitical tensions fuelling energy volatility and safe-haven demand.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
A weaker rupee at **₹95.83** directly reprices India's energy import bill upward — at current **WTI crude of $92.43/bbl**, MCX crude translates to approximately **₹9,023/bbl**, and any further rupee slide mechanically lifts that landed cost without any move in dollar-denominated crude. Simultaneously, **MCX Gold** at approximately **₹1,59,871/10g** and **MCX Silver** at **₹2,51,444/kg** see rupee-denominated valuations lift in lockstep with INR weakness, as safe-haven dollar buying pushes both the commodity price and the exchange rate against domestic holders. **RBI** intervention posture now becomes the swing factor controlling the transmission speed of these import-cost pressures.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
**HPCL**, **BPCL**, and **Indian Oil** crude import desks, **MCX Crude** and **MCX Gold** futures traders, **Zaveri Bazaar** bullion dealers, gold ETF desks at **HDFC AMC** and **SBI MF**, and airline fuel procurement teams at **IndiGo** and **Air India**.

**BOTTOM LINE**
**HPCL** and **BPCL** see their per-barrel import cost structurally elevated as the rupee weakens past **₹95.83**, squeezing refining margins on every unhedged cargo. **MCX Gold** futures carry an upside bias as both dollar gold price and rupee depreciation compound the rupee-denominated price floor. Retail buyers of petrol and aviation turbine fuel face a structurally higher pump-price floor until the rupee recovers or crude reverses.
