**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Natural Gas collapsed **5.98%** to **₹289.40/mmBtu** in afternoon trade, the sharpest mover on the board. Crude Oil retreated **2.52%** to **₹6,895/bbl**, while Copper advanced **2.23%** to **₹1,296.35/kg** in a divergent cross-commodity session. Gold and Silver both posted gains—**1.0%** and **1.36%** respectively—signaling competing narratives within the commodity complex.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

Natural Gas weakness reflects domestic oversupply or demand destruction unrelated to broader commodity sentiment; unlike crude, it carries no geopolitical premium and moves on storage, weather, and regional LNG flows. The simultaneous rally in Gold (**₹145,121/10g**) and Silver (**₹226,483/kg**) on crude weakness points to safe-haven positioning rather than inflation expectations—real yields are the primary driver here, not reflation. Copper's **+2.23%** gain contradicts the risk-off narrative and suggests industrial demand expectations remain intact despite broader commodity pullback, indicating sector-specific confidence separate from macro risk sentiment.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

A power generation company with gas-fired capacity faces margin compression as input costs fall faster than power purchase agreement rates can adjust downward. A jewellery manufacturer benefits from stable precious metal costs for near-term procurement planning when safe-haven flows lock in pricing stability. An EPC contractor engaged in renewable projects (solar wiring, transmission) sees improved margin outlook if Copper consolidation supports capex deployment timelines.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Nat Gas isolation (**−5.98%** vs. Crude **−2.52%**) reveals commodity fragmentation: energy complex splitting on supply structure, not unified macro shock. Copper outperformance amid safe-haven bid confirms industrial demand from capex cycles is pricing independent of geopolitical risk premiums.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

USDoil settlement tonight and MCX Nat Gas storage data release timing—confirm whether domestic oversupply persists or if crude's retreat signals demand softening that should drag gas lower into close.
