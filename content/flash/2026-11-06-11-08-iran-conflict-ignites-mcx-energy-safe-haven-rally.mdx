**WHAT HAPPENED**
Military escalation between Iran and Israel around the Strait of Hormuz has triggered supply-route disruption fears, sending crude oil, natural gas, and gold higher across MCX contracts.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Refineries and oil importers settling crude purchases at elevated **MCX Crude ₹8535/bbl** levels now lock in higher rupee-denominated feedstock costs per barrel, while natural gas procurement desks face spot repricing risk on winter demand overlaying conflict-driven supply anxiety. Gold's safe-haven bid — with **MCX Gold at ₹142116/10g** — immediately reprices rupee-denominated bullion dealer inventory upward, compressing margins on dollar-hedged long positions and lifting acquisition costs for jewellers sourcing physical metal.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Petroleum refiners and bulk fuel importers absorb the crude repricing on every shipment cleared; petrochemical units dependent on naphtha feedstock see input cost step higher, squeezing conversion spreads. Cement manufacturers, power plants, and fertiliser producers using natural gas in production face fuel-cost volatility, forcing faster pass-through to wholesale pricing or margin compression. Jewellers and ornament retailers holding finished-goods inventory see their rupee acquisition value rise, forcing retail price lists upward — festival season gold purchases and bridal jewellery orders now carry elevated ticket prices. Households purchasing cooking gas cylinders and piped natural gas for winter heating absorb utility bill increases as distributors repriced contracted volumes.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Fuel retailers and oil marketing companies operating on fixed-margin models suffer contracted margin erosion on every litre sold at current pump pricing until tariff adjustments flow through. **MCX Crude** has decisively breached typical monsoon-season ranges, signalling structural supply anxiety lasting beyond immediate headlines. Gold jewellery purchasers and bulk bullion buyers will pay materially more per gram at retail counters within 48–72 hours.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
OPEC's next supply guidance and Strait of Hormuz shipping reports will confirm whether the disruption risk is transient or structural. Brent-WTI spread widening or Hormuz transit vessel tracking data will signal real logistics stress.
