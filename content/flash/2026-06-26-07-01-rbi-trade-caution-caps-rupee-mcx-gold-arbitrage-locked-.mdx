**WHAT HAPPENED**
USD/INR opened at **₹94.39** this morning, showing marginal strength as the rupee stabilizes near multi-month highs. Societe Generale's fresh commentary highlights that RBI's policy stance and lingering trade risks are capping further rupee appreciation, preventing a sharper dollar weakness. MCX commodity complex remains range-bound with sub-0.2% moves across gold (**₹143,127/10g**), silver (**₹219,672/kg**), and crude (**₹6,790/bbl**).

**WHAT IT MEANS**
A stronger rupee (lower USD/INR) mechanically compresses import parity for all dollar-linked MCX contracts. At current **₹94.39**, gold's global parity sits ~USD 1,920/oz ÷ 94.39 = **₹181,510 per 10g** (before duty adjustment), yet MCX trades **₹143,127** — indicating embedded arbitrage compression. RBI's policy caution signals the central bank won't aggressively defend rupee strength, creating asymmetric upside for USD/INR that could re-steepen import parity and pull MCX higher into next week.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Gold importers (Tanishq, Malabar Gold, WhiteLabel refiners) face margin compression on fresh shipments priced at current parity. Silver-consuming electronics manufacturers (Rajesh Masrani, Ador Welding) hedge procurement expecting rupee weakness; stable **₹94.39** removes urgency. Crude refiners (Reliance, Essar) gain modest tailwind from rupee stability protecting their downstream INR realization.

**BOTTOM LINE**
RBI's trade-risk caution prevents explosive rupee gains; expect USD/INR to consolidate **₹94.20–₹94.60** through July, pinning MCX imports in narrow bands with no fresh arbitrage entry.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
RBI's next MPC commentary (expected early July) on rupee management and trade flow assumptions — any hawkish pivot triggers USD/INR spike toward **₹95.20**, widening import parity sharply.
