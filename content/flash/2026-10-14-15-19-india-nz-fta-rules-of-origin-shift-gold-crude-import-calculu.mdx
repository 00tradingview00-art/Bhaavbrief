**WHAT HAPPENED**
The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) notified revised Rules of Origin under the India–New Zealand Free Trade Agreement, effective **October 20, 2026**, altering tariff classification and cumulation requirements for gold and crude oil imports from New Zealand.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Gold refiners and crude oil importers sourcing from New Zealand will face recalibrated landed-cost calculations as origin-of-goods thresholds tighten, potentially narrowing duty arbitrage windows that currently favour NZ shipments over third-country suppliers. MCX futures traders holding long positions in **gold at ₹146,284/10g** and **crude at ₹9,049/bbl** will need to reprice forward import parity levels, as the effective import-duty floor shifts and eligibility for preferential tariff treatment becomes contingent on stricter value-add or processing criteria.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Bullion dealers and refineries importing doré or semi-finished gold from New Zealand will absorb higher compliance costs for documentation and origin verification, compressing refining margins on domestic MCX-hedged flows. Downstream jewellery manufacturers and e-commerce gold retailers sourcing refined bullion will see input procurement windows narrow, forcing faster inventory turns or higher working-capital outlay. Petroleum retailers and furnace operators purchasing crude for domestic processing face similar origin-tracking overhead, shifting the economics of spot versus forward buying; households consuming refined fuels and jewellery will experience cost pressure if retailers pass through margin compression over the following quarter.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Bullion refineries will incur higher compliance and origin-verification costs on New Zealand imports, eating into per-gram refining spreads. **MCX Gold futures** may see subtle upward pressure on import-parity prices as arbitrage windows narrow, signalling caution for near-term long holders. Retail jewellery buyers will likely encounter steady-to-firm pricing at point of sale as refiners adjust procurement strategy.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Monitor CBIC clarifications or amendments to the origin rules by end-Q4 2026, and track whether gold and crude import volumes from New Zealand contract or shift to other FTA partners, which will signal real-world impact on MCX spreads.
