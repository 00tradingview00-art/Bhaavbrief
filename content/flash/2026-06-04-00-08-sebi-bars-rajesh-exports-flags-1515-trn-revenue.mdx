**WHAT HAPPENED**
SEBI has barred Rajesh Exports and its owner from securities markets pending investigation into an alleged ₹15.15 trillion revenue inflation scam.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Rajesh Exports, one of India's largest gold refining and jewellery export companies, processes significant volumes of physical gold, and a market ban disrupts its ability to raise capital or execute listed-market hedging operations. With MCX gold sitting at approximately ₹158,076/10g, any forced unwinding of inventory positions or hedge books by the company creates directional pressure on domestic gold spot and futures pricing. Bullion dealers and refiners who use Rajesh Exports as a counterparty for gold procurement or exports face immediate counterparty risk on open contracts.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
MCX gold futures traders, bullion refiners and exporters in the SEEPZ and Zaveri Bazaar ecosystem, gold ETF desks at HDFC AMC and Nippon India MF tracking physical gold flows, Rajesh Exports' institutional shareholders, and export-linked jewellery manufacturers supplying to the Middle East and US markets.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Gold refining and jewellery export operations at Rajesh Exports face a full suspension of capital-market access, directly freezing working-capital cycles tied to high-value gold inventory. MCX Gold October futures carry a bearish overhang from potential forced inventory liquidation at current ₹158,076/10g levels. Retail buyers of Rajesh Exports-branded jewellery face supply disruption and potential price premium from alternative sourcing.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
SEBI's formal investigation order detailing the scope of the revenue inflation and any interim relief application by Rajesh Exports in SAT will set the next directional trigger for the stock and associated gold market positioning.
