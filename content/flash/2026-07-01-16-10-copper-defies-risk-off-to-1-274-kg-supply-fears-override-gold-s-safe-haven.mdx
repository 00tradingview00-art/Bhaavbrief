**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX Copper rose **₹1,274/kg (+0.27%)** in today's afternoon session, bucking the broader risk-off narrative where crude fell **1.66%** and gold surged **1.60%**. LME base metals hit record highs on supply anxieties around aluminium production disruptions, with institutional buying flowing into copper futures as industrial demand signals remain intact.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Copper's modest gain despite a safe-haven rally in gold reveals divergent demand drivers: while equity and geopolitical anxiety pushed traders into bullion, base metals remain tethered to real industrial consumption forecasts rather than macro hedging flows. Global copper import parity (LME spot × **₹95.24/USD** conversion × duty levies) sits above **₹1,272/kg**, creating a floor beneath MCX pricing that mechanical arbitrage is respecting even as risk appetite deteriorates.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
A wire-harness manufacturer hedging Q3 copper exposure faces decision points: forward-locking rates at current **₹1,274/kg** levels absorbs aluminium-related supply-chain premiums already priced into LME, but unhedged procurement assumes further weakness if industrial PMI data softens. An auto-component exporter quoting delivery contracts must decide whether to pass through LME-linked escalation clauses or absorb spreads against locked futures positions.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Copper's resistance despite a **1.60%** gold rally signals that supply-shock fears are overriding macro pessimism—the commodity is pricing structural scarcity, not recession anxiety. The **₹1,272–₹1,275/kg** band marks the pivot between import-parity support and speculative momentum.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
LME copper settlement tonight and any China manufacturing PMI forward guidance; a close below **₹1,270/kg** would confirm macro contagion has breached industrial demand shields.
