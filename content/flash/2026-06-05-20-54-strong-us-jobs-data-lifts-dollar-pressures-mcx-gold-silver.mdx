**WHAT HAPPENED**
US nonfarm payrolls came in stronger than expected, pushing the **10-year Treasury yield** higher and strengthening the **US Dollar Index**, which typically inverses gold and silver valuations.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
A stronger dollar raises the rupee-denominated cost of dollar-priced bullion for jewellers, refiners, and imports across India's gold corridor, while simultaneously reducing the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding precious metals for global ETF funds and central banks. **MCX Gold** and **MCX Silver** face immediate repricing pressure as dollar strength makes bullion more expensive in emerging market currencies; simultaneously, **MCX Crude** and **MCX Copper** see secondary pressure as a stronger dollar typically signals risk-off sentiment, dampening cyclical demand from Chinese manufacturers and reducing real-time import competitiveness for Indian fabricators hedging COMEX exposure.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Bullion importers, refinery operators, and jewellery exporters in Surat face immediate margin compression on dollar-priced raw material procurement, while corporate treasury desks hedging USD payables see their rupee cost basis shift upward in real time. Retail gold buyers timing purchases ahead of festive season demand, petrol pump operators anchored to crude import parity, and copper wire manufacturers in Gujarat absorb the downstream repricing directly through both spot and contract costs.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Jewellery retailers will face tighter margins on imported bullion within the next settlement cycle as rupee import costs reset. **MCX Gold October futures** will likely test support levels as dollar strength persists through Treasury yield momentum. Festival-season retail gold demand may shift downward if jewellery retail prices reflect the full repricing within the fortnight.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Monitor the **RBI's next monetary stance signal** and watch for any pullback in Treasury yields ahead of the next US CPI print, which would reverse this dollar strength narrative.
