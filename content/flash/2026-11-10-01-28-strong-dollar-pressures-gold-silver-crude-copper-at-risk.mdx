**WHAT HAPPENED**
The **US dollar index** strengthened ahead of Thursday's **core PCE inflation print**, with markets pricing in persistent price pressures that keep Federal Reserve rate-cut expectations suppressed versus earlier forecasts.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
A firmer dollar raises the rupee cost of importing **MCX Gold** and **MCX Silver** for Indian jewellers, refiners, and bullion dealers — since these commodities are priced in dollars globally but invoiced in rupees locally. Higher dollar strength also increases the real yield advantage of holding cash versus commodities, reducing speculative demand from portfolio managers and commodity funds that typically hedge inflation via **MCX Crude** and **MCX Copper**. Simultaneously, a stronger dollar signals tighter global financial conditions, which dampens industrial demand expectations from Chinese manufacturers and construction firms — the marginal buyers of copper and crude globally — further pressuring both contracts.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Jewellery fabricators and hallmarking units that source gold and silver on monthly forward contracts face immediate input-cost repricing, compressing working-capital margins. Retail jewellers operating on thin spreads between purchase and catalogue prices absorb this squeeze by either deferring purchases or marking up finished ornament prices, which slows seasonal demand from households and wedding-season buyers. Petroleum distributors and lubricant blenders sourcing crude-based feedstock see procurement costs rise in rupee terms, while industrial users of copper — wire manufacturers, FMEG producers, automotive suppliers — defer non-critical inventory builds, reducing spot demand at MCX.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Bullion dealers face margin compression on jewellery import contracts as rupee import costs rise with dollar strength. **MCX Gold and Silver** are priced for downside pressure if dollar momentum persists through the inflation print, while **MCX Crude** and **MCX Copper** signal softer industrial demand on tightening financial conditions. Retail household purchases of gold jewellery and copper-based consumer durables will face upward price pressure at point of sale.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
**US PCE inflation data** (Thursday) and any forward guidance from Fed speakers will determine if dollar strength is transient or structural; Chinese manufacturing PMI data next week will confirm whether industrial demand truly softens.
