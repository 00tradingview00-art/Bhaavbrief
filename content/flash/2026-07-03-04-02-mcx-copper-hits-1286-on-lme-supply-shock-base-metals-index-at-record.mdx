**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Copper rallied **₹1286/kg, up 0.86%** in this morning's session, joining a broad-based commodity advance alongside gold (**+1.32%**) and silver (**+1.64%**). The LME base metals index hit record highs on supply concerns in aluminium, creating a risk-on backdrop for industrial metals pricing globally.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

The copper advance reflects renewed industrial-demand appetite rather than safe-haven positioning — a divergence from gold and silver's dual safe-haven component. Global copper prices have strengthened on supply-chain anxiety, and with USD/INR at **₹95.23**, the rupee weakness adds **4–5%** annual import parity pressure on MCX levels relative to LME spot, yet today's move shows Indian copper is tracking the global industrial rally rather than being anchored by currency headwinds alone.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

Wire and cable manufacturers importing refined copper for domestic production face cost escalation on fresh procurement; those with forward contracts at lower levels benefit from today's hedge timing. Electrical equipment OEMs locked into fixed-price supply agreements with distributors now face margin compression if they replenish inventory above last month's base prices. Power transmission contractors bidding new infrastructure projects must reassess material-cost assumptions.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Copper's **0.86% gain** while crude rallies **0.81%** and gold climbs **1.32%** shows base metals are responding to genuine industrial-demand recovery signals, not currency or geopolitical premium. The aluminium supply shock is transmitting across the entire base metals complex.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

LME copper settlement tonight and any signals on Chinese demand data (usually released mid-month); a break above **₹1295/kg** on MCX would confirm the global momentum is sustained.
