**WHAT HAPPENED**
COMEX gold dropped $127/oz to $4,520 and silver fell $3/oz to $76.34 as reviving US-Iran tensions triggered inflation-hedging demand reversal, with traders unwinding safe-haven positions on geopolitical repositioning.

**MCX IMPACT**
MCX Gold (June contract) faces direct downside pressure tracking the COMEX slide, with the live benchmark now anchored at ₹1,59,935/10g — a ₹127/oz drop translates to approximately ₹4,500/10g erosion at the current USD/INR rate of ₹95.70. MCX Silver (July contract) mirrors the $3/oz fall, placing spot import cost near ₹2,58,366/kg with further softening likely intraday. Indian bullion importers face compressed margins as the landed cost — inclusive of 15% basic customs duty plus GST — remains elevated even as COMEX prices retreat, widening the domestic premium stress. The COMEX-MCX spread narrows when the rupee holds steady, reducing arbitrage opportunity for desk traders running cross-market positions.

**WATCH**
The next directional trigger is the US CPI release and any formal statement from US State Department on Iran nuclear negotiations — a hawkish inflation print above 3.5% or an escalation confirmation reactivates the safe-haven bid and reverses today's COMEX slide. MCX Gold ₹1,55,000/10g acts as the immediate technical reference level for intraday range confirmation.
