**WHAT HAPPENED**
Iran dismissed U.S. attack threats while signalling continued diplomatic channels remain open, leaving geopolitical risk premium embedded in oil markets as crude futures hold elevated levels.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
**MCX Crude at ₹9,715/bbl** reflects supply-side fear: any escalation in Iran conflict directly constrains Strait of Hormuz throughput, forcing Indian refineries to price barrels at a geopolitical markup regardless of underlying demand. Simultaneously, **MCX Gold near ₹143,150/10g** captures safe-haven inflows as traders hedge tail risks through bullion, making rupee-denominated gold holdings more expensive for spot-market buyers while foreign investors see dollar-based holdings gain nominal cover against INR weakness at **₹95.2/USD**.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Downstream oil processors and power utilities locked into monthly crude tenders face immediate cost pressure: every ₹100/bbl move in MCX contracts translates into margin compression on fuel surcharge recoveries from industrial customers. Mid-chain logistics operators and bitumen suppliers sourcing for road construction projects must either absorb higher feedstock costs or renegotiate quarterly pricing agreements, squeezing working capital for smaller suppliers. Gold jewellery retailers and wedding-season fabricators holding unhedged inventory see their cost base rise in rupee terms, while consumers deferring gold purchases ahead of festival season benefit temporarily from delayed buying decisions.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Aviation turbine fuel refiners and petrochemical blenders will see jet-fuel and polymer feedstock costs reprice sharply on any crude spike beyond ₹9,800/bbl threshold. **MCX Crude** remains biased upward as long as conflict rhetoric outpaces diplomatic progress signals. Retail jewellery buyers and wedding planners purchasing gold ornaments over the next fortnight face steady-state higher ticket prices until geopolitical premia fade.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Next OPEC+ production signal or U.S. State Department diplomatic statement will either confirm or defuse the current ₹9,700+ crude floor. Crude's breach above ₹9,850/bbl would trigger cascading hedging demand in natural gas futures as power plants pre-lock fuel costs.
