**WHAT HAPPENED**
Gold and silver contracts on MCX declined as a strengthening US dollar pressured precious metals globally, even as physical demand from Indian buyers accelerated ahead of the Akshaya Tritiya festival.

**MCX IMPACT**
MCX Gold (June contract) and MCX Silver (May contract) are the primary contracts under pressure, with spot gold already at approximately ₹160,960/10g and silver at ₹260,662/kg. The stronger dollar raises import costs for India, as gold is dollar-denominated and settled at the prevailing USD/INR rate of ₹96.21 — any further rupee depreciation widens the landed cost differential versus COMEX prices. The ₹160,960/10g level on MCX gold is a critical near-term reference; sustained trade below this signals domestic sentiment softening despite festive buying. Indian traders must account for the 15% customs duty plus GST component embedded in MCX pricing, which creates a structural premium over COMEX-derived fair value and limits downside even during dollar-driven global selloffs.

**WATCH**
The US Federal Reserve's next commentary on interest rate trajectory and the USD/INR exchange rate movement at the RBI reference fixing will either accelerate the MCX decline or trigger a reversal as Akshaya Tritiya physical demand provides a demand floor. A breach of $4,500/oz on COMEX gold is the key international level to monitor.
