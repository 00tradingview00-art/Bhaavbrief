**WHAT HAPPENED**
Goldman Sachs lowered its Federal Reserve rate-cut forecast, signalling the central bank will cut less aggressively than previously expected, keeping US interest rates higher for longer than market consensus anticipated.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Higher-for-longer US rates strengthen the **US dollar index**, raising the rupee-denominated cost of importing **MCX Gold** and **MCX Silver** for Indian jewellery fabricators, bullion dealers, and ETF issuers. Simultaneously, elevated real yields increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding precious metals for central banks and large institutional buyers, creating downward pressure on spot premiums. For **MCX Crude**, a stronger dollar makes energy imports costlier for Indian refiners and logistics operators hedging forward contracts. **MCX Copper** faces dual pressure: a stronger dollar reduces purchasing power for Asian fabricators, while persistent high rates delay infrastructure and construction spending in India and China that typically drive industrial metal demand.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Gold and silver fabricators sourcing raw material at Zaveri Bazaar and Jaipur refineries face immediate input cost repricing on monthly hedges, compressing artisan margins already under pressure from weak retail demand. Jewellery retailers and D2C brands holding finished-goods inventory must choose between raising catalogue prices ahead of wedding season or absorbing margin compression. Petroleum marketing companies and bitumen suppliers sourcing crude hedges see their input costs oscillate with dollar strength, directly affecting road construction project costs passed through to state highway authorities and private developers. End consumers purchasing gold ornaments, silver coins, and petroleum products at petrol pumps face potential price increases as importers rebuild hedges at unfavourable dollar rates.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Indian jewellery fabricators and bullion traders face immediate input cost pressure as rupee-denominated gold and silver imports become costlier on the back of a stronger dollar. **MCX Gold** and **MCX Silver** contracts will likely trade lower initially as reduced central bank accumulation demand meets rupee weakness headwinds. Households delaying gold and silver purchases ahead of festivals will encounter firmer retail prices, while petrol pump customers should expect crude-linked fuel prices to remain elevated.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Fed Chair Powell's next speech or the release of **US PCE inflation data** will signal whether Goldman's revised rate-cut forecast gains broader institutional traction. Chinese manufacturing PMI and India's GDP growth revision will reveal whether copper and crude demand can withstand sustained dollar strength.
