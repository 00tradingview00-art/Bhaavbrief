**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX crude fell **1.36% to ₹6,527/bbl** as global energy markets softened during the US/EU session. MCX natural gas dropped **1.76%** to ₹306.50/mmBtu, signalling broader hydrocarbon weakness. Gold bucked the energy decline, rising **1.30% to ₹144,389/10g**, establishing a classic risk-off cross-asset split.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

The crude decline reflects demand-side positioning rather than geopolitical premium contraction—US summer driving season demand signals are softening into late Q2. At current Brent-equivalent levels (₹6,527/bbl ÷ 95.24 USD/INR ≈ $68.5/bbl), India's import parity for crude sits above current MCX settlement, meaning landed cost for refiners tracks below headline futures, reducing immediate hedging urgency. Gold's counter-move to **+1.30%** reveals real-yield compression: if crude demand weakness signals growth deceleration, defensive positioning into gold accelerates despite rupee stability at **₹95.24/USD**.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

An oil marketing company hedging August-September fuel inventory faces a timing decision: crude futures weakness creates a window to roll forward-month protection at lower levels, but the speed of the decline (1.36% in one session) means execution risk on larger volumes. A refinery scheduler managing import crude costs sees narrowing arbitrage to process sour crude as Brent-WTI spreads compress alongside overall price weakness.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Today's data shows demand-side energy weakness (**crude -1.36%, nat gas -1.76%**) driving capital into safe havens (**gold +1.30%**), not supply disruption. The absence of geopolitical premium in nat gas confirms this is a growth-deceleration signal, not a Middle East event.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

US API crude inventory data (Friday, 0030 IST) will confirm whether the decline reflects actual drawdown or demand destruction; a build would accelerate MCX crude toward **₹6,400/bbl support**.
