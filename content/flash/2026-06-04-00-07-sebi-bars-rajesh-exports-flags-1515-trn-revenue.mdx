**WHAT HAPPENED**
SEBI has barred Rajesh Exports and its promoter from securities markets pending investigation into alleged revenue inflation totalling ₹15.15 trillion.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Rajesh Exports, one of India's largest gold refining and jewellery export companies, processes significant volumes of physical gold, and a regulatory freeze on its operations disrupts supply flows into the organised bullion chain at a time when MCX gold trades near ₹158,053/10g. Counterparty exposure for bullion banks and refiners supplying gold feedstock to Rajesh Exports now carries elevated settlement risk. The alleged revenue inflation at this scale signals potential fictitious trade cycles that artificially inflated volumes through the formal gold supply chain, putting reconciliation pressure on downstream buyers.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
MCX gold futures traders, bullion dealers and refiners supplying to Rajesh Exports, jewellery exporters competing in the same segment, gold ETF desks at HDFC AMC and Nippon India MF tracking NAV integrity, and institutional investors holding Rajesh Exports equity or bonds.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Gold refiners and bullion suppliers with outstanding receivables from Rajesh Exports face direct credit loss exposure on unrecovered inventory and payments. MCX Gold futures traders see a supply-chain credibility shock that reinforces the upside case for the April contract near ₹158,053/10g. Retail gold buyers in Zaveri Bazaar see no immediate spot price relief as physical supply disruption from a major refiner tightens near-term availability.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
SEBI's interim order detailing the investigation scope and any Emergency Director orders freezing Rajesh Exports' physical gold inventories will determine whether supply disruption widens into the broader MCX delivery chain.
