**WHAT HAPPENED**
The **United States** waived sanctions on Iranian oil exports while **Tehran** disputed International Atomic Energy Agency nuclear inspection claims, escalating diplomatic tensions without triggering immediate military action.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
The US sanctions waiver creates a paradoxical signal: it suggests de-escalation on the trade front, yet the inspection dispute keeps geopolitical risk premium embedded in crude markets. **HPCL and BPCL** refiners may access cheaper Iranian barrels if secondary sanctions ease further, but this window remains fragile pending IAEA resolution. **Gold dealers and jewellery fabricators** will monitor whether dollar strength weakens on reduced Iran conflict risk, which would compress rupee-denominated bullion valuations.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
**HPCL and BPCL procurement teams, Indian Oil Corporation, and Reliance's refining division** face whipsaw crude pricing as geopolitical risk oscillates between $75–$85/barrel for **Brent**, while **natural gas importers** (Petronet LNG, GAIL) hold hedging positions vulnerable to LNG price repricing. Petrol pump retailers across western coastal zones and LPG distribution networks absorb margin compression or expansion depending on landed crude-cost trajectories, while domestic aviation fuel hedgers (IndiGo, SpiceJet) recalibrate turbine fuel budgets.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Indian refinery margins face compression if **crude oil MCX futures** sustain sub-$80 levels following the waiver, eroding downstream fuel profitability for **HPCL and BPCL**. **MCX crude contracts** are now pivoting on IAEA-Iran talks; breach above **$82/barrel** signals escalation premium re-entry. **Petrol retail prices** will decline incrementally if **Brent settles below $80**, translating to pump-level cost relief in the next fortnight.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
**IAEA inspection update** (next 72 hours) and any **OPEC+ production adjustment announcement** will validate whether crude-friendly waiver sentiment persists or geopolitical risk re-inflates.
