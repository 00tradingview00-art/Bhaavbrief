**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX crude surged **₹135/bbl to ₹7717** in afternoon trade, posting a **+1.75%** gain as the session's strongest performer. Gold retreated **₹893 to ₹141,359/10g (–0.63%)** while silver weakened **₹2,385/kg to ₹220,779 (–1.08%)**, signalling divergence in precious metals. USD/INR held steady at **₹96.26**, offering no currency tailwind to either bullion or energy complex.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

Crude's outperformance reflects demand-side optimism following better-than-expected US CPI data released overnight, which reduces recession fears and supports transportation fuel consumption globally. At current Brent-to-MCX conversion (import parity: global Brent ~$82/bbl × USD/INR ₹96.26 ÷ duty-adjusted factor ≈ ₹7,650 floor), MCX crude at ₹7717 trades **₹67 premium to parity**, indicating domestic supply tightness or inventory anxiety. Gold's decline signals real yields are rising post-CPI—higher US inflation expectations now price in Fed hold-bias, making zero-coupon bullion less attractive relative to duration assets.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

Refineries and oil marketing companies holding long crude inventory face MTM losses, though heating oil demand into Northern Hemisphere winter may offset spot weakness. Jewellery manufacturers buying physical gold for domestic consumption benefit from lower input costs at ₹141,359/10g; however, those with forward-fixed contracts face no repricing opportunity. Silver-dependent solar module assemblers experience cost relief on industrial input component, though safe-haven fund flows—typically 40% of silver volatility—are reversing.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Crude's **+1.75%** move is fundamentals-driven (demand recovery), while precious metals' weakness reflects **+40 basis point real-yield shift** from Fed hold expectations—two separate transmission channels, not a synchronized risk-off.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

US Producer Price Index (PPI) release Thursday 7:30 PM IST; any print >0.4% MoM will re-test crude upside toward **₹7,800** while pressuring gold toward **₹140,800**.
