**WHAT HAPPENED**
Gold and silver rebounded on MCX after a three-day consecutive sell-off, with COMEX gold holding at $4,571/oz (₹1,60,948/10g MCX) and COMEX silver at $77.05/oz (₹2,59,506/kg MCX).

**MCX IMPACT**
The MCX Gold (June) and MCX Silver (July) contracts are the primary instruments affected by this snapback. The rebound in COMEX gold anchors the MCX price floor at ₹1,60,948/10g, with the USD/INR rate at ₹95.24 amplifying every dollar move in the underlying — a $10/oz COMEX move translates to approximately ₹2,990/10g on MCX before customs duty and GST adjustments. India's 15% customs duty on gold imports structurally widens the MCX-COMEX spread, meaning the domestic price floor is significantly elevated versus international spot and limits downside even during global corrections. For Indian traders, this spread compression during the three-day sell-off and its current reversal signals that arbitrage pressure between imported landed cost and MCX futures is normalising.

**WATCH**
The U.S. Federal Reserve's next policy communication and weekly COMEX Commitment of Traders (COT) report will confirm whether speculative long positioning is rebuilding after the sell-off or whether this rebound is a technical dead-cat bounce. ₹1,58,000/10g on MCX serves as the immediate structural support level to monitor.
