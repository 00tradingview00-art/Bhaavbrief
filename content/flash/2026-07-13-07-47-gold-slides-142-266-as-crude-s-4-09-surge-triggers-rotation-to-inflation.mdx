**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX Gold declined **0.84%** to **₹142,266/10g** in this morning session, tracking a broader pullback in precious metals as crude oil surged **4.09%** to dominate intraday flows. Silver fell **1.44%** alongside gold, signalling a coordinated liquidation in the bullion complex rather than selective weakness.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
The gold decline reflects a real-yield headwind — as crude strength signals persistent inflation expectations, market participants are reducing safe-haven positioning in favour of energy exposure. Gold's losses occurred despite USD/INR stability at **₹95.74**, ruling out currency-driven export competitiveness gains; the move is purely demand-driven from domestic MCX participants rotating capital toward crude. Historically, gold exhibits inverse correlation to oil during demand-driven (not geopolitical) rallies, and today's crude advance on fundamental demand signals appears to be extracting that premium.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
A jewellery manufacturer with forward export commitments faces margin compression if they locked rupee-hedged gold prices above current levels last week. An asset manager holding gold as an inflation hedge against elevated real rates now confronts a tactical reallocation decision as the crude rally signals inflationary persistence rather than deflationary crisis. A domestic gold importer planning fresh shipments will encounter a **₹1,900–2,000/10g** window (from current levels) before triggering fresh import parity triggers at current USD/INR.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Gold's **0.84%** decline amid crude's **4.09%** surge shows rotation away from crisis hedges toward inflation-play commodities — not a technical breakdown. The signal is relative, not absolute: rupee stability and gold's stable technical support suggest demand destruction, not panic selling.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
USD/INR movement beyond **₹96.10** would reverse gold's export competitiveness case; crude holds need to sustain above **₹7,150/bbl** to confirm demand narrative over geopolitical speculation.
