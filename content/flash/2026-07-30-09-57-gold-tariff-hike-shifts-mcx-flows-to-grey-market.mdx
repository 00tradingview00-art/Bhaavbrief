**WHAT HAPPENED**
India raised import duties on gold, increasing the landed cost of foreign bullion entering organised channels, as reported by the World Gold Council.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Higher tariffs create a cost arbitrage: **grey market dealers** importing gold through informal channels now undercut organised refiners and bullion traders on price, eroding their working margin on every kilogram processed or sold. **MCX gold futures traders** using physical delivery settlement or hedging against spot purchases now face a wider spread between grey-market spot rates and exchange-quoted futures, making traditional hedge ratios unviable for refiners locking in forward sales.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Gold refiners and hallmarking units sourcing unrefined bullion through organised imports see their input cost jump, while grey-market suppliers offering cheaper material to jewellery manufacturers create a two-tier procurement market. Mid-chain jewellery manufacturers and retail ornament makers sourcing gold on spot or forward contracts now face a choice: buy tariff-laden organised gold at higher cost or source grey-market material and accept regulatory and purity risk, effectively squeezing their ability to hold inventory at fixed prices. Retail jewellery buyers purchasing certified, hallmarked ornaments from organised jewellers face upward price pressure on making charges and base metal costs, since jewellers pass through the tariff-inflated raw material cost, while consumers buying from grey-market dealers absorb undisclosed quality risk.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Organised bullion dealers see their wholesale-to-retail margin compress as grey-market competition undercuts tariff-inclusive pricing. **MCX gold futures** will likely trade at a premium to physical spot, signalling hedging friction for refiners. Retail jewellery buyers purchasing hallmarked pieces will face higher prices faster than those accepting grey-market sourcing risks.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Monitor the next import duty gazette notification or tariff review scheduled by the Ministry of Finance; any reduction or exemption will reverse grey-market incentives. Track WGC or IBJA statements on organised sector volume losses, which trigger supply-chain responses from major refiners.
