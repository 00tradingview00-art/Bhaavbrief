**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX gold and silver contracts each gained 3% while silver ETFs declined as much as 6%, creating a sharp divergence between the futures and fund markets.

**MCX IMPACT**
MCX Gold (June) and MCX Silver (July) are the primary contracts affected by this divergence. The 3% futures gain on MCX Silver aligns with spot COMEX silver at $76.20/oz, translating to an import-parity price of approximately ₹257,896/kg — a level where physical demand and arbitrage calculations become critical for Indian traders. The ETF underperformance signals that institutional and retail fund flows are not chasing the futures rally, creating a structural disconnect: futures premiums over ETF NAVs widen when delivery and roll costs are factored in. For Indian traders, the customs duty component (currently 6% on silver imports plus GST) means the landed cost remains significantly above COMEX-derived theoretical prices, sustaining the MCX premium even as ETF redemption pressure builds.

**WATCH**
Monitor MCX Silver July contract's ability to hold the ₹257,896/kg level on any intraday retracement — a breach signals futures correcting toward ETF-implied fair value. The next SEBI disclosure on silver ETF AUM flows and the upcoming COMEX silver options expiry will confirm whether fund outflows accelerate or stabilise.
