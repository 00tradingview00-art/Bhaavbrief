**WHAT HAPPENED**
The **Indian rupee** depreciated **7 paise** to close at **₹95.83** against the US dollar on Thursday, driven by escalating **US-Iran geopolitical tensions** and aggressive safe-haven demand.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
A weaker rupee at **₹95.83** versus the live rate of **₹95.71** confirms the directional pressure on INR-denominated import costs across energy and metals. **WTI crude at $92.17/bbl** translates to approximately **₹8,998/bbl** on **MCX Crude**, and any further rupee weakness mechanically lifts that landed cost without any move in dollar-denominated oil. Simultaneously, **MCX Gold** priced at **~₹1,59,722/10g** and **MCX Silver** at **~₹2,51,157/kg** see rupee-denominated valuations rise as INR softens against the dollar.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
**HPCL** and **BPCL** crude import desks, **MCX Crude** and **MCX Gold** futures traders, bullion dealers in **Zaveri Bazaar**, **Indian Oil Corporation** refinery procurement teams, and airlines including **IndiGo** and **Air India** managing aviation turbine fuel hedges.

**BOTTOM LINE**
**Indian Oil Corporation** and downstream refiners absorb a higher per-barrel rupee cost on every crude cargo cleared at current **₹95.83** levels. **MCX Crude futures** carry an upward bias as the rupee-dollar spread widens on sustained geopolitical risk. Retail **petrol and diesel** prices face upward pressure at the pump if the rupee holds below **₹95.50**.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
The next **RBI monetary policy statement** and any **US-Iran diplomatic development** that shifts WTI below **$88/bbl** will determine whether this INR pressure reverses or deepens.
