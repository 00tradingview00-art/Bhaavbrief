**WHAT HAPPENED**
Silver ETFs listed on Indian exchanges have crashed up to 24% while MCX Silver futures declined only 4%, creating a sharp 20-percentage-point divergence between the two instruments tracking the same underlying commodity.

**MCX IMPACT**
MCX Silver (current near ₹257,896/kg) is the directly affected contract, with the ETF-to-futures spread blowing out due to structural differences in how each instrument prices the metal. Silver ETFs embed NAV-based pricing with redemption restrictions and expense ratios, whereas MCX futures reflect real-time spot discovery — when ETF outflows accelerate, NAV dislocates sharply from underlying futures. At ₹257,896/kg on MCX versus the COMEX equivalent of $76.20/oz, Indian import costs remain elevated with customs duty and USD/INR at ₹95.70 widening the landed cost further, meaning physical arbitrage to compress the spread is structurally limited. Traders holding ETF positions face a larger mark-to-market loss than those in MCX futures despite identical commodity exposure — a critical distinction for portfolio risk management.

**WATCH**
Monitor the MCX Silver June contract at the ₹252,000/kg support level; a breach confirms sustained selling pressure beyond ETF-specific mechanics. SEBI's next ETF redemption data disclosure and COMEX silver open interest changes at the weekly COT report release will either validate or negate further ETF-futures spread widening.
