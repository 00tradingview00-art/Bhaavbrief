**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX Copper rose **₹1316/kg (+0.31%)** in the afternoon session, holding ground while crude surged **+1.27%** and most other base metals stalled. LME copper benchmarks have hit record highs on supply disruption fears in aluminium, creating a spillover bid into copper despite flat industrial demand signals from Asia overnight.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
LME copper strength transmits to MCX through import parity pricing: global copper futures (~$10,400/tonne equivalent) × USD/INR **₹96.25** × duty and logistics costs converge to MCX spot levels around **₹1316/kg**. The aluminium supply crisis (smelting capacity constraints in major producing regions) is rotating safe hedging demand into copper as a perceived safer base metal, lifting the entire LME complex independent of near-term consumption data.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Electrical equipment manufacturers sourcing 50-500 tonne monthly copper rod commitments must decide whether to forward-hedge at current **₹1316/kg** or await clarity on whether this LME-driven premium sustains into August. Cable producers locking in July-August procurement contracts now face a choice between spot purchases at record parity levels or rolling hedges if they expect the aluminium supply premium to deflate.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Copper is rising on structural LME supply fears, not Indian industrial demand—the **+0.31%** gain contradicts the muted session tone and reveals external portfolio rotation rather than domestic cable/electrical offtake acceleration.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
LME aluminium smelter production data (Thursday) and any price break below **₹1310/kg** would signal whether today's gain was portfolio-driven or demand-anchored.
