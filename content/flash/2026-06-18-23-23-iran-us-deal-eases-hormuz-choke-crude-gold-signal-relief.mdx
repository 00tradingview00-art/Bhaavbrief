**WHAT HAPPENED**
Oil tankers resumed transit through the **Strait of Hormuz** following an interim agreement between Iran and the United States, reducing immediate geopolitical supply disruption risk in the world's most critical petroleum chokepoint.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
The deal mechanically lowers tail-risk premiums embedded in **crude oil futures** by removing acute supply-cut scenarios that refiners price into import contracts. **HPCL and BPCL's** crude procurement desks had been pricing a geopolitical risk buffer into every **WTI-linked** cargo; that buffer now compresses. Simultaneously, **gold futures** shed their safe-haven bid as dollar-denominated asset flows normalize, putting pressure on the **INR/USD cross-rate** that anchors **MCX gold contracts**.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
**HPCL, BPCL, and Reliance Industries'** refining desks face margin recovery as crude import costs normalize from geopolitical premiums, while **Cairn India's** upstream division gains from reduced volatility in hedging its export-grade crude sales. Aviation fuel hedgers at **IndiGo and Air India**, LPG distributors, and petrol pump operators along western ports absorb lower landed costs across jet fuel, cooking gas, and retail motor spirit channels.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Downstream refiners lock in inventory cost relief as **crude import premiums compress**, directly expanding gross refining margins on every barrel processed. **MCX Crude Oil** trades signal downside pressure if the contract breaches the **$75/barrel threshold**, with Brent-WTI arbitrage tightening further. **Retail petrol consumers** in coastal metros face modest price-pump relief over the next 2–3 weeks as terminals clear elevated-cost inventories.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Monitor **OPEC production data** due next week and any Iran sanctions enforcement statement from Washington—both will either confirm supply normalization or reignite geopolitical premiums embedded in spot **MCX Crude** and **Natural Gas** contracts.
