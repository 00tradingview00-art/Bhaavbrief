**WHAT HAPPENED**
The Reserve Bank of India signalled an extended pause on rate cuts due to widening inflation risks, keeping the policy rate on hold and signalling that monetary easing is off the table for the near term.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
A frozen RBI rate path keeps Indian real yields elevated, raising the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets like **MCX Gold** and **MCX Silver** for domestic investors and institutions — however, this same stance weakens the rupee against the dollar as foreign capital becomes more attractive abroad, which immediately raises rupee-denominated import costs for refiners, jewellers, and bullion dealers sourcing overseas metal. **MCX Crude** and **MCX Copper** face dual pressure: the stronger dollar makes dollar-priced commodities costlier for Indian importers, while the hawkish hold signals persistent inflation and delayed demand recovery, capping upside for both energy and industrial metals.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Bullion dealers and small refiners hedging dollar-denominated import contracts see their rupee costs spike immediately, forcing spot procurement decisions to shift upwards. Jewellery manufacturers and D2C brands carrying inventory in rupee terms face margin compression if they've pre-priced finished goods before this rupee move. Downstream, retail jewellery buyers and household gold-scheme subscribers encounter higher entry prices at jewellers' counters, while industrial users of copper — wire manufacturers, electrical equipment makers, construction contractors — absorb higher input costs that ripple into project quotations and equipment pricing.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Bullion importers and refiners experience immediate rupee hedging costs as the currency weakens on rate-hold expectations. **MCX Gold** and **MCX Silver** face competing pressures: rupee weakness supports prices but higher real yields suppress demand, creating volatility around the 70,000–72,000 and 88,000–90,000 zones respectively. Retail jewellery prices and household gold purchase power decline in absolute rupee terms as import costs feed through to retail counters within 2–3 weeks.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Monitor the next RBI MPC communication and US CPI release for any signals of inflation cooling that might permit rate cuts; track USD/INR fixing for further rupee depreciation momentum.
