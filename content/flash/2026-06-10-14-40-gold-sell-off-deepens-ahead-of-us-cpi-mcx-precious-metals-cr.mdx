**WHAT HAPPENED**
Markets are pricing in a **hawkish CPI print** expected to show sticky US inflation, potentially keeping Federal Reserve rate-cut expectations on hold or pushing them further into 2025.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
A stronger-than-expected CPI reading will strengthen the **US dollar index**, raising rupee-denominated import costs for jewellers and bullion dealers at Zaveri Bazaar and corporate hedgers. Higher real US yields reduce the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold for global ETF funds and central banks including RBI, pressuring **MCX Gold** and **MCX Silver**. Energy traders face dollar strength headwinds that could cap upside in **MCX Crude**, while Chinese construction demand uncertainty—tied to US monetary policy spillovers—weighs on **MCX Copper** as fabricators delay procurement decisions.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Jewellery exporters in Surat, Mumbai's precious metals refineries, and corporate treasury teams hedging USD payables see immediate repricing of rupee conversion costs on dollar-denominated bullion purchases, while copper cable and wire manufacturers in Pune face COMEX-linked procurement cost volatility. Retail gold buyers timing festival-season purchases and petrol pump operators managing crude-hedged inventory see input costs shift directionally.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Jewellery retailers dependent on imported bullion margins face compressed spreads if the dollar strengthens post-CPI, eroding working capital efficiency. **MCX Gold** is under pressure to test lower support zones as dollar strength and elevated real yields converge. Festival-season retail gold demand may soften if jewellery retail prices rise faster than consumer purchasing power.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
US CPI data release timing and Fed Chair Powell's subsequent communication on rate trajectory. China PMI and yuan strength will signal whether copper demand recovers or deteriorates further.
