**WHAT HAPPENED**
The **World Bank projects commodity prices will rise 23% in 2026** driven by Middle East conflict, supply chain disruptions, and energy market tightness, reversing earlier deflationary expectations.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Geopolitical risk premiums are embedding into energy and industrial metals as producers face production uncertainty and logistics bottlenecks in critical trade routes. This transmission directly reprices **MCX Crude Oil** upward through tighter global supply, while **MCX Copper** and **MCX Gold** rise as safe-haven demand and inflation hedging intensify among central banks and institutional allocators including the **RBI's reserves strategy**. Crude's repricing raises the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets, but paradoxically strengthens gold and copper as dollar-denominated commodity exporters — including Indian refiners and metal processors — face margin compression from input costs rising faster than their export realizations.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Refinery operators and crude importers on forward contracts face immediate input cost acceleration, while petrochemical manufacturers downstream absorb higher feedstock prices into polyester, fertilizer, and polymer production. Copper rod and wire manufacturers supplying power distribution and renewable energy projects see procurement costs spike, forcing them to renegotiate contracts with utilities and solar developers. Jewellery retailers and bullion dealers holding gold inventory experience positive revaluation on physical stock but face consumer demand softening as retail gold prices — driven by **MCX Gold** futures** — rise steeply; households delaying festival purchases and wedding-season gold buying cascade into lower offtake from hallmarking units and retail chains.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Indian oil refiners locked into margin-based feedstock arrangements will see working capital pressures intensify as crude revaluation accelerates faster than downstream product pricing adjusts. **MCX Crude Oil** signals sustained backwardation and elevated $80–95 trading bands, anchoring geopolitical risk into Indian import costs for the next 12 months. Indian households purchasing gold jewellery and investors in physical bars face retail premiums widening ahead of the 2026 spike, compressing discretionary buying among middle-income segments.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Monitor **OPEC+ production decisions in January 2026** and US shale production capacity data for reversal signals; track **RBI inflation and import cost commentary** in the February monetary policy review to assess rupee defence and commodity hedging intensity.
