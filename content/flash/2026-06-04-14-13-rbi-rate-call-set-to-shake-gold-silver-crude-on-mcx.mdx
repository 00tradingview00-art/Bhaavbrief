**WHAT HAPPENED**
The **RBI Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** in June 2026 will announce its decision on the **repo rate**, influencing rupee strength and domestic borrowing costs across commodity markets.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
A **repo rate cut** strengthens rupee carry trades and reduces hedging costs for **MCX gold and silver futures traders**, making overseas bullion cheaper in rupee terms and pressuring local contract prices. Conversely, a **rate hike** tightens liquidity for **crude oil importers and refiners**, who rely on short-term working capital financing—raising their effective landed costs on **Brent-linked crudes** traded on **MCX Crude Oil futures**. Non-resident portfolio flows into Indian equities also shift with rate signals, indirectly affecting rupee volatility that directly impacts commodity contract valuations.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
**Gold jewellery exporters in Surat and Mumbai** managing inventory hedges on **MCX Gold futures**, **silver bullion dealers in Delhi and Bangalore** taking overnight positions, **refineries in Gujarat and Maharashtra** locking in crude costs, and **retail gold buyers ahead of monsoon weddings and festivals** timing their purchases against expected rupee moves.

**BOTTOM LINE**
**Jewellery manufacturers and refineries**: Rate cuts lower hedging costs but compress margins on forward contracts already locked; rate hikes do the reverse. **MCX traders**: **Gold and Silver futures** typically rally on rate cuts (weaker rupee), while **Crude Oil futures** decline as refiners defer purchases during tighter credit. **Consumers**: Retail gold prices in jewellery shops track rupee strength—rate hikes may delay festive-season purchases.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
The **RBI MPC decision date (June 7, 2026)** and the accompanying **monetary policy statement on inflation targets**. Watch for any **forward guidance on subsequent reviews** through December 2026, which will signal sustained tightening or easing cycles.
