**WHAT HAPPENED**
The United States and Iran exchanged military threats on crude oil supply routes, extending a seven-month conflict and pushing **WTI and Brent futures higher** on supply disruption fears.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Refinery procurement desks importing crude into India now face higher rupee-denominated landed costs per barrel as **MCX Crude Oil** reprices upward on geopolitical risk premium. Gold traders and bullion dealers holding **dollar-denominated inventory see rupee values rise** as safe-haven demand lifts precious metals, while natural gas spot traders linked to Middle Eastern supply corridors brace for volatility in **MCX Natural Gas** as traders price in potential logistics delays.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Oil refineries and petroleum product manufacturers sourcing feedstock on monthly import contracts absorb immediate cost pressures, narrowing refinery margins on diesel and petrol that they price into the fuel pump network. Downstream petro-chemical processors, packaging film manufacturers, and lubricant blenders sourcing naphtha and base oils watch procurement costs tick higher, forcing them to defer non-essential inventory builds or compress working capital. Retail fuel pumps, transport fleets operating on diesel hedges, and households consuming cooking gas cylinders will face upward price revision timelines in line with global crude trends—independent of domestic policy moves.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Oil refinery margins compress as **MCX Crude Oil contract spot premiums widen**, directly reducing per-unit profitability on fuel sales. **MCX Natural Gas futures signal elevated volatility near $250–$280/mmBtu thresholds**, reflecting geopolitical hedging by power and fertiliser plants. Retail diesel and petrol prices will track upward within **7–14 days** as OMCs rebalance hedges against elevated import costs.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Monitor **OPEC+ production statements and U.S.–Iran diplomatic signals over the next 48 hours**; any de-escalation language will trigger a sharp reversal, while shipping incident reports from the Strait of Hormuz will confirm supply-side fear premiums.
