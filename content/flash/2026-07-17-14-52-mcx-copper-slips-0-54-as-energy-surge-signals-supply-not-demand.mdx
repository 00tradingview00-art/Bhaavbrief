**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX Copper declined **0.54%** to **₹1,301.45/kg** in afternoon trade, underperforming a broader LME base metals rally that pushed the index to record highs. The energy complex surged simultaneously, with crude oil advancing **2.82%** and natural gas rising **1.56%**, signalling divergent demand signals across commodities. Copper's weakness emerged despite industrial-demand tailwinds lifting aluminium and other base metals on the London exchange.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
The copper-energy divergence points to a demand composition shift rather than macro weakness: crude and nat gas gains reflect supply-side concerns (geopolitical or production disruptions), while copper's lag suggests softer near-term manufacturing activity despite record LME strength. Global copper prices × USD/INR at **₹96.27** × import duties determine MCX copper's import parity floor; today's **0.54%** drop indicates either weaker London spot prices or real domestic hedging supply overwhelming risk-on positioning. The divergence matters because energy premiums typically compress when industrial demand strengthens.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Copper wire and cable manufacturers importing refined cathode at import parity face margin compression if they have forward sales locked at last week's higher rupee-adjusted prices. Domestic copper fabricators using MCX futures for inventory hedging must decide whether to roll expiring positions into a weaker nearby contract or wait for supply-driven volatility to present better entry points. An auto-parts supplier with quarterly copper consumption contracts will monitor whether this weakness persists through month-end before locking hedges.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Copper's **0.54%** decline contradicts the LME record high, signalling industrial demand divergence from energy-driven rallies—a structural warning that supply concerns, not consumption recovery, are anchoring the broader metals complex.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
London copper settlement tonight and MCX copper's response at **₹1,300** support; also track if energy premiums sustain into close—contraction would confirm demand weakness.
