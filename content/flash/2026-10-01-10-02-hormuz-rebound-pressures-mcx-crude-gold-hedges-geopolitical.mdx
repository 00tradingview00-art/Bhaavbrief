**WHAT HAPPENED**
Middle East crude oil exports have rebounded toward pre-war levels through the Hormuz Strait, signalling stabilised regional supply and reduced transit disruption risk.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Refineries and oil import procurement desks calibrating rupee-denominated barrel costs will face downward pressure on **MCX Crude** as global supply tightens the Brent-WTI-Dubai spread, translating directly into lower landed costs per unit cleared through Indian ports. Simultaneously, geopolitical de-escalation reduces the safe-haven premium embedded in **MCX Gold**, as traders liquidate hedge positions built during elevated regional tension, creating a margin compression for bullion dealers holding long inventory on the expectation of sustained risk premiums.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Petroleum refineries and bulk crude importers locked into forward purchase commitments will see their per-barrel procurement costs ease in INR terms as global oil price benchmarks reflect normalised Hormuz throughput, freeing up working capital previously reserved for elevated crude hedging. Down the supply chain, fuel retailers and logistics operators sourcing diesel and petrol wholesale will experience inventory repricings aligned to this crude softness, allowing a modest margin recovery on pump prices if upstream costs decline faster than retail price adjustments lag. Household consumers purchasing petrol, diesel, and cooking gas will encounter pricing adjustments—either through direct fuel bill reductions or slower pass-through of earlier hedging costs—while jewellery retailers and gold jewellers face inventory valuation pressure as spot **MCX Gold** corrects lower, squeezing margins on finished pieces catalogued at yesterday's premium levels.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Petroleum retailers and fuel distributors will see wholesale crude costs ease, improving gross margins on per-litre fuel sales. **MCX Crude** is signalling a test of lower support zones as Hormuz risk premiums unwind. Household fuel and cooking gas bills will drift downward over the next billing cycle as refiners pass through lower input costs.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Monitor OPEC production guidance at the next scheduled meeting and watch for any further Hormuz Strait transit flow data confirming sustained normalcy above pre-war volumes.
