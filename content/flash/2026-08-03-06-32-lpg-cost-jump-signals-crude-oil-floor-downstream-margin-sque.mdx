**WHAT HAPPENED**
Commercial LPG cylinder prices increased by **Rs 195** across metropolitan cities, reflecting cost pressures from geopolitical tensions in West Asia driving upstream **crude oil** valuations higher.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
LPG retailers and bulk suppliers sourcing cylinders from oil marketing companies face immediate margin compression on fixed retail price points, since their acquisition cost has risen while consumer-facing tariffs remain administratively set. Hospitality operators, commercial kitchens, and small food processing units relying on LPG as a primary fuel input now face elevated energy bills that cannot be immediately passed through to end consumers without renegotiating contracts. **Natural gas** producers and distributors see crude linkages tightening, signalling potential upstream cost recovery attempts in the next pricing window.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Hotel chains and restaurant operators running commercial kitchens absorb this LPG hike as a direct operational cost; those on fixed fuel supply contracts face margin erosion until renegotiation cycles. Packaged food manufacturers using LPG for steam generation and sterilization see input costs rise mid-quarter, compressing EBITDA until they adjust product pricing or sourcing strategies. Last-mile logistics operators and cold-chain handlers dependent on LPG-powered forklifts and refrigeration units face elevated fleet operating costs. Household consumers purchasing refills in non-metro regions, though currently insulated by cross-subsidies, face eventual price normalization once budget allocations adjust.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Quick-service restaurant chains face tighter unit economics on meal costs without immediate pricing flexibility. **MCX Crude Oil futures** holding above **$80/bbl** signals sustained West Asia premium, while **Natural Gas contracts** track downstream fuel-cost propagation. Retail food prices in metros edge higher as commercial kitchen operators eventually recoup input inflation through menu adjustments.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Next OMC price review cycle announcement and any de-escalation signals from West Asia conflict zones affecting crude premiums.
