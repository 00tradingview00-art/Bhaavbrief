**TITLE:** Iran Conflict Threatens MCX Crude, Gold Rally; Hormuz Strait Risk Spikes

**WHAT HAPPENED**
Escalating geopolitical tensions between Iran, Israel, and the United States have triggered conflict concerns centred on the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint through which approximately 21% of global petroleum passes daily.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Indian refinery procurement desks importing **WTI-linked crude** face immediate upward pressure on rupee-denominated landed costs, while geopolitical risk premiums embed into **Brent crude benchmarks** that MCX **Crude Oil futures** track. Gold dealers and accumulation-phase bullion buyers sourcing dollar-priced **spot gold** see inventory rupee valuations rise as safe-haven demand typically strengthens during conflict escalation, directly affecting margin calculations on **MCX Gold futures** positions.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Domestic refiners and oil marketing companies executing monthly term contracts now face renegotiation pressure as **crude import bills swell**, compressing operating margins on fuel retail. Fertilizer manufacturers and power generators dependent on natural gas as feedstock see **input cost volatility** spike, immediately affecting production economics for urea makers and thermal power operators. Downstream petroleum retailers, logistics operators purchasing diesel in bulk, and transport fleets experience pump price volatility that directly feeds into freight costs and final-mile delivery expenses across e-commerce, FMCG distribution, and cold-chain networks. Households purchasing petrol, diesel, cooking gas, and gold jewellery face potential price repricing at retail counters, while inflation pressures on transportation ripple through food prices and packaged goods.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Refinery and shipping companies operating on imported crude face immediate working-capital strain as freight costs and insurance premiums rise with Strait of Hormuz transit risk. **MCX Crude Oil** contracts have historically breached **$90–95/barrel** thresholds during prior Middle East supply disruptions, a level that would trigger refinery margin compression. Retail petrol and diesel prices, along with household cooking gas tariffs, will repriced within 48–72 hours if geopolitical premiums sustain above $3–4/barrel on international benchmarks.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Monitor official OPEC+ production guidance and **Strait of Hormuz shipping traffic data** over the next 72 hours for confirmation of actual supply disruption versus sentiment-driven premium alone. Track **US sanctions announcements** on Iranian oil exports, which would represent the next concrete supply shock trigger.
