**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX crude surged **₹7,775/bbl (+2.11%)** in the afternoon session, while natural gas climbed **1.24%** to **₹278.30/mmBtu** — both energy contracts moving in tandem. Gold slipped **0.03%** to hover near **₹1,60,600/10g**, weighed by steady USD/INR at **₹96.27**, while copper fell **1.00%** to **₹1,295.35/kg** in a divergence from the energy rally.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
The energy complex surge reflects a geopolitical or supply-side tightening rather than broad commodity demand — crude's **2.11%** gain outpaces natural gas's **1.24%** move, suggesting the crude premium is supply-specific, not demand-driven. Copper's **1.00%** decline contradicts this narrative entirely, signalling weakening industrial demand expectations despite higher energy input costs; this divergence indicates traders are pricing manufacturing slowdown risk rather than broad inflation.

Gold's stagnation despite elevated USD/INR reflects competing anchors: the rupee's strength at **₹96.27** supports domestic imports, offsetting traditional safe-haven demand that would otherwise lift the ₹1,60,600 level.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
An oil marketing company's refineries face higher crude procurement costs, pressuring gross margins on diesel and petrol retail pricing decisions. A copper-consuming cable or automotive component producer faces input-cost relief from the **₹1,295.35** weakness, but must contend with demand signals embedded in copper's **1.00%** decline — suggesting order books may be contracting. A jewellery exporter locks MCX hedges near **₹1,60,600/10g**, accepting lower protection as rupee strength compresses export realisations.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Energy and metals are pricing two different stories today: crude and gas embed a **2.11%** supply premium, while copper's **1.00%** slide signals demand contraction despite higher input costs — a divergence that suggests stagflationary risk, not synchronized commodity rally.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Crude's hold above **₹7,700/bbl** into session close validates supply-side tightness; a close below **₹7,650** would suggest the rally lacks conviction. Monitor copper's close relative to **₹1,290/kg** threshold — break below signals sustained demand pessimism.
