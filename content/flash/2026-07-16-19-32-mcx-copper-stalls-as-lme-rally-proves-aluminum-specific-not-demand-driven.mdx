**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Copper fell **₹1308/kg** (down **0.29%**) in today's session, lagging a broader rally in global base metals. The LME base metals index hit record highs on the back of aluminum supply concerns, but copper's subdued performance suggests the rally is being driven by specific supply disruptions rather than broad industrial demand recovery. Natural gas led the decline across MCX commodities at **-2.79%**, signaling energy weakness is not translating into copper support.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

Copper's muted decline despite LME strength indicates the global rally is aluminum-specific—a supply-side story tied to smelter outages—rather than a demand-driven copper story. When LME base metals index rises on aluminum fears while copper stalls, it signals traders are pricing **supply scarcity** for one metal but not expecting broad industrial acceleration that would lift copper. For MCX copper, this divergence means tomorrow's open will depend on whether USD/INR strength (**₹96.34** today) offsets any spillover from global base metals strength, as import parity pricing ties MCX copper to both LME levels and rupee weakness simultaneously.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

A copper rod manufacturer importing refined copper to serve domestic power cable demand faces hedging uncertainty: global prices support higher MCX levels, but weak industrial signals (natgas selling) suggest end-user off-take may remain subdued. A building-wire producer deciding on forward-purchase timing cannot rely on global strength as a demand signal when aluminum's supply shock is what's lifting the LME index.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Copper is diverging from its own sector narrative—base metals index at record highs (**LME +**), but MCX copper flat (**-0.29%**)—revealing this is aluminum supply dislocation, not copper demand awakening.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

Tomorrow's MCX open for a break of **₹1305/kg support**; any LME copper specific commentary (smelter news, China demand data) will override the aluminum supply story.
