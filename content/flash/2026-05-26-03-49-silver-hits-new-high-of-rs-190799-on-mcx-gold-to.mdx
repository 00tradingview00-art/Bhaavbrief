**WHAT HAPPENED**
Silver on MCX hit a fresh record high of ₹1,90,799/kg, while gold simultaneously surged to new highs across Indian markets.

**MCX IMPACT**
MCX Silver (near-month contract) and MCX Gold are both directly affected. The silver print of ₹1,90,799/kg represents a significant premium over the live COMEX-derived theoretical rate of ₹2,64,558/kg — indicating the article's reported figure reflects an earlier session or spot market rate, while COMEX silver at $78.55/oz translates to a materially higher MCX ceiling, giving the domestic contract further upside room before hitting import parity. At USD/INR of ₹95.24, every 1-rupee depreciation in the rupee mechanically adds approximately ₹785/kg to silver's landed import cost, compressing the arbitrage window for Indian bullion importers. MCX Gold's theoretical import parity sits near ₹1,62,302/10g including current customs duty structure of 6% plus AIDC, meaning any further rupee weakness directly widens the COMEX-MCX spread and raises hedging costs for jewelry trade participants.

**WATCH**
Monitor the next RBI USD/INR fixing and COMEX silver's ability to hold $78/oz support — a breach below that level negates the domestic record-high momentum, while a sustained rupee above ₹95.50 confirms continued import cost pressure on both contracts.
