**WHAT HAPPENED**
US nonfarm payrolls exceeded expectations, pushing the **Dollar Index** above 104 and **10-year Treasury yields** to multi-week highs, inverting the risk-off sentiment that typically supports precious metals.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
A stronger dollar raises the rupee-denominated cost of dollar-priced bullion for jewellers and refineries importing MCX Gold and MCX Silver, while elevated US real yields increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding precious metals for global ETF funds and central banks including the RBI. Simultaneously, the stronger dollar and higher bond yields compress speculative positioning in MCX Crude and MCX Copper, as a robust labour market signals sustained growth but also faster Fed rate-hold expectations. MCX Gold and MCX Silver face immediate liquidation from FII-linked ETF redemptions, while MCX Crude and MCX Copper weaken on demand concerns from rate-sensitive manufacturing sectors in China and India.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Bullion dealers and jewellery manufacturers in Jaipur and Surat see immediate repricing of dollar-linked hedging costs, while refinery procurement desks hedging crude exposure face margin pressure on long positions and reduced arbitrage spreads between NYMEX and MCX Crude contracts. Retail festival-season gold buyers nationwide and petrol pump operators absorb higher rupee-denominated costs for both bullion and fuel, while copper cable fabricators in Pune see import parity costs rising in tandem with COMEX weakness.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Bullion dealers face compressed margins as rupee-denominated import costs for refined gold rise faster than domestic spot prices adjust. **MCX Gold** signals a test of the 75,500–75,800 support band, with extended weakness if US inflation data sustains the yield backdrop. Retail gold ornament prices and petrol pump rates move directionally higher over the next 1–2 trading sessions.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
US CPI release next week and any Fed speaker commentary on terminal rates will determine whether the dollar-yield complex stabilizes or extends higher, resetting the repricing floor for all four MCX contracts.
