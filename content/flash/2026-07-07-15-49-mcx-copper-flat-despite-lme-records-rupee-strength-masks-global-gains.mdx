**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Copper declined **₹1,279/kg**, a **−0.66%** loss in the afternoon session, while crude surged **+2.27%** to **₹6,700/bbl**—creating a rare divergence where energy rallied but base metals retreated. Silver fell **−2.23%** to **₹230,823/kg**, extending its weakness from earlier in the week. The LME base metals index touched record highs on Friday, yet Indian copper futures have not replicated that strength, signalling a local pricing disconnect.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

Global copper strength (LME at record levels) has not translated into equivalent MCX appreciation, suggesting either that USD/INR strength at **₹94.96** is offsetting London gains, or that Indian import-parity arithmetic—calculated as LME price (USD/tonne) × **₹94.96** exchange rate + duties—is anchoring MCX below what the headline LME record would imply. This means domestic fabricators face no immediate cost inflection despite the international recovery signal.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

A cable manufacturer importing refined copper cathodes must reconcile LME record highs against flat-to-negative MCX pricing when locking rupee-denominated procurement contracts. An automotive wiring harness producer hedging three-month forward copper exposure sees no margin expansion today despite global copper's supply-squeeze narrative.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Copper's **−0.66%** loss contradicts the LME record-high backdrop—the industrial demand lens shows no confirmation from Indian futures, indicating either exchange-rate dampening or pricing arbitrage at work rather than demand weakness.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

USD/INR movement through **₹95.00** into close, and whether MCX copper recaptures **₹1,285/kg** resistance tomorrow morning to validate LME strength locally.
