**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX precious metals surged in Friday afternoon trade with silver climbing **+1.73%** to ₹233,200/kg and gold advancing **+1.26%** to ₹147,590/10g, tracking a broad dollar weakness narrative across commodities. Copper extended gains to **+0.81%** at ₹1,285.35/kg, while natural gas added **+1.18%** to ₹309.60/mmBtu as India's acute heatwave pushed electricity demand to record peaks, intensifying power generation fuel demand.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

The precious metals rally reflects real-yield compression as USD/INR weakened to **₹95.24**, reducing import parity costs for rupee-denominated bullion and aligning with global reflation positioning. Silver's outperformance over gold suggests dual support: safe-haven demand alongside industrial bidding from solar-panel manufacturers requiring polysilicon feedstock manufacturing inputs. Natural gas's smaller but consistent gain reflects India's electricity crisis tightening the fuel margin — peak demand days compress supply elasticity, pushing gas-fired generation economics upward independently of geopolitical premiums.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

A jewellery manufacturer with forward rupee-denominated gold commitments benefits from lower effective import parity, though may face customer price-resistance if retail markup assumptions lag the rally. A solar-panel manufacturer managing silver-paste procurement for cell contacts faces elevated input costs during a period of government subsidy-driven capex acceleration. A power-distribution utility relying on thermal-gas generation extends operating margins when peak-demand capacity constraints push gas-fired stations into economic merit order.

**BOTTOM LINE**

The afternoon session's commodity breadth — gold, silver, copper all advancing simultaneously — reflects dollar structural weakness rather than India-specific demand confirmation: only natural gas shows price movement correlated to documented domestic electricity stress.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

Grid frequency data through Saturday evening and next week's meteorological heatwave extension forecasts confirm whether natural gas premium sustains post-peak-demand cycle.
