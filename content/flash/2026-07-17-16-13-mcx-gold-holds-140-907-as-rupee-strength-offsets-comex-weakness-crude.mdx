**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX Gold rose **₹140,907/10g** (+**0.40%**) in the afternoon session, bucking weakness across global bullion markets where Comex gold traded lower. The rupee strengthened to **₹96.27/USD**, reducing import parity for foreign gold into India. Energy commodities surged independently, with MCX Crude jumping **+3.94%** to **₹7,914/bbl** and Natural Gas rising **+1.78%** to **₹279.80/mmBtu**, driven by supply concerns unrelated to gold dynamics.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Gold's modest gains despite Comex weakness signal domestic real-yield dynamics overriding global price pressure. At current import parity (global gold price × **₹96.27/USD** + duty), the rupee's strength should suppress MCX premiums, yet gold held firm — implying either local physical demand absorption or hedging by domestic players. The energy complex surge reflects supply-side geopolitical tension (crude carries Middle East premium) and seasonal demand for heating (natural gas is standalone), neither transmitting to gold through demand destruction or inflation expectations yet.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
A jewellery manufacturer importing unfinished gold faces delayed hedging decisions as Comex weakness persists while MCX stays resilient; forward-booking costs remain elevated. An investment fund routing safe-haven capital finds real-yield compression offset partially by rupee stability, altering the relative attractiveness of gold versus fixed income.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Gold's resilience at **₹140,907** despite Comex losses shows rupee strength is the primary MCX price anchor today, not global bullion sentiment. Energy's independent surge (**+3.94%** crude) confirms these are separate drivers.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
USD/INR close below **₹96.20** or breach above **₹96.50** will determine if rupee strength sustains gold support into Monday's London AM fix.
