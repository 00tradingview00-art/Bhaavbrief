**WHAT HAPPENED**
SEBI has initiated a review of the margin framework governing MCX commodity futures to enhance market efficiency and reduce systemic risks across all traded contracts.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
This review signals potential recalibration of **SPAN (Standardised Portfolio Analysis of Risk) margins** and **VaR (Value-at-Risk) parameters** that currently determine capital requirements for overnight positions in gold, silver, crude oil, and natural gas futures. Proprietary trading desks running directional bets and spread strategies on **MCX Gold and MCX Crude Mini** will need to recalculate their overnight margin obligations per the circular's final guidelines. Brokers managing client leverage on these contracts must prepare updated margin systems and client communication templates ahead of implementation.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Full-service brokers like Zerodha, Angel One, and ICICI Direct must reprogram their margin calculators before the effective date, while HNI proprietary desks running arbitrage and calendar spread strategies on MCX contracts need to model revised capital lockup scenarios. Retail traders holding leveraged crude mini positions and overnight gold futures holdings will face recalculated margin calls at the next settlement cycle, potentially tightening their effective leverage.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Brokers offering margin funding on MCX gold futures will need to recalibrate their client leverage caps within days of circular publication. **MCX Crude Mini** contract traders may face higher SPAN margins during volatile sessions, compressing position sizes for undercapitalized traders. This framework revision does not directly alter spot commodity prices since it adjusts derivative capital efficiency rather than supply-demand fundamentals.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
The full circular publication on **mcxindia.com** and the effective date announcement will clarify exact margin percentage changes and phase-in timelines. Monitor MCX's trading statistics in the first week post-implementation to gauge whether tighter margins reduce speculative open interest.
