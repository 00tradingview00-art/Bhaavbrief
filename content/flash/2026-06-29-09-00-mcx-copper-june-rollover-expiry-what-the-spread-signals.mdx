**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Copper June 2026 contract expires tomorrow (June 30), the last business day of the month. As of Friday's close, MCX Copper June traded at **₹1,253.8/kg** — and the migration of open interest from June to July contracts over the past week reveals how professional money is positioned heading into the next cycle. Rollover week is the least covered, most information-dense event in the MCX calendar.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

The June-July spread (the price difference between the expiring contract and the next active month) is the market's verdict on near-term demand. Contango (July above June) means traders expect supply to loosen or demand to soften — they're willing to pay a premium to defer delivery. Backwardation (June above July) signals that demand for immediate copper is outrunning supply — a structurally bullish read. Historically, when MCX Copper enters expiry in contango exceeding **₹8-12/kg**, the next contract underperforms LME by 1.5-2% in the first fortnight as rolled positions are re-established.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

Wire rod manufacturers, cable producers, and electrical contractors who hedge their copper procurement on MCX face their highest basis risk during rollover week. A business holding June short hedges must roll to July before tomorrow's close or face physical delivery obligation — the roll cost directly compresses their hedging efficiency. Hindalco's downstream copper business and Sterlite's treasury desk run systematic rollover programmes to manage this.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Rollover spread direction is a cleaner signal of institutional copper sentiment than the price itself — it strips out the USD/INR and LME noise and reflects purely domestic demand expectations for the next delivery cycle.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

Track the June-July spread in Monday morning's first 30 minutes of trading. If the spread narrows sharply (July approaching June's level), it indicates aggressive short-side rolling — a positioning signal, not a fundamental one. MCX publishes daily OI data at 6:00 PM IST; Tuesday's report will confirm which contracts absorbed the rolled volume.
