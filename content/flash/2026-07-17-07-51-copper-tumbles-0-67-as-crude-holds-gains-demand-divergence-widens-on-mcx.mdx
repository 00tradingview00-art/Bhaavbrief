**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Crude opened the Friday morning session up **0.54% to ₹7,655/bbl**, extending a modest rally while broader commodities remained subdued. Copper declined **0.67%**, the session's largest mover, signalling demand-side weakness despite crude's modest strength. Gold ticked up **0.25%** while silver and natural gas showed fractional gains, creating a divergence between energy and metals.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

Crude's gains reflect geopolitical premium independent of demand fundamentals—Middle Eastern tensions or supply-side disruptions can push prices even as industrial metals retreat. Copper's **0.67% decline** sits in direct contradiction to crude's rise, suggesting traders are pricing *reduced* global manufacturing activity rather than supply shock, a signal that travels inversely to import parity for Indian refiners. When crude appreciates but copper falls, the transmission to MCX breakeven levels favours refiners over industrial input buyers—crude costs rise relative to finished-goods demand.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

An oil marketing company holding long hedge positions in MCX Crude futures at lower levels now faces mark-to-market gains on **₹7,655/bbl** exposure, reducing near-term cash outflows on inventory. A cable or conductor manufacturer relying on copper as primary input faces procurement pressure as global demand signals weaken; forward-buying decisions now depend on whether copper breaches below session lows. A jewellery manufacturer buying physical gold for immediate fabrication sees minimal margin compression at **+0.25%** today.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Copper's **0.67% drop against crude's +0.54% rise** shows demand-destruction expectations dominating industrial metals while geopolitical premium supports energy—two separate risk regimes. This divergence has historically preceded demand-shock repricing within 5–7 trading sessions.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

Copper hold above **₹1,295/kg** level; breach signals extension of demand weakness into broader EM manufacturing data due Monday.
