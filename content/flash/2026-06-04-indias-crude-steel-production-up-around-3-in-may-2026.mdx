**WHAT HAPPENED**
India's crude steel production reached **14.21 million tonnes** in May 2026, rising **2.9% year-on-year**, with finished steel production at **13.94 million tonnes**.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Rising steel output at this scale drives sustained demand for **copper** in electrical infrastructure and industrial machinery, keeping **MCX Copper** import parity anchored against **COMEX copper** at **$6.5190/lb**. Expanded blast furnace and hot metal activity — up **2.0% YoY** — increases coking coal and energy consumption, directly lifting industrial crude demand from steel plant operators. Higher finished steel volumes flowing into construction and manufacturing pipelines reinforce base metal consumption fundamentals, particularly for copper wiring and aluminium structural inputs.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
**MCX Copper** futures desks, steel producers **SAIL** and **JSW Steel**, copper rod and wire manufacturers like **Hindalco** and **Sterlite Copper**, construction contractors sourcing structural steel, and industrial equipment buyers procuring copper-intensive machinery.

**BOTTOM LINE**
**JSW Steel** and **SAIL** register higher throughput revenue as **13.94 million tonnes** of finished steel enters domestic supply chains. **MCX Copper** futures carry a bullish consumption signal with industrial offtake expanding alongside steel sector growth. **Copper wiring and cables** used in construction face sustained price pressure at current import parity levels.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
The next **Joint Plant Committee (JPC)** monthly steel data release for June 2026 will confirm whether the production trajectory holds; a **COMEX copper** break above **$6.60/lb** would reprice MCX import parity higher.
