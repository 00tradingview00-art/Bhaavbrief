**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX Silver dropped over 7% to ₹2.5 lakh per kg while MCX Gold traded near ₹1.52 lakh per 10g during today's session.

**MCX IMPACT**
The MCX Silver December contract bears the primary impact, with spot prices at ₹2.5 lakh per kg sitting approximately ₹9,988 below the current COMEX-derived theoretical import parity of ₹2,59,988/kg — indicating the domestic contract is trading at a significant discount to international benchmarks. This divergence signals either aggressive domestic selling pressure or a lag in rupee-adjusted repricing, given USD/INR at ₹96.21. MCX Gold at ₹1.52 lakh trails the COMEX-derived level of approximately ₹1,60,860/10g by nearly ₹8,860, reflecting a similar discount structure. For Indian traders, this spread compression matters because import cost floors — inclusive of 15% customs duty on silver — establish a structural support band; prices sustained below import parity reduce arbitrage incentive and signal purely domestic demand weakness driving the selloff.

**WATCH**
The next trigger is the COMEX Silver weekly close relative to the $76.41/oz level — a sustained hold above this level widens the MCX discount further and flags potential catch-up buying in domestic contracts. U.S. Core PCE data and RBI's next USD/INR intervention posture will confirm whether rupee depreciation offsets or amplifies the COMEX-to-MCX spread.
