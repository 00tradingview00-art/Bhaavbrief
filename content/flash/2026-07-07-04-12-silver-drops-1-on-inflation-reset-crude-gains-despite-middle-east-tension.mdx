**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Silver slid **₹2,362/kg to ₹233,720/kg** (down **1.01%**) in this morning's session, outpacing losses in gold (**−0.55%**) and copper (**−0.30%**). Crude posted a rare gain of **+0.63%** to **₹6,592/bbl**, signalling divergence within the commodity complex. The silver weakness arrived alongside headlines linking precious metal dips to renewed inflation concerns and US-Iran conflict escalation.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

Silver's 1% drop reflects a tactical pivot away from safe-haven demand despite geopolitical risk — a signal that near-term inflation expectations may be moderating faster than Middle East premium is building into sentiment. At USD/INR **₹95.33**, global silver spot converted to rupee parity sits materially below MCX levels, compressing the import margin and reducing physical arbitrage incentive for refiners. Crude's separate **+0.63%** gain confirms the geopolitical signal is real but ringfenced to energy; it is not radiating across all risk assets.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

A solar panel manufacturer hedging August silver purchases faces narrowed contango spreads, raising the cost of rolling forward procurement hedges. A jewellery exporter locking rupee realisation on silver fabrication contracts now encounters softer MCX benchmarks, compressing already-thin export margins. An oil marketing company managing downstream hedges benefits from crude's intraday resilience, though this remains isolated from broader commodity deflation.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Safe-haven flows are subordinate to inflation-expectation resets today: silver's **1.01%** loss despite geopolitical headlines shows precious metals are tracking real-yield compression, not geopolitical premium expansion. Crude's decoupling (**+0.63%** vs silver's **−1.01%**) proves the Middle East risk is contained to energy.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

US Fed rate-cut probability data (FOMC minutes or PCE CPI if released) will confirm whether inflation fears are driving the unwind. Silver's hold above **₹231,000/kg** support tomorrow will signal whether industrial de-hedging has exhausted itself.
