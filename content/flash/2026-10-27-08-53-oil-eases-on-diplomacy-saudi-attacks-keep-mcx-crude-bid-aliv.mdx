**WHAT HAPPENED**
Middle East diplomatic initiatives have prompted crude oil price moderation, but continued Saudi infrastructure attacks sustain upside supply risk in global markets.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
**MCX Crude at ₹9776/bbl** now trades in a narrowed band as competing narratives — de-escalation talks versus fresh attack threats — offset each other. Indian refinery procurement desks face whipsawing rupee-denominated landed costs: diplomatic progress eases per-barrel import outlay, but Saudi strike risk re-anchors their hedging floor at elevated levels. Refiners cannot aggressively scale down forward crude nominations without risking supply gaps if attacks resume; this dual-pressure dynamic locks working capital into higher inventory buffers than normal, compressing operational margins at the margin.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Downstream fuel retailers tied to crude-indexed diesel and petrol pricing grids now carry asymmetric cost risk — price relief on diplomacy is capped by their inability to fully pass through savings while attack risk persists, squeezing station-level contribution margins. Trucking and logistics operators, whose fuel budgets comprise 35–40% of operating costs, delay route repricing decisions, waiting for sustained crude moderation before updating freight rate cards; this hesitation compounds working capital strain for smaller fleet operators already carrying fuel surcharge disputes. Household cooking gas (LPG) and kerosene retail rates, indexed to crude trends with a 6–8 week lag, will face delayed downward adjustment if crude stabilizes, meaning rural and semi-urban consumers see inventory replenishment costs remain elevated even as wholesale signals ease.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Indian oil refineries will maintain crude run rates near capacity as they cannot trust the current price ease without confirmed supply stabilization, locking refining margins into a mid-cycle squeeze. **MCX Crude holding above ₹9750/bbl** signals market skepticism toward durable de-escalation, keeping producer hedging active. Petrol pump pricing in Indian metros will likely remain sticky at current levels despite crude easing, as retailers await clearer geopolitical resolution before margin restoration.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Track the next **OPEC+ statement or Aramco production guidance** within 10–14 days and any subsequent Saudi installation incident reports; a sustained 48-hour attack silence combined with formal diplomatic breakthrough would crack the current price floor decisively lower.
