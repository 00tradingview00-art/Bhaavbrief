**WHAT HAPPENED**
SEBI has barred Rajesh Exports and its promoter from securities markets pending investigation into alleged revenue inflation totalling ₹15.15 trillion.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Rajesh Exports, one of India's largest gold refining and jewellery export companies, processed significant volumes of physical gold through its Valcambi refinery; a trading suspension freezes the company's ability to raise capital or conduct securities transactions while regulators audit declared revenue figures. With MCX gold currently pricing at approximately ₹158,200/10g, any restatement of historical gold processing volumes directly distorts the declared throughput economics of India's gold refining pipeline. Bullion market participants pricing import parity off COMEX at $4,471/oz now face opacity around one of the sector's largest declared refining counterparties.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
MCX gold futures traders, bullion dealers and refiners in the SEEDA cluster, jewellery exporters dependent on Rajesh Exports' Valcambi-certified gold supply chain, institutional equity desks holding Rajesh Exports positions, and custodian banks financing gold inventory against the company's balance sheet.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Gold jewellery exporters relying on Rajesh Exports as a certified refining counterparty lose access to a major supply chain node, compressing available Valcambi-certified gold for export contracts. MCX Gold October futures carry elevated counterparty-uncertainty premium as India's largest listed gold processor faces regulatory suspension. Certified hallmarked jewellery sourced through Rajesh Exports' supply chain faces provenance verification delays at retail.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
SEBI's interim order detailing the specific revenue inflation methodology and the next scheduled hearing date — confirmation of restated revenue figures will determine whether additional bullion market participants face regulatory scrutiny.
