**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Copper rallied **₹1,293.20/kg, up 1.98%** in today's session, extending gains alongside gold and silver despite crude oil's **3.18% fall to ₹6,848/bbl**. The divergence signals industrial metals are detaching from energy weakness — copper's advance reflects LME base metals index hitting record highs on supply concerns, not broad risk-on momentum. Silver rose **1.35%** while gold gained **1.14%**, creating a three-metal rally despite safe-haven demand typically appearing in isolation.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

The copper move isolates industrial-demand strength from the crude collapse, suggesting procurement expectations are hardening independent of macroeconomic recession fears. LME copper strength transmits to MCX via import parity — as global LME prices hold firm and **USD/INR trades at ₹95.38**, domestic smelters and fabricators face anchored rupee-denominated costs despite currency stability. This decoupling matters because crude's fall usually signals demand destruction, yet copper's **+1.98%** indicates sectors like power generation, telecom infrastructure, and EV cable assembly are pricing in supply tightness rather than demand contraction.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

A cable manufacturer hedging Q3–Q4 procurement must decide whether to lock in current MCX levels or await further clarity on global supply; aluminum and copper shortage narratives directly impact copper rod pricing into auto wiring harnesses. A power distribution equipment maker's margin on transformer windings now depends on whether this copper strength sustains, given working-capital pressure on 45-60 day payment terms.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Copper's **+1.98% isolation** from crude's **−3.18%** collapse shows industrial-demand pricing is now independent of energy-recession signals — a structural divergence.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

LME copper settlement (London 1500 IST) confirming or reversing today's record high; any China manufacturing PMI data signaling demand revision.
