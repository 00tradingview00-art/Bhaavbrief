**WHAT HAPPENED**

**MCX IMPACT**
The MCX Crudeoilm contract — a smaller 10-barrel lot variant of the standard Crude Oil futures — directly tracks WTI price action converted at the prevailing rupee rate. At ₹9,666/bbl, the contract reflects a near-full pass-through of the WTI rally, with no meaningful rupee cushion given the dollar's current strength above ₹96. Indian refiners and bulk consumers using MCX as a hedge face elevated import costs, as India's customs duty and GST structure layers additional cost over the landed price benchmark. Any further rupee depreciation against the dollar widens the spread between COMEX WTI and effective MCX settlement, amplifying the cost burden for Indian traders holding unhedged long positions in the July 2026 expiry.

**WATCH**
The next U.S. EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report will either reinforce or pressure the $98.50 WTI level; a build in crude inventories above consensus pulls the MCX contract lower, while a draw tightens it further. Monitor USD/INR at the ₹96.50 resistance level — a break higher directly inflates MCX settlement prices independent of COMEX movement.
