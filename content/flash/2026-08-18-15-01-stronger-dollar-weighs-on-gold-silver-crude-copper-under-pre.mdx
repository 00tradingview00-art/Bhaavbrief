**WHAT HAPPENED**
The **US Dollar Index extended its recovery from a two-month low and traded above 99.50**, signalling renewed strength in the greenback amid shifts in Fed rate expectations and risk sentiment.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
A stronger dollar raises the rupee-denominated import costs for gold and silver, making **MCX Gold** and **MCX Silver** more expensive for Indian jewellers, coin dealers, and wholesale bullion buyers in Zaveri Bazaar and regional wholesalers. For **MCX Crude** and **MCX Copper**, dollar appreciation compresses margins for Indian oil refiners and metal processors who import feedstock priced in dollars but sell finished products locally in rupees — a headwind that typically precedes downward repricing in rupee terms. The mechanism works through currency translation: dollar strength narrows hedging windows for importers and increases working capital strain on refiners managing dollar-payable inventory.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Gold and silver fabricators and hallmarking units sourcing raw material on monthly contracts face immediate input cost repricing, while jewellery retailers and D2C e-commerce brands carrying finished inventory absorb margin compression on pre-set catalogue prices. Downstream, oil marketing companies managing crude cost pass-through to petrol pump pricing and copper wire manufacturers supplying electrical contractors see procurement windows narrow as dollar strength locks in higher rupee costs mid-month. Retail gold buyers and households purchasing jewellery ahead of weddings and festivals face higher per-gram pricing; motorists and industrial users of copper wire experience slower price reductions than dollar weakness would otherwise deliver.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Crude oil refiners operating on tight dollar-rupee conversion spreads face margin headwinds if dollar strength persists. **MCX Gold** and **MCX Silver** are repricing upward in rupee terms as dollar strength raises import parity pricing above 99.50 DXY levels. Retail gold jewellery prices and motor fuel costs will trend higher unless rupee stabilises.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
**Fed speaker commentary on rate hold expectations** and **RBI's next monetary policy signal** on rupee support; a retest of DXY 100.00 would extend commodity repricing pressure.
