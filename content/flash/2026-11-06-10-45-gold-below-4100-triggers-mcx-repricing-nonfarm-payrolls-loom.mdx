**WHAT HAPPENED**
**Gold spot prices fell below $4,100/oz**, shifting market expectations that a stronger-than-expected October nonfarm payrolls print could reverse the rally and keep US real yields elevated, supporting the dollar.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
A hotter jobs report would delay Fed rate cuts, raising real yields and increasing the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets—directly pressuring **MCX Gold** and **MCX Silver** as lower rupee-denominated gold makes imports cheaper for refiners and jewellers sourcing from London Bullion Market dealers. Simultaneously, a stronger dollar reduces purchasing power for non-US industrial buyers, weighing on **MCX Copper** demand from Chinese fabricators and construction contractors who price input costs in dollars. **MCX Crude** faces headwinds if payroll strength signals recession fears ease, reducing safe-haven oil demand, though energy traders monitor Brent-WTI spread adjustments tied to OPEC+ production policy and geopolitical risk premiums.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
Indian bullion refiners and hallmarking units locking rupee hedges on forward gold contracts see their margin profiles compress if spot prices remain suppressed through month-end settlement. Tier-2 jewellery manufacturers procuring raw gold and copper for alloy-making face inventory write-downs on high-cost SKUs, forcing promotional pricing at retail. Retail gold buyers—especially festival-season shoppers and bridal segment purchasers—benefit from lower ornament prices at jeweller counters, though purchasing power remains tied to broader rupee weakness against the dollar.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Indian gold refiners face narrower processing margins if spot gold remains anchored below $4,100 through settlement cycles. **MCX Gold futures** signal a downside bias until nonfarm payrolls data clarifies US monetary policy direction. Retail buyers of gold jewellery across urban and semi-urban markets stand to benefit from lower ornament pricing over the next fortnight.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
US nonfarm payrolls release (early November) and Fed speakers' commentary on terminal rate levels will reset gold-dollar correlation; simultaneous China industrial output data will determine whether copper demand stabilization holds.
