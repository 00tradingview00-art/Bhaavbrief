**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Natural Gas collapsed **2.72%** to **₹275/mmBtu**, leading a broad commodity selloff across the session. Silver followed with a **2.08%** decline to **₹216,021/kg**, while gold fell **1.03%** to **₹140,395/10g** and copper edged down **0.29%** to **₹1,308/kg**. USD/INR held at **₹96.34**, providing no currency hedge to offset the commodity losses.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

Natural gas weakness typically reflects either demand destruction (weather, industrial shutdown) or inventory builds in global storage — neither carries crude's geopolitical premium, so this is a structural demand signal. The silver decline splits between two drivers: its industrial component (solar manufacturing, semiconductors) suggests manufacturing PMI weakness offshore, while its safe-haven half (real yields rising in USD markets) indicates risk-on sentiment reducing hedging demand. Gold's modest **1.03%** dip despite negative real yields implies buyers are stepping in near **₹140,400**, keeping the safe-haven bid intact even as risk sentiment improves.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

A natural gas-fired power producer hedging Q3 generation costs faces lower input costs if this reflects demand destruction, but refinance risk if it signals longer-term industrial contraction. A solar module manufacturer buying silver for wafer metallization finds raw material costs easing, improving gross margins on export orders. A jewellery retailer importing unrefined silver for processing sees procurement advantages at current levels but faces uncertainty if industrial weakness deepens.

**BOTTOM LINE**

The **2.72%** nat gas lead suggests demand-side pressure, not supply shock—consistent with offshore PMI deterioration rather than geopolitical disruption. Silver's dual weakness (industrial + safe-haven) amplifies this demand-destruction narrative across both lenses.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

US API crude inventory print (tonight, 20:30 IST) and ECB services PMI Friday 15:00 IST—both confirm whether offshore demand is genuinely contracting.
