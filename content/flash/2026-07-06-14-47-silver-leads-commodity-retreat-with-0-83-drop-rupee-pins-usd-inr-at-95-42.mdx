**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Silver plunged **-0.83%** to ₹235,443/kg in afternoon trade, leading a broad commodity retreat as safe-haven demand evaporated. Gold followed with a **-0.71%** decline to **₹146,327/10g**, while crude oil bucked the trend with a modest **+0.43%** gain to **₹6,596/bbl**. USD/INR held steady at **₹95.42**, anchoring within Commerzbank's forecast **94–96 range**.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

Silver's outsized selloff reflects the industrial component overwhelming safe-haven demand—solar and semiconductor input costs are retreating alongside risk-off sentiment. Gold's smaller decline (**-0.71% vs -0.83%**) signals residual safe-haven bid, with real-yield compression likely supporting prices despite the intraday weakness. Crude's counterintuitive strength despite falling precious metals suggests geopolitical premium is holding independent of broad commodity demand signals, while the rupee's stability at **₹95.42** prevents import-parity tailwinds from translating into fresh MCX cost relief.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

A solar panel manufacturer procuring silver in August contracts faces downside revision opportunities as spot weakness creates hedging windows below ₹235,400/kg levels. A jewellery exporter locking export margins today encounters compressed rupee tailwinds—with USD/INR pinned at **₹95.42**, last week's cheaper imports no longer offset today's gold spot decline proportionally. An oil refiner managing crude hedges must separately evaluate whether holding positions through the **₹6,596** level reflects justified geopolitical positioning or crowded consensus.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Silver's **-0.83%** lead over gold's **-0.71%** collapse confirms today's move is industrial deleveraging, not systemic risk-off; rupee stability at **₹95.42** prevents import-parity relief from compounding the decline.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

USD/INR breach of **₹96.00** intraday or Commerzbank's stated range breakdown would revert the transmission mechanism, allowing rupee depreciation to floor MCX prices.
