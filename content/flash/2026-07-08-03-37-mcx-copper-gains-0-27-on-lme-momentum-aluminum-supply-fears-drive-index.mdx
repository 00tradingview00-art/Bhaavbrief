**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX Copper rose **₹1,279.50/kg to ₹1,280/kg** in this morning session, gaining **0.27%** alongside a broader LME base metals rally driven by aluminum supply concerns. Crude dominated the cross-asset move with a **+2.74%** surge, while copper held modest upside as gold and silver retreated by **-0.34%** and **-0.40%** respectively.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
The copper move reflects external LME momentum rather than domestic demand signals, as the index reached record highs on "aluminum black hole" supply fears—a supply-side constraint that does not directly impact Indian copper procurement costs. Global LME copper strength transmits to MCX through USD/INR at **₹95.12**; a stable rupee this morning has muted the import-parity pass-through that would normally amplify London gains into domestic prices, suggesting the **0.27%** MCX gain is lagging global momentum.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
A cable manufacturer or transformer core producer holding unhedged forward copper exposure benefits from the MCX uptick, as spot procurement costs inch upward in line with global benchmarks. Conversely, a copper scrap collector or recycler selling into the domestic market faces margin compression if LME-linked forward contracts price higher than recycled material input costs.

**BOTTOM LINE**
MCX copper is following aluminum-driven LME gains, not industrial demand; the **0.27%** rise masks an absence of domestic Indian demand data to independently justify price momentum. Supply-side reshuffling in non-correlated metals (aluminum) is lifting copper via index effects, not consumption signals.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
LME aluminum inventory data (Thursday AM London close) and USD/INR stability at **₹95.10–₹95.20** will clarify whether the rally sustains or reverses into Thursday's MCX session.
