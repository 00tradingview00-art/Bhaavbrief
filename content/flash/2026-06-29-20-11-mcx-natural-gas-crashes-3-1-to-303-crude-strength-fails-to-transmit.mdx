**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX Natural Gas collapsed **3.10%** to **₹303.20/mmBtu** in today's session, marking the sharpest single-commodity move across the complex. Crude oil posted a modest **+2.30%** gain to **₹6,728/bbl** on renewed US-Iran conflict headlines, while gold and silver both retreated — gold down **1.21%** to **₹142,413/10g** and silver down **0.78%** — signaling a divergence between geopolitical risk (crude strength) and safe-haven demand (gold weakness).

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Natural gas weakness reflects North American supply recovery and softer US summer cooling demand, a structural shift independent of Middle East tensions. Crude's geopolitical premium (US-Iran escalation) is not transmitting to gas because the two markets decouple on supply geography — OPEC crude depends on Gulf stability; US-sourced LNG and pipeline gas do not. For MCX traders, this means a **₹303** floor in gas implies imported LNG-linked contract pricing at approximately **USD 9.50/mmBtu** (accounting for **₹94.53/USD** and terminal spread), a level that undercuts domestic renewal demand into Q3.

**WHO AFFECTED**
A power generation utility operating coal-to-gas fuel switching strategies will see margin compression if gas remains below **₹305/mmBtu**, reducing the economics of bidding gas-fired capacity into merit order. A city gas distribution network purchasing spot inventory will face inventory write-downs if volumes purchased above **₹310/mmBtu** clear at current levels.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Gas weakness is demand-driven, not geopolitical spillover — crude strength and gas decline in the same session is historically rare and signals structural oversupply in LNG relative to crude-linked supply tightness.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
US Energy Information Administration weekly gas storage data (Thursday midnight IST); a break below **₹300/mmBtu** would signal sustained structural weakness into July contract roll.
