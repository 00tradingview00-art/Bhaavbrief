**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Copper rallied **₹1259/kg (+1.16%)** as London Metal Exchange base metals surged to record highs, driven by aluminum supply-side anxiety and industrial demand revival signals from the US session. Crude Oil fell **1.59%** to **₹6621/bbl**, establishing a divergence within the commodity complex. Gold and silver remained subdued, with the copper move standing isolated in the session.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

Copper's outperformance signals industrial-demand confidence—a structural break from macro anxiety narratives dominating energy. LME copper strength transmits to MCX through import-parity pricing: global copper moves approximately **₹70–90 per kg** per **1% move in spot USD/INR at ₹94.65**, plus domestic duty and logistics costs. This **1.16% rally** suggests Indian fabricators perceive near-term construction and manufacturing demand, independent of crude's weakness.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

An electrical cable manufacturer sourcing **250–300 tonnes monthly** faces procurement decisions at elevated local prices; if hedging via MCX futures, this session's rally compresses their forward-cover window. A copper rod producer locking raw material costs for Q3 delivery now faces working capital pressure if spot prices hold above **₹1250/kg** without corresponding demand visibility in downstream orders.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Copper's **+1.16% move** against crude's **−1.59% fall** reveals divergent demand narratives: industrial activity pricing strength independent of energy weakness. This divergence persists only if manufacturing PMI data from US or eurozone confirms demand, not inventory restocking.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

US ISM Manufacturing PMI (Friday release) and LME copper's ability to hold above **$10,500/tonne**—breach signals reversion to macro weakness.
