**WHAT HAPPENED**

MCX Gold fell **0.69%** to **₹141,271/10g** in this morning's session, tracking a broader softness across precious metals despite crude's strength at **+1.17%**. Silver declined more steeply at **−0.92%**, suggesting selective pressure on the precious metals complex rather than a flight-to-safety bid. The USD/INR rate held firm at **₹96.21**, ruling out rupee depreciation as the transmission channel for gold weakness.

**WHAT IT MEANS**

Gold's decline without rupee headwinds points to real-yield pressure—rising global bond yields or expectations of sustained higher rates are eroding the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding bullion. When USD/INR is stable at **₹96.21** but global gold still weakens, domestic import parity (global spot × exchange rate + duty) signals margin compression for refiners and wholesalers. This mirrors silver's steeper **−0.92%** loss, where the industrial component (solar, semiconductor demand) is being outweighed by real-yield headwinds, not safe-haven accumulation.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**

A jewellery manufacturer importing refined gold for casting faces tighter working-capital timing—forward hedges struck when global gold was stronger now show unrealised losses if unwound early. A precious-metals refiner holding inventory at cost basis experiences mark-to-market pressure on unsold stock. An ornament exporter's quoted prices (locked 2–3 weeks forward) now embed higher effective duty costs relative to today's lower spot, compressing margins on fresh orders.

**BOTTOM LINE**

Gold's **−0.69%** slide despite stable rupee indicates real-yield dominance over currency factors; silver's sharper **−0.92%** loss rules out safe-haven demand as the driver.

**WHAT TO WATCH**

US 10-year yields at next Asia open (Thursday 16 July). If yields fall materially below 4.1%, gold should recover; if they hold above 4.3%, further downside to **₹140,500** is likely.
