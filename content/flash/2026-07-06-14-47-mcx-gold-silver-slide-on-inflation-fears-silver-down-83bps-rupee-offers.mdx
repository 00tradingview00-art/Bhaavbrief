**WHAT HAPPENED**
MCX gold fell **0.71%** to **₹146,327/10g** in the afternoon session, tracking a broader precious metals selloff. Silver led the decline at **−0.83%**, while crude edged higher by **+0.43%** to **₹6,596/bbl**, creating a divergence between safe-haven and energy assets.

**WHAT IT MEANS**
Gold's retreat reflects renewed inflation concern in global markets, which typically pressures real yields and reduces the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding bullion. At spot USD/INR of **₹95.42**, the rupee strength is partially offsetting the decline from global gold prices, meaning offshore weakness is transmitting into MCX at a dampened rate. Silver's steeper fall suggests the industrial component—semiconductor and solar manufacturing demand signals—is absorbing a disproportionate hit from deflation expectations, even as geopolitical risk (US-Iran tensions) ordinarily supports the safe-haven bid.

**WHO IS AFFECTED**
A jewellery manufacturer hedging August forward purchases through MCX gold futures now faces lower strike prices but must confirm whether their retail order book can absorb margin pressure from intraday volatility. An industrial metal buyer in the solar equipment space using silver as a procurement hedge is exposed to widening storage and financing costs if this industrial selloff extends into July settlement.

**BOTTOM LINE**
Silver's **−0.83%** outpace of gold's **−0.71%** indicates the market is pricing deflation risk (industrial demand erosion) ahead of geopolitical premium—a signal that inflation-fighting expectations are overriding war-risk hedging flows.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
US inflation data release this week; any breach of **₹146,000/10g** support in gold closes the risk-off narrative confirmation.
