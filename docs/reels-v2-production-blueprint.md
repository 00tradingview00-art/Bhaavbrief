## Creative decision

Make the viewer notice an Indian commodity consequence, SEE the missing mechanism,
and leave with one memorable line. Cover the consumer, business and market sides:
jewellery, solar cells, fuel, wiring, aluminium packaging, contracts and options.
Market updates and practical learning are both core coverage. Contract jargon must
not be the price of admission; advanced explainers name and explain the term.

A keyword audit of the 70 measured entries found 54 mentioning gold, five silver,
12 crude, zero copper, zero aluminium and one options. Categories overlap and
keyword matching misses some topics; these are coverage clues, not exclusive
classifications or evidence that untested topics perform poorly. Do not narrow
the programme to gold because the old sample is dominated by it.

First 14 pilots: default 10–15 seconds, 24–34 spoken words. Up to 18 seconds if measured
narration or a readable comparison needs it; complex options/contract lessons can
use 18–25 seconds when necessary for a complete explanation and be compared within
their own duration band. This is a test, not a universal
optimal length. No intro bumper, mandatory carousel, product tour or generic end card.

- Frame zero: physical object and complete hook already visible, usually 5–8 words.
- By 2–3 seconds: begin answering. Stop restating the puzzle.
- Middle: animate one change; keep other variables constant in an illustration.
- End: land the answer, hold briefly, connect visually to the opening.
- Caption: source, caveat and at most one natural topic-specific question.

Questions and statements are both allowed. Specificity and truthful payoff decide.
Evergreen copy cannot imply a move occurred today. Label illustrative numbers
on the first frame and throughout, not just in a caption. Avoid 'comment GOLD',
fake urgency and promises of better trades.

## Art, motion and sound

Tactile close-ups, clear silhouettes, warm gold/copper against charcoal, generous
space and a small BhaavBrief signature. Original generated art is a scene asset,
not the finished story. Animate the explanatory element: currency conversion,
a price tag, contract card or bill components. Remove decorative sine waves,
fake charts, glowing currency ribbons, blinking text and zoom-only sequences.
Typeset numbers in code. Live charts require real data and named axes.

1080×1920, 30fps. Essential copy inside x=80–920, y=280–1480; reserve right/bottom
for platform controls. Review at phone size. Use short captions aligned with
spoken phrases. Female narration stays; verify the actual voice, not just the
manifest label. Local system TTS is a labelled draft fallback only. Music enters
after the hook and stays below speech. Measure audio first; never chop the last
sentence to hit a duration target.

Seven permanent editorial lanes, with two posts per lane in the first 14 approved
releases. This is a coverage commitment, not a promise to publish on 14 consecutive
days. Options/learning posts also carry an underlying commodity tag; use different
underlyings across the cycle. Never default every example to gold.

Natural gas, zinc, lead, nickel and other commodities can enter a sourced expansion
slot as audience questions or significant events warrant. Verify current exchange
products and contract availability first. USD/INR, dollar, rates and macro events
connect the lanes; they do not replace commodity coverage.

Each 14-post cycle includes both current-market stories and evergreen learning.
Aim for one evidence-led market/event post and one mechanism/learning post in each
commodity lane; if no meaningful verified event exists, use its evergreen fallback.
Do not invent news to fill a slot. A breaking event can move a slot forward, but the
displaced lane keeps the next available slot. Do not repeat the same commodity
consecutively except for a material ongoing event; no lane disappears during a cycle.

### Ready-to-develop scripts for the next six lanes

These are editorial drafts. Verify each stated mechanism against its primary
source before finalizing the manifest. Numbers and current-market claims need
separate dated evidence. Use one idea per reel; complex expiry/strategy lessons
may take 18–25 seconds when the full explanation needs it, never force unsafe
compression to satisfy the default runtime.

**Silver — industrial use (29 words).**
“Silver isn't only jewellery. It also helps carry electricity inside solar cells.
That's one industrial use behind silver demand. Understanding silver means looking
beyond the jewellery counter.”
Visual: jewellery glint → macro solar-cell contact → two-use takeaway.
Source starting point: Silver Institute, Silver in Industry.

**Crude — input versus final price.**
“A crude-oil price isn't a petrol-pump price. Refining, taxes, distribution and
domestic pricing decisions sit between them. So a global move needn't reach your
pump immediately.”
Visual: barrel input separates from a complete pump-price stack. Verify with
official domestic pricing documentation; no current tax rates without dated proof.

**Copper — tangible use.**
“Copper runs through the wires around you. That links it to electrical equipment
and construction. It's one demand connection—not a guarantee that every building
boom lifts copper prices.”
Visual: copper coil → cable cross-section → named end-use. Source the application;
do not depict a fabricated live demand chart.

**Aluminium — production input.**
“Before aluminium becomes a can, producing the metal needs electricity. Power is
one part of its production cost. The finished can's price includes much more.”
Visual: can → billet → power-input layer. Source the smelting-energy relationship;
no unsourced percentage or simplistic power-price forecast.

**Options — one driver can offset another.**
“Your market view can be right while an option loses value. Its premium also
depends on time and implied volatility. Direction is only part of the story.”
Visual: silver underlying reference + one labelled illustrative premium component.
Choose ONE offsetting driver for the worked example; reserve the other for a sequel.

**Learn — margin versus exposure.**
“The margin on your screen isn't the full contract value. A small deposit supports
a larger exposure. Understand the contract size before interpreting what that
margin means.”
Visual: copper contract card expands to exposure. Final caption explains that
margin is not a maximum-loss figure; exact rules depend on contract and broker.

Carousels are optional supporting material, especially for contract specifications
and options payoff examples. They do not replace any commodity lane's reel slot.

## Review and release

Keep factual/compliance checks intact. Existing manifest fields can be short;
do not create a new planning document per reel. Review source, illustration labels,
captions, safe areas and complete narration. Watch the exported MP4 muted and
with sound. Placeholder source names do not count as evidence. Rendering a draft
does not grant human approval.

Retain published posts and history as evidence; do not delete merely for low views.
Old V2 concepts are reference material, not the next release queue. Never overwrite
a posted media identity with a new creative. The 35-second `prompts/reel_v2.md`
belongs to the legacy brief generator, not this creative reset.

The reset branch prepares local changes; it does not itself alter deployed cron
jobs or publish to Instagram. Before the first pilot release, pause legacy reel
posting as part of that release while leaving brief/site publication intact.
Keep automated legacy output out of the 14-post comparison.

## Measure and decide

Collect a first observation after 24 hours and another after seven days, preserving
the actual timestamp and age. Label late snapshots as late; never backdate them.
Keep missing metrics absent/null and preserve earlier observations.

Compare average watch time AND average watch/duration (viewing-depth proxy, not
completion), then measured saves/shares per reach. Read comments for confusion.
Log 3-second hold/completion only when actually obtained from the platform.
Record hook variant, runtime, voice and source with each pilot.

Below 100 reached accounts per concept, treat differences as directional; pool
several comparable pilots. That threshold is an operating heuristic, not statistical
significance. After 14 pilots, select promising hooks and visual treatments within each lane.
Do not eliminate copper/aluminium/options because gold had a larger old sample.
Expand successful executions across different commodities, then change one variable
per comparison. Coverage remains broad even while individual treatments change. Low watch: answer sooner. Good watch but
no useful response: improve topic/payoff. Low reach with good watch: gather more
observations and test another opening. Do not promise engagement or growth.
